   `quality_reports/reviews/`, and `dev-docs/` inventory docs (several of the latter turned out
   stale — 2025-01 snapshots referencing the old package name and file counts; not relied on).
2. Ran four parallel audit workstreams (background subagents, capped at the 3-4 concurrency
   limit):
   - `domain-reviewer`: code-theory alignment / correctness of the Stage-A/Stage-B two-stage
     estimator and Rashomon machinery against `inst/paper/main.tex`. This is the highest-value
     pass — 33 findings (5 Critical, 8 High documented in full, 12 Medium, 7 Low), each with an
     independent re-derivation, not just a docstring reading.
   - `explore` x3: silent-fallback sweep, dead-code/duplication sweep, defaults-consistency
     catalog across exported entry points.
3. Direct (non-delegated) efficiency review of R-level hot paths: `bisect_lambda_to_budget.R`
   and `cross_fitted_rashomon.R` (well-engineered, no waste found); found one real inefficiency
   + undocumented tie-break in `predict.cf_rashomon`'s ensemble majority vote
   (`R/predict.R:442-444`); independently confirmed the already-fixed (commit `140d110`, 2 days
   prior) exponential tied-tree-enumeration bug and that its Rashomon-set-path counterpart is
   correctly disclosed via `cli::cli_warn()`.
4. Spot-checked the automated sweeps' severity tags before trusting them: **2 of 3 sampled
   "Critical" silent-fallback claims turned out to be false positives** (properly-disclosed
   `%||%` fallbacks that the sweep flagged without reading the surrounding warning/disclosure
   code). One genuine Critical was confirmed directly: `src/model.cpp:92-106` silently
   substitutes a uniform class distribution on exception, logging only to `std::cerr` (not an R
   condition).
5. Wrote up the full report to `quality_reports/reviews/2026-09-10_full-package-audit.md`.
6. **Mid-session correction, at the maintainer's explicit prompt:** `inst/paper/main.tex` should
   not be treated as ground truth — it was already revised once (2026-09-04) after a headline
   result was found false. Cancelled the first "outside audit" subagent (it was framed as
   "verify against `main.tex`") and relaunched a corrected version that classifies every
   Critical/High finding as **paper-independent** (a real bug regardless of the paper's fate) or
   **paper-dependent** (only as strong as this specific, evolving paper's claim), and requires
   independent verification of paper-dependent items against general statistical/decision-tree
   theory rather than trusting `main.tex`'s text.
7. That independent second pass agreed with 100% of the first pass's factual code claims (9
   AGREE, 4 PARTIALLY AGREE, 0 DISAGREE) and confirmed every `main.tex` citation is accurate, but
   substantially re-sorted priority: **8 of 13 Critical/High findings are paper-independent**
   (C4, C5, H1, H3, H4, H5, H6, H7 — these are the ones to act on), one is hybrid (C1: the core
   unit-mismatch is paper-independent, the "breaks the headline rate" escalation is not), and
   4 are paper-dependent (C2, C3, H2 — **do not act on these as originally stated**; it found
   that `main.tex`'s own rate conditions and its own Step-1 feasibility inequality are mutually
   unsatisfiable below roughly n ~ 1e5-1e6, so "fixing" C3 by raising `m_ladder` would make C2
   worse). Also corrected two overstated severities (H2, H8: High → Low/Medium) and a few minor
   citation errors in the first pass. Appended as an addendum to the same report file, with a
