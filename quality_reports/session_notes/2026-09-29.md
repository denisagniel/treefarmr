## What was done

### 1. Theory re-evaluation (no code)

Reconstructed why the continuum-tree paper stalled: it targeted EXACT topology
recovery of an assumed-exactly-tree-shaped truth, which needs a GLOBAL density floor
over the whole covariate space (excludes discrete covariates) and an internally
incompatible penalty regime, and was never run against a single simulation. Two
rounds of Oracle consultation (same session) validated a reframe — target the fitted
`L`-leaf tree's convergence to the population BEST-in-class approximation `f*`, not
to the true function — which drops the realizability assumption and the density
floor, at the cost of the claim being weaker (no exact recovery, no downstream causal
license — confirmed NOT to transfer to a doubletree-style CLT, a separate, harder
problem doubletree itself already shelved).

### 2. Two package bugs fixed

- **`R/stage_b.R`**: Stage B's off-grid search radius (`rho_n = M_n/r_n`) was applied
  in RAW covariate units against a grid built in QUANTILE space
  (`R/discretize.R`), silently collapsing the search window in low-density regions.
  Fixed by mapping the anchor through the coordinate's own empirical quantile
  function. New regression test in `tests/testthat/test-stage-b.R` (exponential-tail
  coordinate where the old raw-unit window provably never reaches the true boundary).
  Removed the now-false runtime warning in `R/fit_twostage.R` that told users to
  manually rescale covariates to work around this.
- **`R/fit_tree.R`**: separate, previously-latent bug surfaced while testing the
  Stage-B fix's `model_limit` pinning — `fit_tree()` crashed with `"formal argument
  'model_limit' matched by multiple actual arguments"` whenever a caller passed
  `model_limit=` explicitly (the exact override path its own warning tells users to
  use), because the raw `...` splice into `optimaltrees()` still carried the caller's
  value alongside an explicit `model_limit=` argument. Nothing in the existing test
  suite exercised this path (confirmed via grep). Fixed via `do.call()`, stripping
  `model_limit` from the forwarded extras first.
- The tied-optimal-tree solver blowup (`src/optimizer/extraction/models.hpp`) was
  investigated and confirmed **already fixed** at `HEAD` (commit `e7cecd4`, prior
  session) — no code change needed here, only confirmed via the repro script below.
- Full `devtools::test()` after both fixes: 0 failures (only pre-existing
  `RUN_SLOW_TESTS`-gated skips and 2 unrelated package-version warnings).

### 4. Simulation study designed and approved

`quality_reports/specs/2026-09-28_continuum-tree-grid-tradeoff.md` (archetype:
`decision`, status: `APPROVED`). Two research questions: (Q1) does excess risk
against `f*` go flat past some `m_n^†(n) ≈ O(√n)` as grid resolution grows; (Q2) does
a Stage-B-refined threshold at a genuine jump in `γ₀` converge at `O(n⁻¹)` versus
`O(n^{-1/2})` at a purely budget-forced ("cosmetic") boundary. Pre-committed success
criteria and a decision rule mapping every combination of outcomes to one of: write
up the grid-tradeoff result as a modest methods paper (± the harder superconsistency
conjecture), write up the paper without the conjecture, or abandon it and ship the
algorithm as unguaranteed software with a negative-results note.

### 5. DGP defect caught by its own verification gate, before any replication ran

The spec's own mandatory topology-verification step (an exhaustive check across all 8
depth-2 shapes, deliberately built into the design specifically so a wrong ground
truth couldn't be silently trusted) found the originally-approved DGP's constructed
topology was NOT `f*` — it lost to an alternative shape by 22.6% of its own risk, not
a near-tie. Three compounding causes: the right side's quadratic canceled to a
constant in `x2` (`b_L+b_R=0`, so the cosmetic-right threshold wasn't even
identified); the jump crossed zero inside the support (undermining the "true break
point is exact" argument); and, most fundamentally, the optimal single cutpoint for
ANY `a+b·x2²` under `Uniform(0,1)` is provably `(1+√17)/8` independent of both `a` and
`b` (verified over 20 parameter pairs) — no re-tuning within that family could ever
have separated the two cosmetic thresholds. Fixed by mirroring the right piece
(`(a_L+Δ1) + b·(1-x2)²`); re-verified 20/20 (closed form vs. `integrate()` vs. 1e7-draw
Monte Carlo vs. brute-force grid search), topology margin `+5.72e-02`. Spec Section 3
updated in place with the full derivation, kept visible rather than silently deleted;
Regime S1's `Δ1` grid rescaled to match (`{4.5, 3.5, 2.5}`, the last value deliberately
crossing back into the zero-jump regime as the most severe stress point).

### 6. Real per-fit cost measured — 2-3 orders of magnitude over the spec's assumption

The spec assumed "well under a second to a few seconds" per fit based on this
session's earlier `L=30` solver-timing evidence. Measured, via a 90-call timing pilot
(10 reps × 9 configs): mean 71.5s/call, up to **771s at the single worst cell**
(`n=16000, m_n=128`), Stage A (`bisect_lambda_to_budget()`) being ~97% of it,
scaling superlinearly in both `m_n` and `n`. Extrapolating the originally-approved
~90,300-fit table would cost on the order of 10,000+ CPU-hours. Tested the one
plausible one-argument fix (`model_limit=0`, since `fit_tree()`'s own heuristic
silently caps it at 1e6 for no benefit on single-tree extraction) — ruled out with a
proper control cell and a 405-comparison bit-identical correctness check: 1.01-1.04×
timing difference, indistinguishable from noise. The cost is structural (branch-and-
bound over `p·(m_n-1)` highly-correlated threshold-indicator features at fine grids),
not a solver misconfiguration.

**Resourcing decision** (spec Section 8, updated in place): cap `m_n` at 64 everywhere
(drop 128, 256), commit to Regime F only for now, defer Regime S1/S2 and any cluster
commitment until Regime F's own result is in. Following the `simulations` skill's own
explicit anti-pattern ("go straight to the full run"), staged as 1 → 10 → 100 reps
before the pre-committed 500/750: the 100-rep rung (`n`(6) × `m_n`(5) = 30 configs for
Q1, `n`(6) × `m_n`(3) = 18 configs for Q2, 4,800 `one_sim()` calls total) is
**in progress**, 4,416/4,800 done as of this note, `furrr`-parallelized, incrementally
saved with resume-by-skipping-completed-cells (added after one OOM kill during the
earlier 10-rep pilot lost no data, since incremental saving already existed by then —
confirmed bit-identical on standalone re-run of the killed cell).
