existing globally-optimal decision-tree solvers search a

Stage A fits an ordinary grid-restricted optimal tree, at a coarse

coordinate splits where) rather than its exact thresholds; the topology is

the nearest cutpoint, and the resulting $O(1/r_n)$ localisation error is
accepted rather than removed. Stage B then refines each threshold by an
exact, grid-free

interval around the Stage-A split, node by node,
root-first. We show that, under a continuum-sparsity condition whose
minimal-leaf representative we assume, rather than prove, is unique, topology
is recovered with probability tending to one on a refining grid, and that
every refined threshold is simultaneously superconsistent, at rate
$O_p(n^{-1})$. The algorithm is implemented, and its
guarantees are illustrated empirically, in the \texttt{optimaltrees} package.]

\subsection{Covariate space, tree partitions, and the fitting problem}
\label{sec:setup-basics}

We observe $n$ independent copies of $(X,Z)$, where $X$ takes values in a
covariate space $\cX$ and $Z$ is a scalar response, and we wish to recover the
regression function
\[
  \gamma_0(x):=\E[Z\mid X=x],
\]
which is assumed throughout to be piecewise constant on an axis-aligned tree
partition of $\cX$. Both a regression problem (real-valued $Z$) and a
classification problem (binary $Z$, with $\gamma_0$ the conditional class
probability) are covered without change; the only structure used below is that
the fitting criterion is squared error, so that excess risk equals squared
$L_2$ distance to $\gamma_0$.

$\cX$ need not itself be finite; it may be any measurable space on which the
covariate law $P_X$ and the regression function $\gamma_0$ are well defined (a
finite product $\cX=\prod_{j\le p}\cX_j$ is the special case in which every
coordinate's cutpoint family below is its full value set). What is finite, and
fixed in $n$, is not $\cX$ but the \emph{candidate partition family},
generated by a pre-specified finite collection of cutpoints on each
coordinate --- chosen by the analyst before seeing the data and never updated
by it. These cutpoints partition $\cX$ into a finite family of \emph{grid
atoms} $\cA$, $\card\cA<\infty$ fixed in $n$, each atom a product of one
inter-cutpoint interval (or category group) per coordinate; write $P(a)$ for
the mass of $a\in\cA$. When $\cX$ is itself finite, atoms coincide with the
individual cells of $\cX$.

A \emph{tree partition} $\tau$ of $\cX$ is a partition induced by recursive
binary splits \emph{at the pre-specified cutpoints only}; its blocks are
\emph{leaves}, each leaf a union of atoms, $\card\tau$ is its leaf count, and
$\ell_\tau(x)$ is the leaf containing $x$. Restricting splits to the
pre-specified cutpoints is Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} below, not a
convention: without it the candidate class ceases to be fixed and finite. For
a leaf budget $\bar L$,
\[
  \cT_{\bar L}=\bigl\{\tau:\tau\text{ a tree partition of }\cX\text{ built
  from the pre-specified cutpoints},\ \card\tau\le\bar L\bigr\},
\]
a fixed finite set: any $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$ is a partition of the finite set
$\cA$ into at most $\bar L$ blocks realisable by cutpoint splits, so
$\card{\cT_{\bar L}}<\infty$ and does not depend on $n$. More generally, for a
finite cutpoint family generating atoms $\cB$ we write $\cT_\cB$ for the
unrestricted class of tree partitions built from those cutpoints, and
$\cT_{L}(\cB)$ for its restriction to at most $L$ leaves; thus
$\cT_{\bar L}=\cT_{\bar L}(\cA)$.

\begin{definition}[Rooted tree, nodes, depth, and level]\label{def:tree-vocab}
Every tree partition $\tau$ above is \emph{realised} by a rooted binary tree
$\mathbf T$ whose leaves are the blocks of $\tau$: every vertex of
$\mathbf T$ other than a leaf is an \emph{internal node}, and every internal
node carries a splitting coordinate, a threshold, and exactly two children.
Leaves are nodes. The \emph{cell} of a node $N$, written $R_N$, is the subset
of $\cX$ consistent with every ancestor test on the root-to-$N$ path:
$R_{\mathrm{root}}=\cX$, and each child's cell is the half of its parent's
cell cut by the parent's threshold; the cell of a leaf is the corresponding
block of $\tau$. (Some assumptions below work with the closure of $R_N$ for
convenience, e.g.\ Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens)'s $R_N$; this
changes nothing that follows, since adjacent leaves' cells share only a
$\nu$-null boundary face under Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens).)
Node $M$ is an \emph{ancestor} of node $N$, and $N$ a \emph{descendant} of
$M$, if $M$ lies on the root-to-$N$ path; $M$ is $N$'s \emph{parent} if in
addition no node lies strictly between them. The \emph{level} of a node is
its distance from the root (the root has level $0$); the \emph{depth} of
$\mathbf T$ is the greatest level attained by any leaf. A partition $\tau$ can
be realised by trees of different depths (e.g.\ by reordering splits on
distinct coordinates), so ``depth'' below is always a property of a
specified realising tree, not of $\tau$ alone; Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}
fixes which realisation depth bounds refer to for the unknown truth.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Cutpoint, split, threshold, boundary.}
Four closely related words for locations along a coordinate recur below, and
they are not interchangeable. A \emph{cutpoint} is one of the analyst's
pre-specified candidate values on a single coordinate, fixed in $n$ and never
updated by the data. A \emph{split} is a tree's use of a cutpoint at one
internal node --- or, for the continuum truth of \S\ref{sec:setup-twostage},
its use of an arbitrary real value there --- and that split's \emph{threshold}
is the value it carries. A \emph{boundary} is a split of the unknown truth
$\gamma_0$ rather than a candidate split of a fitted tree, and is written
``true boundary'' wherever the contrast matters; the same word also appears in
its ordinary geometric sense for the face of a cell, which context
distinguishes. A Stage-A split and a true boundary are compared as points:
Stage~A represents each true boundary by exactly one split of $\cA_r$, at a
nearest cutpoint, so the two are at distance $O(r^{-1})$ rather than
coinciding (\S\ref{sec:topology}).

The \emph{topology} of a tree partition is its split structure with the
threshold \emph{values} discarded: formally, the rooted binary tree together
with the splitting coordinate attached to each internal node, and the
ancestor--descendant order of those coordinate tests. Two trees share a
topology when they differ only in where, along the designated coordinates,
their thresholds sit. Recovering the topology is therefore a strictly weaker
task than recovering the partition, and it is the task Stage~A below is asked
to perform.

For a strictly positive weight $\nu$ on $\cX$, let $\Pi^\nu_\tau$ denote the
$\nu$-weighted projection onto functions constant on the leaves of $\tau$;
we write $\Pi_\tau$ for $\Pi^{P_X}_\tau$. The measure $\nu$ is the
\emph{design measure} of the fitting problem: it is a fixed, strictly
positive measure on $\cX$, equivalent to $P_X$ in the sense of
Assumption~\ref{ass:design} below, and every mass, projection, and risk
below is taken with respect to it. Taking $\nu=P_X$ recovers the ordinary
unweighted problem; allowing a general $\nu$ costs nothing in the arguments
and covers weighted least squares, in which the analyst's target is the
$\nu$-weighted rather than the $P_X$-weighted projection of $\gamma_0$.

Given $\tau$, the \emph{full-sample leaf refit} sets, on each leaf $\ell$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refit}
  \widehat\gamma_\tau\restriction_\ell
  =\frac{\sum_{i:X_i\in\ell}Z_i}{\card{\{i:X_i\in\ell\}}} .
\end{equation}
By convention, a leaf containing no observations is assigned the fitted value
of its nearest ancestor that contains at least one (the root mean if none
does). The choice is immaterial to everything below: the minimum-leaf-count
requirement of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d) makes every leaf of a
selected candidate non-empty once $m_n\ge1$, an empty leaf contributes nothing
to an empirical risk, and every population risk below is computed from
$\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_0$ rather than from~\eqref{eq:refit}.
Structure is selected by penalised empirical risk minimisation over the
\emph{feasible} candidates. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasible}
  \cT_n=\bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}:\text{every leaf of }\tau\text{ carries
   at least }m_n\text{ observations}\bigr\},
\end{equation}
and for a penalty $\lambda_n>0$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:select}
  \widehat\tau\in\argmin_{\tau\in\cT_n}
  \bigl\{R_n(\tau)+\lambda_n\card\tau\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $R_n$ is the empirical risk of the leaf-refit fit and $R$ its population
counterpart. Both risks are on the unnormalised scale, so that $R(\tau)$ is
the $\nu$-weighted risk $\E_\nu[\{\Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_0-\gamma_0\}^2]$ rather
than any conditional average of it. Restricting the argmin to $\cT_n$ is what
reconciles selection with the minimum-leaf-mass requirement of
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d): an unconstrained argmin over
$\cT_{\bar L}$ could return a partition violating it, so the constraint
belongs in the selection rule rather than being asserted of its output. It is
required in addition, as a standing condition on the sequence $m_n$ in its own
right rather than as a consequence of anything already assumed, that
\[
  m_n/n\to0 .
\]
This does not follow from the bound $\bar Lm_n\lesssim n$ of
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d): with $\bar L$ fixed in $n$ that bound gives
only $m_n/n\lesssim1/\bar L$, a constant, which $m_n=n/(2\bar L)$ already
attains.

\subsection{The two-stage algorithm}\label{sec:setup-algorithm}

Recovering a topology is not only a weaker task than recovering a partition;
it is also a task a \emph{fixed} grid can discharge, since a grid fine enough
to separate distinct true boundaries can say which coordinate is split where
in the tree even though none of its cutpoints sits at a true threshold. The
procedure below is built on that separation of concerns. Stage~A runs an
ordinary grid-restricted optimal-tree solver of the kind
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} describes, at resolution $r$, under the
\emph{true} leaf and depth budget and with a penalty large relative to the
grid mesh, and then discards every threshold \emph{value} it returns, keeping
only the topology. Stage~B
reinstates the discarded values by an exact one-dimensional scan at each node
in turn, over \emph{observed covariate values} rather than cutpoints. The
combinatorial search is thus paid for once, on a fixed finite grid, and the
continuum enters only through a sequence of univariate scans, each of which is
a sort.

Two features of Stage~A distinguish the procedure below from the one this
paper's earlier drafts described, and both are deliberate. First, the leaf and
depth budgets are \emph{not} inflated: Stage~A searches at $\bar L$ and
$\bar D$, exactly the budgets the truth respects. A true boundary interior to
a grid atom is therefore \emph{not} represented exactly --- no single grid
split can represent it --- and Stage~A instead spends one split at a nearest
cutpoint, accepting an $O(r^{-1})$ localisation error rather than removing it.
Second, and consequently, the penalty is taken \emph{large} relative to that
error, $\lambda_n\gg1/r_n$, which is what makes a tree carrying an extra
split strictly worse than the true-topology comparator even though the extra
split could reduce the population risk by $\Theta(1/r_n)$. Nothing is
collapsed, merged, or post-processed between the two stages: the topology
Stage~B refines is the topology Stage~A returns.

For each resolution $r$, let $\cA_r$ be the full product grid generated by $r$
cutpoints per coordinate, up to fixed multiplicative constants: the ratio of
the largest to the smallest cutpoint spacing, in any coordinate and at any
resolution $r$, is bounded by a fixed quasi-uniformity constant $\bar Q\ge1$.
Its mesh is $O(r^{-1})$, tends to zero as $r\to\infty$, and
\[
  N_r:=\card{\cA_r}\asymp r^p ,
\]
$p$ denoting the number of covariate coordinates of $\cX$. Stage~A searches
the grid-restricted class $\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)$ of \S\ref{sec:setup-basics}
under the \emph{true} leaf budget $\bar L$ and the depth bound $\bar D$
supplied by the analyst (Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) below
requires the unknown truth to respect both). Its feasible set is accordingly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stage-a-feasible}
  \cT^A_n:=\left\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r}):
  \begin{array}{l}
    \tau\text{ is realised by a tree of depth at most }\bar D,\text{ and}\\
    \text{every leaf of }\tau\text{ carries at least }m_n
    \text{ observations}
  \end{array}
  \right\},
\end{equation}
the minimum-leaf-mass requirement of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d) being
imposed \emph{during} Stage~A. Write $s(\tau):=\card\tau-1$ for the number of
internal splits of $\tau$; since $\card\tau=s(\tau)+1$, penalising $s(\tau)$
and penalising $\card\tau$ as in~\eqref{eq:select} differ by the additive
constant $\lambda_n$ and select the same minimiser, and the split count is
used below because every margin in \S\ref{sec:topology} is stated per split.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Two-stage topology recovery and threshold refinement}
\label{alg:twostage}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Data $(X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^n$; leaf budget $\bar L$; depth bound
  $\bar D$; grid resolution $r$ (a sequence $r_n$ in the asymptotic
  statements); penalty $\lambda_n>0$; minimum leaf count $m_n$; interval
  scale $M_n$.
\Ensure A tree partition with topology $\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)$ and
  continuum-valued thresholds $(\hat c_N)_N$.
\Statex \textbf{Step 1 (grid).}
\State Form the resolution-$r$ quasi-uniform grid $\cA_r$; set
  $N_r\gets\card{\cA_r}$ and let $\cT^A_n$ be the feasible set
  \eqref{eq:stage-a-feasible}.
\Statex \textbf{Step 2 (Stage~A: one grid-restricted global search).}
\State $\hat\tau^A_n\gets$ an exact global minimiser of
  $R_n(\tau)+\lambda_ns(\tau)$ over $\tau\in\cT^A_n$.
  \Comment{cutpoints of $\cA_r$ only}
\Statex \textbf{Step 3 (read off the topology).}
\State Retain the topology $\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)$ and, for each node $N$,
  its Stage-A grid threshold $\hat t^A_N$; discard nothing else and merge
  nothing. \Comment{no collapse, no post-processing}
\Statex \textbf{Step 4 (Stage~B: node-by-node exact off-grid scan).}
\For{each node $N$ of $\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)$ in root-first order}
  \State Form the search interval $I_n(N)$ of
    Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}, centred at $\hat t^A_N$ with
    half-width $\rho_n=M_n/r_n$.
  \State Sort the observed values of $N$'s splitting coordinate lying in
    $I_n(N)$ as $x_{(1)}<\dots<x_{(M)}$ and evaluate $R_n$ at each
    of the $M-1$ candidate splits separating consecutive values, holding
    every other threshold in the tree fixed: already refined for every
    ancestor of $N$, still at its Stage-A grid value for every descendant and
    every not-yet-visited node.
  \State $\hat c_N\gets\tfrac12\{x_{(i)}+x_{(i+1)}\}$ for the minimising
    step $i$. \Comment{midpoint convention}
\EndFor
\State \Return the tree with topology $\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)$, thresholds
  $(\hat c_N)_N$, and leaf values \eqref{eq:refit}.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Step~3 is a reading-off, not a computation: because Stage~A operates at the
true budget, the tree it returns already carries exactly one split per true
boundary with probability tending to one (Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}),
so there are no artifacts to remove. What Stage~A does \emph{not} deliver is
threshold precision: each $\hat t^A_N$ sits at a cutpoint, hence within
$O(r^{-1})$ of its true boundary and generically not at it. Step~4's interval
is sized to that error, and the whole burden of the continuum falls on it.
Figure~\ref{fig:stagea-stageb-1d} shows the sequence on a single coordinate,
and Example~\ref{ex:offgrid-1d} works it through numerically.

\begin{definition}[Stage-B search interval]\label{def:stageb-interval}
Let $M_n\to\infty$ with $M_n/r_n\to0$, and set
\[
  \rho_n:=\frac{M_n}{r_n},
\]
so that $\rho_n\to0$ while $\rho_n r_n\to\infty$. Let $N$ be a node of
$\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)$, with splitting coordinate $q$ and Stage-A grid
threshold $\hat t^A_N$, processed in the root-first order of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage}, Step~4. The \emph{search interval} is
\[
  I_n(N):=\bigl[\hat t^A_N-\rho_n,\ \hat t^A_N+\rho_n\bigr].
\]
Three properties of this construction, all used below, are worth recording at
once.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Centred at the Stage-A value, sized above the Stage-A error.}
  Stage~A localises $N$'s boundary to $O_p(r_n^{-1})$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}), and $\rho_n=M_n/r_n$ exceeds that
  error by the diverging factor $M_n$; the true threshold therefore lies in
  $I_n(N)$ with probability tending to one
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}).
  \item \emph{Shrinking, not fixed.} Unlike a bracket bounded by neighbouring
  splits, $I_n(N)$ has width $2\rho_n\to0$. It is eventually contained in any
  fixed interval about the true threshold, which is what
  Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} exploits; it is \emph{not} eventually
  containing one, so a nesting argument in the opposite direction is not
  available and is not used.
  \item \emph{Ancestors enter only through the cell, not the endpoints.} The
  endpoints depend on Stage~A alone and are therefore fixed before the sweep
  begins. The \emph{observations} scanned are those in $N$'s cell as
  determined by the already-refined ancestor thresholds, so the root-first
  order still matters; but no endpoint of $I_n(N)$ moves during the sweep,
  and no circularity between a node's own scan and its descendants' arises.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[A grid-misaligned boundary, end to end]\label{ex:offgrid-1d}
Let $\cX=[0,1]\times[0,1]$ and let the true tree have a single boundary, at
$X_1=0.37$. Take $r=10$, so that $\cA_{10}$ places cutpoints at
$0.0,0.1,\dots,1.0$ on each coordinate and $0.37$ falls strictly inside the
atom $(0.3,0.4]$. No single split of $\cA_{10}$ represents the truth exactly.
Stage~A nonetheless spends exactly one: at the true budget $\bar L=2$ it
returns the split at $X_1=0.4$, the cutpoint nearest $0.37$, and pays an
approximation cost of order $r^{-1}$ for the mismatch. It does \emph{not}
return a second split at $X_1=0.3$ --- that would cost a leaf the budget does
not have, and, at a larger budget, would cost a penalty $\lambda_n\gg1/r_n$
exceeding the $O(1/r_n)$ risk it could recover. So the recovered topology has
one split on coordinate~$1$, at distance $0.03$ from the truth. That split is
the root; with, say, $M_n=3$, Step~4 gives it the interval
$I_n(\mathrm{root})=[0.4-0.3,\,0.4+0.3]=[0.1,0.7]$, which contains $0.37$.
Stage~B sorts the observed values of $X_1$ in $[0.1,0.7]$, evaluates the
candidate splits between consecutive values, and reports the midpoint of the
minimising step. By Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} that
off-grid value is within $O_p(n^{-1})$ of $0.37$, rather than merely within
the grid mesh $0.1$ or the interval half-width $0.3$.
\end{example}

presumes: \S\ref{sec:setup-assumptions} the standing assumptions common to

under which the tree it targets exists at all, and \S\ref{sec:setup-solver}
the fidelity conditions its two solvers must meet.

\subsection{Standing assumptions}\label{sec:setup-assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Sampling]\label{ass:iid}
$(X_1,Z_1),\dots,(X_n,Z_n)$ are independent and identically distributed copies
of $(X,Z)\sim P$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Design measure and boundedness]\label{ass:design}
The design measure $\nu$ is a fixed measure on $\cX$, not depending on $n$,
for which there exist constants $0<\underline W\le\bar W<\infty$ with
\[
  \underline W\,dP_X\ \le\ d\nu\ \le\ \bar W\,dP_X .
\]
Moreover $\gamma_0$ is bounded: $\sup_{x\in\cX}\abs{\gamma_0(x)}\le
C_\gamma<\infty$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What replaces an identification condition here]
\label{rem:design-role}
Assumption~\ref{ass:design} is used for exactly two purposes below, and for
nothing else. The two-sided comparison of $\nu$ with $P_X$ ensures that every
grid atom of positive $P_X$-mass has positive $\nu$-mass, which is what makes
the population ANOVA identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:grid-optimal} sharp; and the
bound on $\gamma_0$ ensures that the profiled leaf means appearing in
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} are bounded, uniformly in the candidate
threshold. Neither use requires $\nu$ to arise from any particular modelling
consideration. In an application in which the design measure is itself
estimated, $\nu$ should be read as the population weight; the arguments below
are stated at a fixed $\nu$.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Moments]\label{ass:moments}
$\E[Z^2\mid X=x]\le C_Z<\infty$ for every $x\in\cX$. Equivalently, in view of
Assumption~\ref{ass:design}, the conditional residual second moment is
uniformly bounded:
\[
  \sup_x\E\bigl[\{Z-\gamma_0(X)\}^2\mid X=x\bigr]<\infty .
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Finite candidate partition family]\label{ass:finite}
The grid atoms $\cA$ generated by the pre-specified cutpoints are finite in
number, fixed in $n$, and each of positive mass: $\card\cA<\infty$ and
$P(a)>0$ for every $a\in\cA$. ($\cX$ itself is not required to be finite; see
\S\ref{sec:setup-basics}.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Estimator construction]\label{ass:construct}
The fit is constructed as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Loss.} The fit is by squared error, so that excess risk equals
  squared $L_2(\nu)$ distance to $\gamma_0$.
  \item \emph{Leaf refit.} Leaf values are given by~\eqref{eq:refit}: the
  within-leaf response mean.
  \item \emph{Range restriction.} Reported fitted values are confined to a
  fixed compact interval containing the range of $\gamma_0$, whose existence is
  Assumption~\ref{ass:design}. Structure selection uses the
  \emph{unrestricted} leaf refit; the restriction is applied only to the
  reported fit.
  \item \emph{Leaf mass.} Selection is restricted to the feasible set
  \eqref{eq:feasible}: candidates whose every leaf carries at least $m_n$
  observations, with $\bar Lm_n\lesssim n$. This is a property of the
  selection rule, not a claim about its output.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Solver fidelity]\label{ass:solver}
The optimisation procedure computing~\eqref{eq:select} considers only tree
partitions whose splits lie at the pre-specified cutpoints of $\cX$: the
search space is $\cT_{\bar L}$, never a data-dependent or
continuously-refined set of thresholds. When $\cX$ is itself finite with every
value a pre-specified cutpoint, this holds automatically for any solver, since
every threshold on a finite value set already lies at a cutpoint; it is
recorded as a standing assumption because it is not automatic once $\cX$ is a
general space and a generic branch-and-bound solver would otherwise consider
splits at observed data values, which are neither fixed nor finite in $n$.
Relaxing precisely this restriction --- without paying for it in the
combinatorial search --- is the purpose of the two-stage procedure of
\S\ref{sec:setup-twostage}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed leaf budget]\label{ass:budget}
$\bar L$ is a constant, fixed in $n$, with $\bar L\le\card\cA$; consequently
$\cT_{\bar L}$ is a fixed finite set and $\card{\cT_{\bar L}}$ does not depend
on $n$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Exact global optimisation]\label{ass:global}
$\widehat\tau$ attains the global minimum in~\eqref{eq:select} over the
feasible set $\cT_n$ of~\eqref{eq:feasible}, and $\lambda_n\to0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Sufficient class]\label{def:sufficient}
\[
  \cS=\bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}:
  \Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_0=\gamma_0\ \ P\text{-a.s.}\bigr\},
\]
equivalently the set of $\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}$ on whose leaves $\gamma_0$ is
$P$-a.s.\ constant. The characterisation is independent of the strictly
positive weight $\nu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Structural sparsity]\label{ass:sparsity}
$\cS\ne\emptyset$; equivalently, some tree partition built from the
pre-specified cutpoints, with at most $\bar L$ leaves, represents $\gamma_0$
exactly.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity} is the grid-exact hypothesis this paper is
designed to dispense with: it requires every true threshold to coincide with a
pre-specified cutpoint. Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) below
replaces
it by its continuum analogue.

\subsection{Continuum sparsity and the two-stage estimator}
\label{sec:setup-twostage}

The class-relativised form of Definition~\ref{def:sufficient} will be used
below. For a general tree class $\cC$, define
\[
  \cS(\cC)
  :=
  \bigl\{\tau\in\cC:
  \Pi^\nu_\tau\gamma_0=\gamma_0\ \ P\text{-a.s.}\bigr\}.
\]
Thus $\cS=\cS(\cT_{\bar L})$ denotes the grid-restricted class of
Definition~\ref{def:sufficient}, whereas
\[
  \cS^{\mathrm{cont}}
  :=
  \cS(\cTcont)
\]
denotes its continuum analogue, $\cTcont$ being the class of axis-aligned tree
partitions whose thresholds are unrestricted real numbers, with at most
$\bar L$ leaves and realisable by a tree of depth at most $\bar D$. Both
restrictions are carried by the class itself, so that
Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) below asserts exactly
$\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\ne\emptyset$ and nothing stronger. This is strictly
weaker than the grid-exact Assumption~\ref{ass:sparsity}, because a true
continuum boundary need not coincide with any cutpoint of a finite grid.

Nonemptiness of $\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$ alone does not pin down a single object
for later results to be \emph{about}: the definition below fixes what ``the
true tree'' means.

\begin{definition}[The true tree]\label{def:true-tree}
Call $\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$ \emph{minimal} if
$\card\tau\le\card{\tau'}$ for every $\tau'\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$. Two
minimal elements of $\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$ are \emph{equivalent} if they
induce the same partition of $\cX$ into leaf cells
(Definition~\ref{def:tree-vocab}) --- equivalently, if every realisation of
one is a realisation of the other, whatever the order of the underlying
split tests. \emph{The true tree} is a minimal element of
$\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$, and \textbf{Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S)
is strengthened, for the remainder of this paper, to assert that all minimal
elements of $\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$ are equivalent in this sense.} Every
subsequent use of ``the true tree'', ``the true node $N$'', ``the true
boundaries'', and ``$c_{0,k}$'' refers to (a fixed choice of realisation of)
this equivalence class; a stated bound on the true tree's depth
(Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S)) refers to the minimal-depth
realisation of it, per the convention of Definition~\ref{def:tree-vocab}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Uniqueness: assumed in general, derived under an added support
condition]\label{rem:true-tree-open}
Definition~\ref{def:true-tree} \emph{asserts} that the minimal elements of
$\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}$ coincide as partitions; in general this is not derived
from clauses (S), (J), (Dens), (Sep), (Node), (Local-Jump), (Reduced), and
(Joint) alone, and in general it is carried as an additional standing
hypothesis rather than as a consequence of the others. Under one added
support condition it \emph{is} derived:
Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition} of \S\ref{sec:setup-uniqueness} shows that
(S), (Dens), (Node), and (Reduced) --- together with compactness,
full-dimensionality, and convexity of $\cX$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:support-geometry}) --- already force
this coincidence, for a witness satisfying the leaf-level hypotheses, without
appeal to (J), (Sep), or (Local-Jump). \S\ref{sec:setup-uniqueness} also
supplies the canonical rooted-tree realisation
(Definition~\ref{def:canonical-realisation}) that node-indexed assumptions
such as (J) implicitly require, and shows (Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-nonunique})
that partition-level uniqueness does not by itself extend to the ordered
tree. Whether the geometric condition of
Assumption~\ref{ass:support-geometry} can be relaxed, or dropped in favour of
(Sep) and (J) alone, is left open, and is carried on the open-questions
ledger of
\texttt{quality\_reports/plans/2026-09-02\_paper-a-theory-clarity-remediation.md}.
\end{remark}

It is worth stating at the outset what this paper borrows and what it adds,
since the two are easily conflated. The localisation machinery is not new: the
rate-$n$ behaviour of a least-squares change point under random design is due
to \citet{chan1993} and \citet{seijosen2011}; the simultaneous estimation of
finitely many step heights alongside their locations is \citet{baiperron1998},
\citet{yaoau1989}, and \citet{boysen2009}; and the argmax theorem for
discontinuous, locally-defined criteria that converts a drifting local process
into a tight minimiser is \citet{seootsu2018}. The profiling-correction
argument of Remark~\ref{rem:profiling-correction} is likewise a standard
device, applied here rather than developed. Confidence sets for split points
have also been studied directly, by \citet{banerjee2007}, but under model
misspecification (a smooth true regression function approximated by a tree),
which yields non-standard, cube-root asymptotics; that is a different regime
from the one considered here, where the truth is exactly a tree, and it is
what makes the $n^{-1}$ threshold rate of
Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} available at all.

What the tree structure adds is narrower, and amounts to two reductions and
one population margin. First, Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} shows that
refining one threshold inside a tree is not a two-group problem: the moving
slab must be decomposed into source--target leaf pairs, and the resulting
finite sum of marked processes --- not any single change-point process ---
is what carries the drift. Second, Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}
shows that superconsistency survives conditioning on \emph{estimated}
rather than true ancestors, which has no counterpart in a fixed-design
break model and is what makes the levelwise induction legitimate. Third,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} supplies a \emph{global} topology margin: any tree
within the true split budget that fails to match the true tree structurally
pays a fixed, non-vanishing excess risk, whatever coordinates it splits on.
That is what lets a fixed-grid search identify a continuum topology at the
true leaf budget, and it has no analogue in a change-point literature where
the number and identity of the breaking coordinates are given. Everything
else below is the cited machinery instantiated at those three points.

\begin{assumption}[Continuum sparsity]\label{ass:continuum-sparsity}
The regression function \(\gamma_0\) satisfies the following condition.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item[(S)] \emph{Tree realisability.}
  The function \(\gamma_0\) is piecewise constant on a continuum
  partition realised by an axis-aligned tree with at most \(\bar L\) leaves
  and depth at most \(\bar D\), where \(\bar L\) and \(\bar D\) are fixed in
  \(n\). Equivalently, \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\ne\emptyset\) for the class
  \(\cTcont\) of \S\ref{sec:setup-twostage}.
\end{enumerate}
The two budgets are required to be \emph{compatible}, in the sense that the
leaf budget is attainable at the stated depth:
\[
  \lceil\log_2\bar L\rceil\ \le\ \bar D,
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \bar L\le2^{\bar D}.
\]
A binary tree of depth \(\bar D\) has at most \(2^{\bar D}\) leaves, so a pair
with \(\bar L>2^{\bar D}\) would leave the leaf budget inoperative --- the
depth bound alone would cap the leaf count at \(2^{\bar D}<\bar L\) --- and
every appearance of \(\bar L\) below would then overstate the class actually
searched.

Clause (S) is to be read together with Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}, which
selects the minimal element of \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) that every later result
is \emph{about}, and which strengthens (S) by asserting that the minimal
elements coincide as partitions. That strengthening is stated there and is not
restated here.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What continuum sparsity buys, and where]
\label{rem:sparsity-role}
Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity} is used for exactly three purposes
below, and for nothing else. It makes the target of every later result
\emph{exist}, so that ``the true tree'' of Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}
denotes something and \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) is not vacuously empty; it
supplies the two budget constants that Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage} searches
under, Stage~A searching \(\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)\) at depth \(\bar D\), the
analyst-supplied \(\bar D\) of \S\ref{sec:setup-algorithm} being exactly the
bound (S) requires the unknown truth to respect; and it enters two population
constants through its \emph{fixedness} in \(n\) alone --- the comparator term
\((\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}, and the finite template count of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}'s compactness argument. Because Stage~A now searches
at \((\bar L,\bar D)\) rather than at an inflated pair, the compatibility
condition needs no propagation: the pair (S) already requires to be
compatible is the pair Stage~A uses. No stochastic
argument below invokes (S): every concentration and rate statement is proved
at a fixed, already-existing truth.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Jump regularity]\label{ass:jump-regularity}
Fix constants \(0<\kappa_{\min}\le\kappa_{\max}<\infty\), independent of
\(n\), and let \(N\) range over the internal nodes of the true tree, with
splitting coordinate \(q\) and threshold \(c_{0,N}\). Both conditions below
hold uniformly over such \(N\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item[(J)] \emph{Genuine jumps, pooled across the split.}
  Write
  \[
    H^-_N:=\{x\in R_N:x_q\le c_{0,N}\},
    \qquad
    H^+_N:=R_N\setminus H^-_N
  \]
  for the two sides of \(N\)'s split --- equivalently, the cells of \(N\)'s
  two children (Definition~\ref{def:tree-vocab}) --- and let
  \[
    \gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}_{N,\pm}
    :=
    \frac1{\nu(H^\pm_N)}\int_{H^\pm_N}\gamma_0\,d\nu
  \]
  be the \(\nu\)-averaged population value on each side, each side
  \emph{pooled over all of its own descendant leaves}. Then
  \[
    \kappa_{\min}
    \ \le\
    \abs{\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}_{N,+}-\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}_{N,-}}
    \ \le\ \kappa_{\max}.
  \]
  Both averages are well defined, since \(\nu(H^\pm_N)\ge q_{\min}>0\) by
  Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Node).

  \item[(Local-Jump)] \emph{Jumps across every adjacent leaf pair.}
  For every pair of leaves adjacent across the boundary \(\{x_q=c_{0,N}\}\),
  the corresponding population values \(\gamma^-_{N,j}\) and
  \(\gamma^+_{N,j}\) satisfy
  \(\kappa_{\min}\le\abs{\gamma^+_{N,j}-\gamma^-_{N,j}}\le\kappa_{\max}\).

  \item[(Reduced)] \emph{Reducedness of the true partition.}
  Clause (Local-Jump) holds for \emph{every} geometrically adjacent pair of
  true leaves --- that is, whenever two leaves of the true partition share a
  relatively open codimension-one face, their population values differ by an
  amount in \([\kappa_{\min},\kappa_{\max}]\), whether or not that face is
  the face of a single true split. Equivalently, the true partition is
  \emph{reduced}: no two geometrically adjacent true leaves carry the same
  value.
\end{enumerate}
The three clauses share the constants \(\kappa_{\min},\kappa_{\max}\) by
construction rather than by consequence: the constants are chosen as a common
lower and upper bound for \emph{all} the families of differences ---
(J)'s two pooled side averages, (Local-Jump)'s adjacent leaf pairs, and
(Reduced)'s geometrically adjacent pairs --- so that any family may be bounded
by either constant below. No implication between the clauses is claimed; see
Remark~\ref{rem:jump-role}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Reducedness is an independent hypothesis, not a consequence of
minimality]\label{rem:reduced-independent}
Clause (Reduced) strengthens (Local-Jump) from the leaf pairs adjacent across
a \emph{true split's own} face to \emph{all} geometrically adjacent true-leaf
pairs, and it is worth being explicit that this strengthening is not free ---
in particular, that it does \emph{not} follow from the minimality built into
Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}. A minimal-leaf-count tree can carry two
geometrically adjacent, equal-valued leaves whenever their union is not
itself an axis-aligned rectangle, because no tree split can then represent
that union as a single leaf and the merge which would reduce the leaf count is
simply unavailable. Concretely, split \([0,1]^2\) at \(x_1=1/2\) and split
only the left child at \(x_2=1/2\), giving the three leaves
\(UL=[0,\tfrac12]\times[\tfrac12,1]\),
\(LL=[0,\tfrac12]\times[0,\tfrac12]\), and \(R=[\tfrac12,1]\times[0,1]\);
give \(UL\) and \(R\) a common value and \(LL\) a different one. Then \(UL\)
and \(R\) share the positive-length face
\(\{x_1=\tfrac12,\ x_2\in[\tfrac12,1]\}\), so they are geometrically
adjacent and equal-valued, yet \(UL\cup R\) is L-shaped and the three-leaf
tree is minimal. Clause (Reduced) is therefore stated as a standing
hypothesis in its own right. It is used in exactly one place ---
Step~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}'s proof, where a candidate rectangle
straddling two true leaves must be shown to incur strictly positive error ---
and if it fails, a zero-risk tree outside the structural-equivalence class of
Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching} could exist and that step's
refinement argument would require re-examination.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What jump regularity buys, and where]\label{rem:jump-role}
The three clauses of Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity} are invoked at
disjoint places, and the distinctions are the pooling and the notion of
adjacency.

Clause (Local-Jump) is used wherever a jump must be visible at the level of an
\emph{individual} pair of leaves adjacent across a true face, because the
argument decomposes the face into cells and needs each one to contribute. That
is the lower half of Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s margin, where two points
straddling an omitted boundary must share a leaf and therefore incur squared
error at least \(\kappa_{\min}^2/4\); and it is every drift computation of
\S\ref{sec:stageb} --- Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}'s prism scaling, in which
each active face cell carries its own jump mark, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability}, which transfers those marks to perturbed
leaf pairs.

Clause (J) is used only where a single \emph{pooled} comparison across a whole
split suffices. That is the comparator half of
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}, where snapping a true boundary to a neighbouring
cutpoint costs at most \(\kappa_{\max}^2\) per unit of slab mass, and the same
comparator's approximation bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(b).

Clause (Reduced) is used in exactly one place, and only there: Step~3 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}'s proof, whose refinement argument must exclude a
candidate rectangle straddling two true leaves that meet along a face no
single true split provides. (Local-Jump) does not cover that configuration,
which is why (Reduced) is stated separately; see
Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-independent}.

None of the clauses is used to bound anything about the estimator: all three
are population statements, and every stochastic step below takes them as
given.

Whether (J) and (Local-Jump) are genuinely different conditions or the same
condition read at two resolutions is not settled here. (Local-Jump) constrains
more pairs than (J) does, but (J) constrains a mass-weighted average of them,
and averaging can in principle cancel what the individual pairs do not; no
implication is asserted in either direction, and the citations above are
therefore kept clause-specific rather than consolidated. The question ---
together with the consequent question of whether some of the citations of (J)
above could be discharged from (Local-Jump) alone --- is not resolved in this
paper; it is carried on the correctness ledger
\texttt{quality\_reports/\allowbreak plans/\allowbreak
2026-09-02\_paper-a-theory-clarity-remediation.md}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of the local-jump clause]
Condition (Local-Jump) is stronger than requiring merely that a split produce
some aggregate risk reduction.  It rules out cancellation caused by an
adjacent pair of leaves having the same population value.  Any design whose
true leaf values are pairwise distinct satisfies the condition by
construction, with \(\kappa_{\min}\) the smallest and \(\kappa_{\max}\) the
largest gap between values on leaves adjacent across a true face.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Local design regularity]\label{ass:local-design}
Let \(N\) range over the internal nodes of the true tree, with splitting
coordinate \(q\) and threshold \(c_{0,N}\), and let \(R_N\) be the closed
axis-aligned box associated with \(N\) (Definition~\ref{def:tree-vocab}). The
design measure \(\nu\) satisfies the following, with every constant fixed
uniformly over such \(N\) and in \(n\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item[(Sep)] \emph{Separation and interiority.}
  Distinct true boundaries on the same coordinate are separated by at least
  \(\varsigma>0\), and every true boundary is at least \(\varsigma\) from the
  boundary of the support of its own splitting coordinate \(X_q\). (Which
  measure's support is meant does not matter: \(\nu\) and \(P_X\) are mutually
  absolutely continuous by Assumption~\ref{ass:design}, hence have the same
  support.)

  \item[(Dens)] \emph{Local connected density.} With \(\varsigma\) as in
  (Sep), let
  \[
    R_N^+
    :=
    \bigl\{x\in\cX:
      \operatorname{dist}_\infty(x,R_N)\le\varsigma/2
    \bigr\}
  \]
  denote the \emph{collar} of \(R_N\). Two things are required of it.
  \begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item[(Dens-i)] \emph{Two-sided local density.} On \(R_N^+\), the
    covariate law under \(\nu\) admits a density \(f_{\nu,N}\) with respect to
    Lebesgue measure, satisfying
    \[
      0<f_{\min}
      \le f_{\nu,N}(x)
      \le f_{\max}<\infty
      \qquad\text{for every }x\in R_N^+ .
    \]
    \item[(Dens-ii)] \emph{Bounded collar diameters.} The sets \(R_N^+\) are
    fixed compact rectangles whose coordinate diameters are bounded, uniformly
    over true nodes, by a fixed constant \(D_{\max}<\infty\).
  \end{enumerate}

  \item[(Node)] \emph{Positive node mass.}
  Every true node and leaf has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(q_{\min}>0\), fixed in
  \(n\).

  \item[(Joint)] \emph{Ancestor--descendant joint density.}
  If an ancestor split uses coordinate \(q_a\), a descendant split uses
  \(q_\ell\), and \(q_a\ne q_\ell\), the pair \((X_{q_a},X_{q_\ell})\) has a
  joint density bounded above by a fixed constant \(\bar f_2<\infty\) on a
  neighbourhood of \((c_{0,a},c_{0,\ell})\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What local design regularity buys, and where]
\label{rem:local-design-role}
Each clause of Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design} has a single job, and the
jobs do not overlap.

\emph{(Sep)} is used everywhere a local region must be shown to contain no
\emph{second} true boundary, and nowhere else. That is: the choice of \(r_0\)
preceding Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}, which makes the mesh smaller than
\(\varsigma\) so that no grid atom holds two boundaries; the paired face patch
inside that lemma's proof; the separation and collar clauses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}, where a search interval must be shown
not to contain a second true threshold; the fixed interval
\(B^-=(c_0-\varsigma/4,c_0+\varsigma/4)\) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}, whose width \(\varsigma/2<\varsigma\) is
what excludes every other same-coordinate true threshold from it
outright --- used in that form in Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}'s proof
--- and the equal-coordinate case of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}, where (Sep) makes an ancestor collar and
a descendant window disjoint outright. The interiority half does one further
thing: it is what places the collar \(R_N^+\) of (Dens) inside the support, so
that (Dens-i) is a hypothesis about a set the design actually charges.

\emph{(Dens-i)} is used in both directions and for nothing else. The floor
\(f_{\min}\) converts a slab's \emph{width} into a \(\nu\)-mass, which is what
produces the \(\Theta(\abs{c-c_0})\) drift of
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(iii) and the lower half of
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s margin; the ceiling \(f_{\max}\) converts a
slab's width into a \emph{risk cost}, which is what bounds the comparator loss
in the same lemma and, with (Joint) absent, nothing further. \emph{(Dens-ii)}'s
\(D_{\max}\) appears only where a mass bound must be turned into a
cross-sectional-area bound --- inside
\(c_{\mathrm{box}}:=1/(f_{\max}D_{\max})\), fixed before
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} --- and where a node box's extent along one
coordinate must be a fixed compact interval, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}'s \([q_N^-,q_N^+]\).

\emph{(Node)} supplies one quantity, the floor \(q_{\min}\) on the mass of
every true node and leaf, and it is invoked wherever a denominator must not
degenerate: the cross-sectional bound
\(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}q_{\min}\) used from
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} onwards, the leaf-refit denominators of
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability}, and --- with
Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid} and~\ref{ass:design} and the law of large numbers ---
the sample-size floor at the true partition recorded in
Remark~\ref{rem:not-assumed}.

\emph{(Joint)} is used in exactly one place: the unequal-coordinate case of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}, to bound the chance that a single
observation lies simultaneously in an ancestor collar of half-width \(M/n\)
and a descendant window of half-width \(U/n\). When the two coordinates
coincide, (Sep) settles the matter and (Joint) is not invoked.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of the density clause]\label{rem:dens-interpretation}
The lower bound in (Dens-i) implies that the support has no gaps in a
neighbourhood of any true splitting face.  Thus, whenever \(x^-\) and \(x^+\)
differ only in the splitting coordinate and lie sufficiently close to opposite
sides of a true boundary, both points belong to the local support.  This rules
out, by construction, supports consisting locally of disjoint rectangles.  It
is the local version of the customary assumption that covariates have a
density bounded away from zero and infinity on a compact connected support.
The converse implication need not hold: connected support alone does not
require its density to be bounded away from zero.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What (Dens) costs in generality]\label{rem:dens-scope}
Clause (Dens-i) is a genuine additional restriction and not merely a scoping
convention, and it is worth being explicit about what it excludes.
\S\ref{sec:setup-basics} allows a coordinate of \(\cX\) to be a finite
category set, and Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} allows that coordinate's
cutpoint family to be a grouping of its values; (Dens-i) requires instead that
every coordinate on which the \emph{true} tree splits carry a Lebesgue density
on the collar \(R_N^+\). Every result of
\S\S\ref{sec:topology}--\ref{sec:stageb} is therefore stated for continuous
true splitting coordinates, and every cross-sectional-measure argument below
--- in particular the definition of \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\) preceding
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} --- is written for the Lebesgue case only. A
discrete or mixed \emph{true splitting} coordinate is excluded rather than
covered: it would violate the \(\omega_{N,j}(c)=\Theta(\abs{c-c_0})\) prism
scaling of Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(iii) on which the \(n^{-1}\) rate
depends, and an off-grid threshold is in any case not a meaningful target on a
coordinate with finitely many values. What (Dens-i) does \emph{not} restrict
are the coordinates the true tree never splits on, nor the coordinates
Stage~A's grid \(\cA_r\) ranges over --- though
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density} below, which this paper's Stage-A argument
does invoke, restricts both.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Global design density]\label{ass:global-density}
The support of \(P_X\) is a fixed compact axis-aligned box
\(\cX=\prod_{j=1}^p[a_j,b_j]\), and the design measure \(\nu\)
admits a density \(f\) with respect to Lebesgue measure on all of \(\cX\)
satisfying
\[
  0<f_{\min}\ \le\ f(x)\ \le\ f_{\max}<\infty
  \qquad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e.\ }x\in\cX,
\]
with \(f_{\min},f_{\max}\) the same fixed constants as in
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens-i) and fixed in \(n\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What the global density bound replaces, and why the local
version does not suffice]\label{rem:global-density-role}
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density} supersedes clause (Dens-i) of
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design} for the purposes of this paper's Stage-A
argument: it asserts on all of \(\cX\) exactly what (Dens-i) asserts on the
node collars \(R_N^+\), with the same two constants, so it implies (Dens-i)
and every use of (Dens-i) below may equally be read as a use of it. It is
invoked by name in exactly one result, Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}, and the
reason the node-local version will not do there is structural rather than
technical.

Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} must exclude candidate trees that split on the
\emph{wrong coordinate} --- trees bearing no relation to the true tree's node
boxes at all. Its mechanism is to bound the risk excess below by
\(f_{\min}\) times a purely Lebesgue-geometric discrepancy, uniformly over
\emph{every} admissible candidate; a density floor holding only on
neighbourhoods of the true splitting faces says nothing about a candidate
whose leaves live where the truth has no face, so no such uniform bound is
available from (Dens-i). Two counterexamples make the point concretely.
A density that is exactly zero on part of \(\cX\) --- \(f=2\) on two diagonal
quadrants of \([0,1]^2\) and \(0\) elsewhere --- lets a wrong-coordinate tree
achieve zero excess risk, and is excluded by
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density} outright rather than by any patch. A
correlated design \(f_\rho(x_1,x_2)=1+\rho(2x_1-1)(2x_2-1)\) with
\(\gamma_0=\mathbf 1\{x_1>1/2\}\) does \emph{not} degrade the margin as
\(\rho\to1\): the Lebesgue discrepancy of a tree splitting on \(x_2\) instead
is \(\int_{[0,1]^2}(\mathbf 1\{x_1>1/2\}-\tfrac12)^2\,dx=\tfrac14\) for every
\(\rho\), so the resulting bound \(f_{\min}/4\) is invariant to how the
density is reweighted within \([f_{\min},f_{\max}]\). This is the sense in
which Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} needs a floor and a ceiling, but no further
control of the density's \emph{shape}.

What the global bound costs is the generality (Dens-i) deliberately retained:
Remark~\ref{rem:dens-scope}'s observation that a coordinate the true tree
never splits on may be discrete no longer applies, since a density is now
required on every coordinate of \(\cX\). This paper's scope is therefore
continuous covariates throughout, not merely continuous \emph{true splitting}
covariates, and the grid cardinality \(N_r\asymp r^p\) of
\S\ref{sec:setup-algorithm} counts all \(p\) of them --- which settles, for
this architecture, the question Remark~\ref{rem:dens-scope} left open.
\end{remark}

The estimator that operates under this assumption is
Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage} of \S\ref{sec:setup-algorithm}, whose grid
$\cA_r$, budgets $\bar L$ and $\bar D$, and Stage-A feasible set
$\cT^A_n$ are defined there. What the algorithm statement does not fix, and
what the results below need, is how a Stage-A split is to be \emph{compared}
with a true boundary; that convention is an accounting device for the proofs
rather than a step any solver executes, and it is recorded here.

Only the topology \(\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)\) and the Stage-A grid thresholds
\((\hat t^A_N)_N\) are retained from Stage~A; nothing is merged, paired, or
discarded (Step~3 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage}). Both a Stage-A split and
a true boundary are points on a coordinate, so the distance between them is
the ordinary one, and Stage~A is said to \emph{localise} a true boundary to
within \(\eta\) when the matched split's threshold lies within \(\eta\) of it.
Because every Stage-A threshold sits at a cutpoint of \(\cA_r\), the best
achievable \(\eta\) is half the mesh, of order \(r^{-1}\); the results of
\S\ref{sec:topology} show that this order is attained and, in particular,
that a true boundary interior to an atom is neither omitted nor represented
twice.

The minimum-leaf requirement of Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d) is imposed
in Stage~A, at the budget \(\bar L\)
(\eqref{eq:stage-a-feasible}). Two standing rate conditions on \(m_n\) are
carried in every rate display below, and neither follows from
Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(d)'s \(\bar Lm_n\lesssim n\):
\[
  m_n=o(n/r_n),
  \qquad
  m_n/\sqrt n\to\infty .
\]
The first keeps the comparator of Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle} feasible ---
its leaves have fixed positive population mass, so an \(m_n\) growing more
slowly than \(n/r_n\) is more than enough --- while the second is what makes
Stage~A's \emph{empirical} leaf-count floor imply the \emph{population}
leaf-mass floor the concentration argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment} requires; see
Lemma~\ref{lem:empirical-feasible-mass}. Both reappear as clauses (R4) and
(R5) of the rate conditions~\eqref{eq:stage-a-rates}, where they are collected
with the conditions on \(r_n\) and \(\lambda_n\).

\subsection{Uniqueness of the true partition}\label{sec:setup-uniqueness}

Remark~\ref{rem:true-tree-open} leaves Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}'s
equivalence claim as an open standing hypothesis in general. This subsection
derives it under one added geometric condition on the support.

\begin{assumption}[Support geometry]\label{ass:support-geometry}
\(\cX\subset\R^d\) is compact, full-dimensional, and convex; a box is the
principal intended example. This clause, denoted (Conn), is invoked only by
the results of this subsection and nowhere else in the paper.
\end{assumption}

Throughout this subsection, piecewise constancy of \(\gamma_0\)
(Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S)) is read pointwise: for every
leaf \(L\) of a realising tree there is a number \(v_L\) with
\(\gamma_0(x)=v_L\) for every \(x\) in \(L\)'s interior, consistent with the
boundary-face convention of Definition~\ref{def:tree-vocab}.

\begin{definition}[\(\nu\)-equivalence of finite partitions]\label{def:nu-equivalence}
Finite measurable partitions \(\cP=\{A_1,\ldots,A_K\}\) and
\(\cQ=\{B_1,\ldots,B_M\}\) of \(\cX\) are \(\nu\)\emph{-equivalent},
written \(\cP\equiv_\nu\cQ\), if \(K=M\) and there is a bijection
\(\rho:\cP\to\cQ\) with \(\nu(A\triangle\rho(A))=0\) for every
\(A\in\cP\). This is a relation between partitions only; it does not
identify rooted-tree realisations up to reordering split tests, a distinct
matter taken up in Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-nonunique}. A partition
\(\cQ\) \emph{refines} \(\cP\) modulo \(\nu\) if there is
\(\phi:\cQ\to\cP\) with \(\nu(B\setminus\phi(B))=0\) for every
\(B\in\cQ\).
\end{definition}

For a tree \(\tau\), write \(\cP(\tau)\) for its leaf partition. Define
\[
L^\star:=\min\{\card\tau:\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\},
\]
well defined and finite since \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\ne\emptyset\)
(Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S)) and leaf counts are positive
integers bounded above by \(\bar L\). Call \(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\)
\emph{minimal} if \(\card\tau=L^\star\).

Both hypotheses of the theorem below are conditions on the single tree
\(\tau^{\dagger}\), quantified existentially over \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\),
which Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) defines without reference
to Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}. The parenthetical citations to (Reduced)
and (Node) identify the standing clauses that discharge these conditions in
the intended application, read with \(\tau^{\dagger}\) in place of ``the
true tree''; neither clause is invoked as stated, so nothing here
presupposes Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}'s uniqueness assertion.

\begin{theorem}[Unique coarsest realising partition]\label{thm:true-partition}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) and
Assumption~\ref{ass:support-geometry} hold, that \(\nu\) and Lebesgue
measure are mutually absolutely continuous on \(\cX\) (as supplied by
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density}, or by
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens-i) at the root node when
\(L^\star\ge2\)), and that there exists a minimal
\(\tau^{\dagger}\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item every pair of geometrically face-adjacent leaves of \(\tau^{\dagger}\)
has distinct population values, as required by (Reduced)
(Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}) for \(\tau^{\dagger}\). This is a
genuine additional hypothesis on \(\tau^{\dagger}\), not a consequence of
its minimality (Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-independent}): merging two
equal-valued face-adjacent leaves need not yield a partition realisable by
an axis-aligned tree, so a minimal realisation need not automatically
satisfy (Reduced).
\item every leaf \(A\in\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) satisfies \(\nu(A)\ge q_{\min}\)
for some \(q_{\min}>0\), as supplied by the leaf instance of (Node)
(Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}) for \(\tau^{\dagger}\).
\end{enumerate}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item every \(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) refines \(\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\)
modulo \(\nu\), and consequently \(\card\tau\ge L^\star\);
\item a realisation \(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) is minimal if and only if
\(\cP(\tau)\equiv_\nu\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\);
\item for every minimal \(\tau\), matched cells have equal closures, so all
minimal realisations share the same geometric leaf closures, boundary
faces, boundary set, and numerical threshold locations.
\end{enumerate}
The proof is given in \S\ref{sec:supp-setup}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}]\label{rem:uniqueness-scope}
The proof uses (Reduced) for \(\tau^{\dagger}\) and the connectedness and
generic-perturbability of leaf interiors that (Conn) supplies; it uses
neither minimality of the comparison tree \(\tau\) nor (Dens) at that step,
which is invoked only afterward, to upgrade a Lebesgue-null bad set to a
\(\nu\)-null one. Two edge cases are worth recording. If \(L^\star=1\), both
(Local-Jump) and (J) are vacuous (no adjacent leaf pairs or internal nodes
exist), every realising partition trivially refines \(\{\cX\}\), and
uniqueness holds without the transversal-chain argument. The corollary
below transfers (Reduced) and (Node) to every minimal realisation, but not
(J): its pooled side value is indexed by an internal node and depends on how
the common leaf partition is assembled into an ordered rooted tree, which
equality of leaf partitions, closures, values, and adjacency graphs does not
determine (Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-nonunique} makes this concrete).
Consequently, whenever the manuscript states a node-indexed assumption or
result ``for the true tree,'' it is to be read as a statement about the
canonical realisation of Definition~\ref{def:canonical-realisation} below ---
a well-posing convention, not a claim that the assumption holds. Statements
depending only on the boundary-face set or numerical threshold locations
need no such convention, since Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}(3) already
shows those are invariant across minimal realisations.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Transfer across minimal elements]\label{cor:minimal-transfer}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}, let
\(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) be minimal. The bijection between
\(\cP(\tau)\) and \(\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition},
conclusions (2)--(3), preserves leaf closures and their face-adjacency
graph, leaf population values, and leaf masses. Hence the leaf-level
versions of (Reduced) and (Node) hold for every minimal realisation
whenever they hold for the witness \(\tau^{\dagger}\); in particular,
Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}'s hypothesis (1) is satisfied by every
minimal realisation, not only by \(\tau^{\dagger}\). The proof is given in
\S\ref{sec:supp-setup}.
\end{corollary}

Call a rooted axis-aligned tree \emph{non-degenerate} if every leaf cell is
nonempty and full-dimensional and no internal node has an empty child
(equivalently, no split is redundant); this is a structural condition,
distinct from clause (Reduced) of Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity},
which constrains leaf values. Let \(\cP^\star\) denote the minimal
partition of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}, represented by its leaf
closures, and let \(\mathfrak T(\cP^\star)\) be the set of non-degenerate
rooted axis-aligned trees in \(\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) whose leaf partition is
\(\nu\)-equivalent to \(\cP^\star\); this set contains \(\tau^{\dagger}\)
and so is nonempty. Fix
the lexicographic order \((q,c)<_{\mathrm{lex}}(q',c')\) iff \(q<q'\), or
\(q=q'\) and \(c<c'\); breadth-first traversal with left child visited
before right; and the usual deterministic boundary convention for split
tests.

\begin{definition}[Canonical realisation]\label{def:canonical-realisation}
Starting at the root, process unresolved nodes in breadth-first order. At a
node carrying \(\cA\subseteq\cP^\star\): if \(\card\cA=1\), declare the node
a leaf; otherwise, among all split tests \((q,c)\) that (i) do not cut the
interior of any cell in \(\cA\), (ii) place at least one cell of \(\cA\) on
each side, and (iii) admit at least one completion of the current partial
tree to an element of \(\mathfrak T(\cP^\star)\) respecting the remaining
depth budget, choose the lexicographically least \((q,c)\). The resulting
rooted tree is \(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness of the canonical rule]\label{prop:canonical-welldefined}
The canonical construction terminates and produces a unique
\(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}}\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) with leaf partition
\(\nu\)-equivalent to \(\cP^\star\), at most \(\bar L\) leaves, and depth at
most \(\bar D\). The proof is given in \S\ref{sec:supp-setup}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Ordered realisations need not be unique]\label{prop:ordered-nonunique}
Partition \([0,1]^2\) at \(x_1=1/2\) and \(x_2=1/2\), and assign pairwise
distinct quadrant values \(a,b,c,d\) (bottom-left, top-left, bottom-right,
top-right) with \(a+b\ne c+d\) and \(a+c\ne b+d\). Under uniform \(\nu\),
the minimal four-leaf partition admits both an \(x_1\)-first and an
\(x_2\)-first realisation --- different ordered rooted trees inducing the
same leaf partition --- and its two possible root jumps under (J),
\(\tfrac12\abs{a+b-c-d}\) and \(\tfrac12\abs{a+c-b-d}\), are not merely both
positive but provably unequal (equality would force \(b=c\) or \(a=d\),
excluded by pairwise distinctness). Concretely, \(a,b,c,d=1,2,4,8\) gives
root jumps \(9/2\) and \(5/2\). Any bound stated in terms of ``the root jump
of the true tree'' is therefore numerically ambiguous absent
Remark~\ref{rem:uniqueness-scope}'s canonical-realisation convention. The
canonical rule of Definition~\ref{def:canonical-realisation} selects
between the two here: at the root, \((1,1/2)<_{\mathrm{lex}}(2,1/2)\), so
\(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}}\) is the \(x_1\)-first tree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every quadrant has mass \(1/4\) and every internal node mass at least
\(1/2\), so (Node) holds with \(q_{\min}=1/4\); every face-adjacent pair
differs since \(a,b,c,d\) are pairwise distinct, so (Local-Jump) holds; and
since \(\gamma_0\) is pairwise distinct on the four quadrants, no leaf can
contain the interior of two of them, so every realisation needs at least
four leaves and both displayed constructions attain four. The two
realisations have different roots and different node-descendant groupings
but induce the same four-quadrant partition, demonstrating that partition
uniqueness does not extend to the ordered tree.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Solver fidelity for the two stages}\label{sec:setup-solver}

\begin{assumption}[Two-stage solver fidelity]\label{ass:solver-twostage}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage} is executed as stated, and the solvers
implementing its Steps 2 and 4 satisfy the following, none of which an
implementation gives for free.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Stage-A exactness.} Step~2's minimum is attained globally, not
  approximated: a heuristic or early-terminated search is outside the scope of
  the results below. Every Stage-A split lies at a cutpoint of $\cA_r$, so
  Assumption~\ref{ass:solver} remains in force for Stage~A itself.
  \item \emph{Stage-B exactness and scope.} Each Step-4 scan likewise attains
  its minimum globally over the observed values in $I_n(N)$. Stage~B
  is therefore a sequence of single-threshold exact scans, not a joint
  minimisation over the Cartesian product of all thresholds at once. Only
  Stage~B may evaluate continuous or observed-value thresholds.
  \item \emph{Full-sample construction.} Stages A and B both use all $n$
  observations. There is no sample splitting or cross-fitting between them.
  \item \emph{Topology preservation.} Step~4 moves thresholds and nothing
  else; an implementation must add, delete, reorder, and re-coordinate no
  split, for instance by pruning a node whose refined scan empties a child.
  \item \emph{Measurability.} Both stages use deterministic, measurable
  tie-breaking rules. Consequently $\hat\tau^A_n$ is a well-defined random
  tree and the refined threshold vector $\hat c$ is a
  well-defined random vector. The rate conclusions below hold for
  \emph{any} such selection --- Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} is proved
  for an arbitrary measurable choice from the Stage-B argmin set, with no
  uniqueness assumption --- so this clause is required for well-definedness
  only, not for the proofs.
  \item \emph{Refined leaf mass.} There exists a constant $\rho>0$, independent
  of $n$, such that, with probability tending to one, every leaf of the refined
  partition contains at least $\rho n$ observations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Two properties that are not assumed here]\label{rem:not-assumed}
Two properties that a solver-fidelity condition might be expected to carry are
in fact consequences of what is already assumed, and are recorded here so that
no later argument appears to take either as a hypothesis.

\emph{Search-interval validity.} That the search interval $I_n(N)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval} contains $N$'s true threshold --- and no
other true threshold on $N$'s coordinate --- with probability tending to one
is Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}, proved there from
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}. Nothing in \S\ref{sec:topology} uses
it, so no circularity arises in obtaining it; its first use is in
\S\ref{sec:stageb}. Stage~A supplies only the topology and the grid
thresholds $\hat t^A_N$ from which the intervals $I_n(\cdot)$ are formed;
every threshold \emph{value} in the final estimator is
supplied by the off-grid Stage-B scan of Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage}, Step~4.

\emph{Leaf mass at the anchor.} Clause~(f) above asserts the $\rho n$
leaf-mass floor at the \emph{refined} partition $\hat c$, and only there. The
corresponding floor at the \emph{true} partition $c_0$ needs no assumption.
Every leaf of the true partition has $\nu$-mass at least $q_{\min}$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Node), hence $P_X$-mass at least
$q_{\min}/\bar W$, since $d\nu\le\bar W\,dP_X$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:design}; there are at most $\bar L$ such leaves and they
are fixed in $n$, by Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S). Applying the
law of large numbers to each of those finitely many fixed leaves under
Assumption~\ref{ass:iid} therefore gives, with probability tending to one,
\[
  \#\{i\le n:X_i\in\ell\}\ \ge\ \rho_0n
  \quad\text{for every leaf }\ell\text{ of }c_0,
  \qquad
  \rho_0:=\frac{q_{\min}}{2\bar W}>0 ,
\]
the factor $\tfrac12$ absorbing the estimation error of the law of large
numbers. Finite-dimensional leaf-refit control is thus available at the anchor
$c_0$ as well as throughout the topology-preserving perturbation from $c_0$ to
$\hat c$; where an argument below needs the floor at both ends, it may take
$\rho\wedge\rho_0$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Population-level identities and margins}
\label{sec:topology-population}

Neither result in this subsection is stochastic: both are statements about the
population risk $R$ and the projection $\Pi^\nu$ alone, and neither proof uses
a concentration inequality or a union bound.

\begin{lemma}[The grid-optimal class is the projected-sufficient class]
\label{lem:grid-optimal}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:design} and~\ref{ass:finite} at $\cA_r$,
$\cS^{\cA_r}=\argmin_{\tau\in\cT_{\cA_r}}R(\tau)$, and
$\tau^{\mathrm{sat}}_r\in\cS^{\cA_r}$, so $\cS^{\cA_r}\ne\emptyset$
unconditionally, where $\tau^{\mathrm{sat}}_r$ is the partition of $\cA_r$
into its individual atoms,
$\gbar_r:=\Pi^\nu_{\tau^{\mathrm{sat}}_r}\gamma_0$
and
$\cS^{\cA_r}:=\bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\cA_r}:\Pi^\nu_\tau\gbar_r=\gbar_r\
P\text{-a.s.}\bigr\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The population ANOVA identity gives, for every $\tau\in\cT_{\cA_r}$,
$R(\tau)-R(\tau^{\mathrm{sat}}_r)
 =\sum_{\ell\in\tau}\sum_{a\subseteq\ell}\omega_a(\gbar_a-\gbar_\ell)^2\ge0$,
with $\omega_a$ the $\nu$-mass of atom $a$ and $\gbar_\ell$ the
$\omega$-weighted mean of $\gbar_a$ over $a\subseteq\ell$. Every atom has
$\omega_a>0$ by Assumptions~\ref{ass:finite} and~\ref{ass:design}, so the sum
vanishes exactly when $\gbar_r$ is leafwise constant. The membership of
$\tau^{\mathrm{sat}}_r$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The argument uses only the population ANOVA identity, positivity of every
atom mass, and finiteness of the grid.  No stochastic rate or tail bound is
used.  It can fail if a purported grid atom has zero \(\nu\)-mass.

\paragraph{Constants for the refining-grid argument.}
Fix \(r_0\), depending only on \(\varsigma\), the quasi-uniformity constant
\(\bar Q\), the leaf budget \(\bar L\),
and the constant \(C\) selected below, such that, for every \(r\ge r_0\),
the mesh in every coordinate is smaller than \(\varsigma/2\),
\(C/r<\varsigma/2\), and the grid carries at least as many cutpoints as the
comparator constructions below place on it,
\[
  N_r\ \ge\ \bar L
  \qquad\text{(in particular }N_{r_0}\ge\bar L\text{)}.
\]
Hence no grid atom contains more than one true boundary, no two true
thresholds on one coordinate snap to the same cutpoint or cross in order, and
a comparator with up to \(\bar L-1\) grid splits is available at every such
resolution. The depth bound \(\bar D\) requires no separate condition on
\(r_0\): a tree respecting \(\bar L\) already has depth at most
\(\bar L-1\le N_r-1\) (\S\ref{sec:setup-algorithm}), so the capacity just
imposed accommodates it.
This convention remains in force throughout this section.

A tree \(\tau\) is said to \emph{omit} a true boundary
\(b=(q,c_{0,N})\) if no split of \(\tau\) on coordinate \(q\) lies within
distance \(C/r\) of \(c_{0,N}\).  Let \(D_{\max}\) be the coordinate-diameter
bound of Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens) and
set
\[
  c_{\mathrm{box}}:=\frac1{f_{\max}D_{\max}}>0,
\]
so that, for every true internal node \(N\) with splitting coordinate \(q\),
the \((p-1)\)-dimensional cross-section \(\Gamma_N\) of \(R_N\) transverse to
\(q\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}
q_{\min}\).  Indeed Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Node) gives
\(\nu(R_N)\ge q_{\min}\) while (Dens) gives
\(\nu(R_N)\le f_{\max}\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)D_{\max}\).  Set
\[
  c_{\mathrm{topol}}:=\frac{c_{\mathrm{box}}}{8}.
\]
Choose the fixed constant \(C\) sufficiently large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-margin-choice}
  c_{\mathrm{topol}}\kappa_{\min}^2 f_{\min}q_{\min}C
  >
  4(\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[A refining-grid margin for omitted true boundaries]
\label{lem:topol-margin}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:design}, \ref{ass:budget},
\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}, \ref{ass:jump-regularity},
and~\ref{ass:local-design}, there exist \(r_0<\infty\) and
\(c_\Delta>0\), independent of \(r\) and \(n\), such that, for every
\(r\ge r_0\) and every
\(\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)\) that omits at least one true boundary,
\[
  S_r(\tau)
  :=
  R(\tau)
  -
  \min_{\sigma\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)}R(\sigma)
  \ge \frac{c_\Delta}{r}.
\]
One may take
\[
  c_\Delta
  :=
  \tfrac12
  c_{\mathrm{topol}}\kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}C
  -
  (\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}
  >0,
\]
the factor \(\tfrac12\) reflecting the discard adjustment fixed once and for
all when \(r_0\) is enlarged in the proof below (the constant \(C\) in
\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice} is chosen large enough that this halved quantity
is still positive).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Both halves of the bound are population-level; neither a concentration
inequality nor a union bound is used. For the lower bound, fix the omitted
boundary and consider a pair of points differing only in the splitting
coordinate and straddling it at distance $t\le C/2r$: no split on another
coordinate can separate them, and, precisely because the boundary is omitted,
no $q$-split of $\tau$ lies between them, so they share a leaf. Clause
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(Local-Jump) then forces a squared error of
at least $\kappa_{\min}^2/4$ at one
of the two points, and integrating over $t$ and over the active face cells ---
using the density floor of (Dens) together with the cross-sectional bound
$\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}q_{\min}$ that (Node)
and (Dens) supply --- gives an approximation loss of order $C/r$. For the
upper bound, snapping every true boundary to a nearest cutpoint while
preserving the true ancestor order produces a feasible comparator in
$\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)$ --- the object
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle} names $\tau_n^\circ$ --- that disagrees with
$\gamma_0$ only in slabs of width
$O(r^{-1})$, costing at most $(\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}/r$ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(J) and
the upper density bound. Subtracting the second bound from the first leaves
$c_\Delta/r$, and the choice~\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice} of $C$ is exactly what
makes $c_\Delta>0$ after the $O(r^{-1})$ discard loss at the edge of the face
patch is absorbed. The full proof is given in the Supplementary Material,
\S\ref{sec:supp-topology}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The margin is a statement about threshold precision, not only
about literal omission]\label{rem:omission-is-localisation}
The proof just sketched uses exactly one property of \(\tau\): that no
\(q\)-split of \(\tau\) lies strictly between \(c_{0,N}-C/2r\) and
\(c_{0,N}+C/2r\). It never distinguishes the case in which \(\tau\) carries no
split on coordinate \(q\) at all from the case in which it carries one that is
simply farther than \(C/2r\) from \(c_{0,N}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}
therefore does double duty. Read as a statement about candidates lacking a
\(q\)-split, it excludes literal omission --- though
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} below excludes that at a strictly larger, fixed
margin, so the local version is not what does the work there. Read as a
statement about candidates that \emph{do} carry the right splits on the right
coordinates but place one badly, it says such a candidate pays \(c_\Delta/r\),
which is exactly the claim Step~3 of Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}'s proof
needs and which Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} does \emph{not} supply: a global
topology margin says nothing about threshold precision \emph{within} a correct
template. The two margins are complementary in this specific way, and the
distinction is worth keeping in view, since only one of them vanishes with
\(r\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Population scope of the refining-grid margin]
The empirical comparison that Stage~A additionally requires is separate
from the population margins established here, and is carried out in
\S\ref{sec:topology-empirical}, Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The lower bound uses the density lower bound precisely to ensure that paired
points on both sides of a true face exist with uniformly positive local
mass.  The upper bound uses the density upper bound and the fixed number of
true boundaries.  Separation ensures that the paired patch contains no
second true boundary.  The conclusion can fail if the local support has a
gap, if a jump vanishes on an active face cell, or if node cross-sections
degenerate with \(n\). The restatement of the lemma at the \emph{true} budget
\(\bar L\), rather than at an inflated one, changes neither half of the
argument: the lower bound never refers to the class \(\tau\) is drawn from,
and the comparator achieving the upper bound uses at most \(\bar L\) leaves
in any case, so the same tree attains the minimum in both classes and the
subtraction remains valid with the same constant \(c_\Delta\).

\subsection{The global topology margin}\label{sec:topology-global}

Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s margin is \(\Theta(1/r)\) and therefore
vanishes as the grid refines. That is the right order for a statement about
\emph{threshold precision}, where the competing approximation error is itself
\(\Theta(1/r)\); it is far too weak for a statement about \emph{which
coordinates get split}. A tree splitting on entirely the wrong coordinates
must be excluded at a cost that does not shrink, or no penalty regime can
separate it from the truth uniformly in \(r\). The next two results supply
that. Both are population statements; neither is stochastic.

The first difficulty is definitional. On a refining grid, no candidate can
reproduce the true partition \emph{exactly} --- a true threshold interior to
an atom is unreachable at any finite \(r\) --- so ``recovers the true
partition'' cannot be the object a margin is stated against. What is both
achievable and sufficient is that the candidate have the right \emph{shape}:
the right splitting coordinates in the right ancestor relationships, with
thresholds free to be imprecise. The following definition isolates that.

\begin{definition}[Templates and structural matching]
\label{def:structural-matching}
A tree \emph{template} \(T\) is a rooted binary tree shape together with a
splitting coordinate attached to each internal node, and no threshold values;
thus the topology of \S\ref{sec:setup-basics} is exactly a template. For a
template \(T\) and an admissible threshold assignment \(\theta\) --- one
threshold per internal node, each ranging over the compact coordinate extent
of \(\cX\) --- let \(\mathcal P(T,\theta)\) denote the resulting partition of
\(\cX\) into its \emph{positive-volume} leaf rectangles. A partition is a
\emph{set} of rectangles: degenerate, zero-volume leaves are simply absent
from it, which is what makes comparison ``up to leaf relabelling'' automatic,
no ordering or labelling of leaves being part of the data compared.

Call \(\theta\) \emph{non-degenerate} for \(T\) if every internal node's
threshold splits that node's cell into two positive-volume children. Two
templates \(T,T'\) are \emph{structurally equivalent} if there exist
non-degenerate admissible \(\theta,\theta'\) with
\[
  \mathcal P(T,\theta)=\mathcal P(T',\theta')
\]
exactly, modulo Lebesgue-null sets --- that is, if \emph{some} parameter
choice on each side realises the identical partition. A candidate tree
\(\tau\), with template \(T_\tau\) and thresholds \(\theta_\tau\),
\emph{structurally matches} the true tree \(\tau_0\), with template \(T_0\),
if \(T_\tau\) and \(T_0\) are structurally equivalent in this sense.

Three features of this definition are load-bearing and are recorded here
rather than rediscovered downstream.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Non-degeneracy is necessary, not cosmetic.} Without it, any two
  templates whatsoever could choose fully degenerate thresholds, both realise
  the trivial one-leaf partition \(\{\cX\}\), and so satisfy the existential
  clause --- collapsing the relation to the trivial one in which everything is
  equivalent to everything. With it, \(\mathcal P(T,\theta)\) has exactly as
  many leaves as \(T\) has leaf-nodes, so a witnessing match forces the two
  templates to agree on leaf count and to realise the partition honestly.
  \item \emph{The quantifier is existential, and matching is a property of
  \(\tau\)'s template alone.} \(\tau\)'s own thresholds \(\theta_\tau\) play
  no part in whether it matches; what matters is whether \emph{some} other
  assignment on \(T_\tau\) could reproduce \(\tau_0\)'s partition. (If
  \(\tau\)'s own realised partition happens to equal \(\tau_0\)'s, that
  specific witness certifies matching --- a special case of the definition,
  not a second notion.) The existential form is what
  Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}'s compactness argument requires: it gives a finite,
  symmetric relation on templates that does not depend on which true
  threshold configuration is later realised.
  \item \emph{No separate notion of commuting splits is needed.} Two templates
  differing only in the order of splits that do not affect the achievable
  partition are already equivalent under the clause above, since matching
  thresholds across the reordered branches produces identical partitions. This
  is also why the definition is stated at the level of \emph{partitions} and
  not of rooted-tree isomorphism, matching the convention
  Definition~\ref{def:true-tree} already adopts for the truth: a specific
  split order is not identified by the data and is not claimed to be
  recovered.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Global topology margin]\label{thm:gtm-b}
Let \(\tau_0\) be the true tree of Definition~\ref{def:true-tree}, with
\(s_0\) internal splits and \(s_0+1\) positive-volume leaves. Under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:construct}(a), \ref{ass:continuum-sparsity},
\ref{ass:jump-regularity} --- including (Reduced) --- \ref{ass:local-design},
and~\ref{ass:global-density}, there exists a constant
\[
  c_{\mathrm{top}}
  =
  c_{\mathrm{top}}\bigl(\cX,f_{\min},f_{\max},\kappa_{\min},\kappa_{\max},
  \varsigma,q_{\min},\bar L,\bar D,p\bigr)>0,
\]
fixed in \(n\) and in the grid resolution \(r\), such that every axis-aligned
tree \(\tau\) with at most \(s_0\) splits that does \emph{not} structurally
match \(\tau_0\) (Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}) satisfies
\[
  R(\tau)-R^\star\ \ge\ c_{\mathrm{top}},
  \qquad
  R^\star:=R(\gamma_0).
\]
For the special case of a literal coarsening --- one candidate leaf containing
two adjacent true leaves --- the sharper explicit bound
\(R(\tau)-R^\star\ge q_{\min}\kappa_{\min}^2/2\) is available.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Squared-error loss and the conditional-mean-zero property give
\[
  R(\tau)-R^\star\ \ge\ f_{\min}D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau),
\]
where
\(D_\lambda\) is the best leafwise-constant \(L_2\) approximation error to
\(\gamma_0\) computed against \emph{Lebesgue} measure, not \(\nu\). This is
the step at which the global density bound of
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density} is used, and it is what makes the rest of
the argument purely geometric: \(D_\lambda\) does not depend on the shape of
the density beyond the floor \(f_{\min}\).

The heart of the argument is a refinement lemma: if a finite rectangular
partition's best leafwise-constant approximation to \(\gamma_0\) achieves
\emph{exactly} zero Lebesgue error, then every positive-volume candidate leaf
lies inside one true leaf. Its proof takes a candidate rectangle meeting the
interiors of two distinct true leaves in positive volume, joins density points
of the two intersections by a segment --- which stays inside the convex
rectangle --- perturbs it to avoid codimension-two intersections of true
boundaries, and finds a crossing through a relatively open codimension-one
face, where (Reduced) forces the adjacent constants to differ by at least
\(\kappa_{\min}\) while both sides retain positive volume inside the
rectangle; no function constant on that rectangle can match both. Counting
leaves then upgrades this: a candidate with at most \(s_0\) splits has at most
\(s_0+1\) positive-volume leaves, a refinement of an \((s_0+1)\)-cell
partition needs at least \(s_0+1\) cells, so equality holds throughout and the
two partitions agree modulo null sets --- i.e.\ \(\tau\) structurally matches
\(\tau_0\). Contrapositively, structural mismatch forces
\(D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau)>0\) pointwise.

Uniform positivity is then a compactness argument, and its two delicate
points are worth naming. There are finitely many templates (finitely many
shapes with at most \(\bar L\) leaves, finitely many coordinate assignments at
fixed \(p\)), and for each the threshold-parameter space is compact --- the
compactification deliberately \emph{including} support-boundary thresholds,
coincident thresholds, and zero-volume leaves, which are exactly the
problematic limits. The geometric-error functional is continuous on that
compactified space, including at those degenerate points, because a
vanishing-volume leaf's contribution vanishes with it. Minimising over the
finite set of structurally-mismatched template pairs therefore attains its
minimum; were the minimum zero, the refinement lemma would force the
minimising candidate to match, a contradiction. Setting
\(c_{\mathrm{top}}:=f_{\min}\Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}\) with
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}\) that attained minimum finishes the proof. A tree
with strictly fewer than \(s_0\) splits cannot refine an \((s_0+1)\)-leaf
partition and so falls under the same argument. The full proof is given in the
Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-topology}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The global density bound is used exactly once, to pass from \(\nu\)-weighted
risk to Lebesgue-geometric discrepancy; the node-local (Dens-i) does not
suffice there, because a candidate splitting on the wrong coordinate need not
place any leaf near a true face (Remark~\ref{rem:global-density-role}).
Clause (Reduced) is used exactly once, in the refinement lemma, and is not
implied by minimality (Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-independent}). The compactness
step needs continuity of the error functional \emph{at} the degenerate
boundary of the parameter space, not merely on its interior; this is verified
rather than assumed, and is where an argument of this shape would otherwise
silently fail. The constant \(c_{\mathrm{top}}\) is not explicit: it is
obtained from an attained minimum over a finite set of template pairs, so the
result is qualitative in that respect, and nothing below requires a numerical
value for it. The conclusion can fail if the density vanishes on part of
\(\cX\), if two geometrically adjacent true leaves share a value, or if
\(\bar L\), \(\bar D\), or \(p\) grows with \(n\).

\subsection{Empirical topology recovery}\label{sec:topology-empirical}

The remaining results are stochastic: each concerns the penalised empirical
minimiser \(\hat\tau^A_n\) and holds with probability tending to one. Three
ingredients are assembled first --- a comparator, a uniform concentration
bound, and the lemma linking Stage~A's empirical leaf-count floor to the
population leaf-mass floor that bound needs --- and the main result follows by
comparing the minimiser with the comparator on three disjoint classes of
candidate.

\paragraph{Rate conditions.}
Every result in this subsection is stated along the joint sequence
\((n,r_n,\lambda_n,m_n)\) subject to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stage-a-rates}
\begin{aligned}
  &\text{(R1)}&&r_n\to\infty,
    \qquad r_n=o\bigl(\sqrt{n/\log r_n}\bigr);\\
  &\text{(R2)}&&\lambda_n\to0;\\
  &\text{(R3)}&&\max\Bigl(\frac1{r_n},\ \sqrt{\frac{\log r_n}{n}}\Bigr)
    \ \ll\ \lambda_n\ \ll\ 1;\\
  &\text{(R4)}&&m_n\to\infty,
    \qquad \frac{m_nr_n}{n}\to0,
    \qquad m_n\lambda_n\to\infty;\\
  &\text{(R5)}&&\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}\to\infty.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Clause (R2) is Assumption~\ref{ass:global}'s second clause, restated here so
that~\eqref{eq:stage-a-rates} is self-contained. Clause (R3) is the substantive
one and deserves emphasis, because its direction is the opposite of what a
grid-exact analysis would require: the penalty must \emph{dominate} the grid
approximation error, \(1/(r_n\lambda_n)\to0\), rather than be dominated by it.
That is what makes a candidate carrying one split more than the truth strictly
worse than the comparator, even though such a candidate can genuinely reduce
the population risk --- by \(\Theta(1/r_n)\), the cost of representing an
interior boundary at a cutpoint. Clauses (R4) and (R5) are the two standing
conditions on \(m_n\) recorded in \S\ref{sec:setup-twostage}, (R4)'s middle
condition being \(m_n=o(n/r_n)\).

The five clauses are simultaneously satisfiable, and a polynomial family
exhibits this: \(r_n=n^a\), \(\lambda_n=n^{-b}\), \(m_n=n^c\) with
\[
  0<b<a<\tfrac12,
  \qquad
  \max\bigl(b,\tfrac12\bigr)<c<1-a .
\]
The interval for \(c\) is non-empty precisely because \(a<\tfrac12\), and
\(c>\tfrac12\) is exactly what (R5) adds on top of (R1)--(R4). A concrete
choice is \(m_n=\lfloor n^{3/4}/\sqrt{r_n}\rfloor\), which satisfies
\(m_n/\sqrt n\to\infty\) and \(m_nr_n/n\to0\) simultaneously, with
\(m_n\lambda_n\to\infty\) following from \(\lambda_nr_n\to\infty\) and
\(m_n/r_n\to\infty\).

\paragraph{The comparator.}
Every exclusion below is a comparison against one fixed tree, constructed by
snapping the truth onto the grid. This is the same construction that supplies
the upper half of Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s margin; it is given a name
and its properties collected because the empirical argument uses all of them.

\begin{lemma}[The snapped-oracle comparator]\label{lem:snapped-oracle}
Let \(\tau_0\) be the true tree, with \(s_0\) splits, and let \(r_n\ge r_0\)
so that the grid mesh is smaller than \(\varsigma/2\) and no two true
thresholds on one coordinate snap to a common cutpoint or cross in order.
Define \(\tau_n^\circ\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})\) by snapping every true
boundary to its nearest grid cutpoint, preserving the true ancestor order.
Then, under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:design},
\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}, \ref{ass:jump-regularity},
and~\ref{ass:local-design}, and with (R4) in force:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \(\tau_n^\circ\) has exactly \(s_0\) splits and structurally matches
  \(\tau_0\) (Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}), by construction.
  \item \(R(\tau_n^\circ)-R^\star\le C_{\mathrm{app}}/r_n\) with
  \(C_{\mathrm{app}}:=(\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}\).
  \item Every leaf of \(\tau_n^\circ\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least
  \(q_{\min}/2\), for \(r_n\) large enough.
  \item \(\tau_n^\circ\) is Stage-A feasible --- \(n_\ell(\tau_n^\circ)\ge
  m_n\) for every leaf \(\ell\) --- with probability tending to one.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part (a) holds by construction: snapping moves thresholds and neither adds nor
deletes a split, and the resulting tree has the same template as \(\tau_0\),
with the choice of \(r_n\ge r_0\) guaranteeing that no two snapped thresholds
coincide or reverse order, so the realised partition has the full
\(s_0+1\) positive-volume leaves and the template match is witnessed
non-degenerately. Part (b) is exactly the upper-bound half of
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s proof, stated directly rather than as one side
of a subtraction: each snapped boundary disagrees with \(\gamma_0\) only in a
slab of width \(O(r_n^{-1})\), which by
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(J) and the upper density bound of (Dens)
costs at most \(\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}/r_n\), and there are at most
\(\bar L-1\) boundaries.

For (c), snapping perturbs each true boundary by at most half the mesh,
\(O(1/r_n)\). Each leaf of \(\tau_n^\circ\) is therefore a rectangle differing
from the corresponding true leaf only in slabs of total width \(O(1/r_n)\), so
by the upper density bound its \(\nu\)-mass differs from that leaf's by
\(O(f_{\max}/r_n)=o(1)\). Since every true leaf has mass at least
\(q_{\min}>0\) by Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Node), a fixed constant,
taking \(r_n\) large enough that the perturbation is below \(q_{\min}/2\)
gives the claim.

For (d), note first that \(\tau_n^\circ\) is a \emph{fixed} tree once \(n\) is
fixed --- it depends on the truth and the grid, not on the data --- which is
what makes the argument elementary. Fix a leaf \(\ell\) of it, with
\(P_X\)-mass \(q_\ell\ge q_{\min}/(2\bar W)>0\) by (c) and
Assumption~\ref{ass:design}. Then \(n_\ell\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n,q_\ell)\),
and since \(m_n/n\to0\) --- which (R4) supplies, \(m_nr_n/n\to0\) and
\(r_n\to\infty\) giving \(m_n/n\to0\) jointly --- the target \(m_n\) is
eventually below \(nq_\ell/2\), so the multiplicative Chernoff bound gives
\(\PP(n_\ell<m_n)\le\exp\{-nq_\ell/8\}\to0\). A union bound over the at most
\(\bar L\) leaves, a number fixed in \(n\), preserves this. No condition
beyond (R4) is needed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
Parts (a)--(c) are population statements; only (d) is stochastic, and it is
the easiest kind of stochastic statement --- a Chernoff bound for a
\emph{single fixed} tree whose leaf masses are bounded below by a constant not
shrinking with \(n\). This is worth contrasting with
Lemma~\ref{lem:empirical-feasible-mass} below, which must control a
\emph{shrinking} mass floor across an entire \emph{random} class and
consequently costs an extra rate condition. The comparator's own feasibility
costs nothing extra. Part (c) can fail if \(q_{\min}\) is not fixed in \(n\);
part (b) if the number of true boundaries grows.

\paragraph{Uniform concentration.}
The comparison of \(\hat\tau^A_n\) with \(\tau_n^\circ\) requires the
\emph{difference} of empirical risks to track the difference of population
risks, uniformly over the candidate class. Two features of the following lemma
matter. It assumes only a bounded conditional second moment on the response,
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}, and in particular no sub-Gaussian or bounded
response; and it uses the class's fixed VC complexity rather than its
cardinality, a naive union bound over the \(\Theta(r_n^{\,\bar Lp})\)
candidate trees being too lossy to close under (R1).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform empirical-risk concentration]\label{lem:uep-second-moment}
Let \(q_n>0\) be a deterministic sequence and let
\[
  \cT^{\mathrm{pop}}_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n};q_n)
  :=
  \bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n}):
    \nu(\ell)\ge q_n\text{ for every leaf }\ell\text{ of }\tau\bigr\}
\]
be the subclass whose every leaf carries population mass at least \(q_n\).
Let \(\tau_n^\circ\) be a non-random member of that class. Under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:design}, \ref{ass:moments},
and~\ref{ass:budget}, there is a constant \(C<\infty\), depending only on
\(C_Z\), \(p\), \(\bar L\), and the class's VC characteristics, such that for
every \(u>0\)
\[
  \PP\bigl(\Delta_n>u\bigr)
  \ \le\
  C\left\{\frac1{nq_n^2}+\frac1{nu^2}+\frac1{nuq_n}\right\},
\]
where
\[
  \Delta_n
  :=
  \sup_{\tau\in\cT^{\mathrm{pop}}_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n};q_n)}
  \Bigl|
    \bigl\{R_n(\tau)-R_n(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}
    -\bigl\{R(\tau)-R(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}
  \Bigr| .
\]
In particular, if \(q_n\ge c_q/r_n\) and \(r_n=o(\sqrt n)\), then
\(\PP(\Delta_n>\eta/r_n)\le C_\eta r_n^2/n\to0\) for each fixed \(\eta>0\),
and \(\Delta_n=O_p\bigl(\sqrt{\log r_n/n}\bigr)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Write \(R_n(\tau)=P_nZ^2-\hat G(\tau)\) and \(R(\tau)=PZ^2-G(\tau)\), where
for a leaf \(A\) one puts \(q_A=P\mathbf 1_A\), \(s_A=P(Z\mathbf 1_A)\),
\(\mu_A=s_A/q_A\), and \(G(\tau)=\sum_As_A^2/q_A\), with \(\hat G\) the
empirical analogue. The first move is the one that removes any need for a tail
condition on \(Z\): the heavy-tailed term \((P_n-P)Z^2\) is common to
\(R_n(\tau)\) and \(R_n(\tau_n^\circ)\) and \emph{cancels exactly} in the
difference, leaving \(\Delta_n\le2\sup_\tau\abs{\hat G(\tau)-G(\tau)}\).

An exact algebraic identity then splits each leaf's contribution into a linear
part, which sums over a tree's leaves to \((P_n-P)h_\tau\) for a function
\(h_\tau\) with square-integrable envelope, and a quadratic remainder carrying
\(1/\hat q_A\). Because \(p\) and \(\bar L\) are fixed, all three relevant
function classes --- leaf indicators, the linear functionals \(h_\tau\), and
the centred products \((z-b)\mathbf 1_A\) --- are VC-type with fixed entropy,
so symmetrisation and the entropy integral give second moments of order
\(1/n\) for each of their suprema. On the event that the leaf-indicator
supremum is below \(q_n/2\), every empirical leaf mass is at least \(q_n/2\),
which converts the quadratic remainder into a bound of order
\(W_n^2/q_n\). Markov's inequality applied to each of the three suprema gives
the display. Substituting \(u=\eta/r_n\) and \(q_n\ge c_q/r_n\) gives the
consequences. The full proof is given in the Supplementary Material,
\S\ref{sec:supp-topology}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The cancellation of \((P_n-P)Z^2\) is essential and is what keeps
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} --- a bounded conditional \emph{second} moment,
nothing more --- sufficient; a bound on \(\sup_\tau\abs{R_n(\tau)-R(\tau)}$
itself, rather than on the difference against a fixed comparator, would not
enjoy it. The VC route is also not a matter of elegance: a union bound over
the \(\Theta(r_n^{\,d})\) trees in the class, \(d=O(\bar Lp)\), would require
\(r_n^{\,d+2}/n\to0\), strictly stronger than (R1). The population mass floor
\(q_n\) enters only through the quadratic remainder, and the lemma is stated
for a \emph{deterministic} class satisfying it, which is why
Lemma~\ref{lem:empirical-feasible-mass} is needed to apply it to the class
Stage~A actually searches. The bound degrades if \(\bar L\) or \(p\) grows
with \(n\), or if \(q_n\) decays faster than \(r_n^{-1}\).

\paragraph{From the empirical floor to a population floor.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment} needs every leaf of every candidate to carry
population mass at least \(q_n\). Algorithm~\ref{alg:twostage} imposes no such
condition: it imposes an \emph{empirical} floor, \(n_\ell\ge m_n\), on the
trees it searches. The gap is real and cannot be closed by fiat in either
direction.

Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment} to the unrestricted class
\(\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})\) is not available: \(q_n\) would have to bound
below the mass of every leaf of every tree in it, including a single-atom
leaf, whose mass is \(\Theta(r_n^{-p})\) rather than \(\Omega(r_n^{-1})\).
Nor does the empirical floor already enforce a \(c_q/r_n\) population floor:
under (R4), a leaf of population mass \(c_q/(2r_n)\) has
\(m_n/(n q_\ell)=2m_nr_n/(c_qn)\to0\), so its empirical count reaches \(m_n\)
with probability tending to one and it passes the test comfortably. Restricting
the algorithm's own search space to a population-mass class would require
replacing (R4) by an incompatible constant-order regime, changing the
procedure's tuning behaviour rather than adding to it.

What does work is to localise the concentration statement to the class the
algorithm actually searches, and to establish for that class the (shrinking)
population floor it does satisfy.

\begin{lemma}[Empirical feasibility implies a population leaf-mass floor]
\label{lem:empirical-feasible-mass}
Let
\[
  \cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n
  :=
  \bigl\{\tau\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n}):
    n_\ell(\tau)\ge m_n\text{ for every leaf }\ell\text{ of }\tau\bigr\}
\]
be the empirically feasible class, and fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)$, writing
\(h(\alpha):=\log(1/\alpha)-1+\alpha>0\). If
\[
  m_nh(\alpha)-(\bar L-1)\log r_n\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
which (R5) implies, then
\[
  \PP\left\{
    \inf_{\tau\in\cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n}\ \min_{\ell\in\tau}\ P_X(\ell)
    \ \ge\ \frac{\alpha m_n}{n}
  \right\}
  \longrightarrow1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Consider a candidate leaf \(\ell\) whose population mass satisfies
\(q_\ell\le\alpha m_n/n\), so that \(nq_\ell\le\alpha m_n<m_n\). The binomial
Chernoff bound in the form
\(\PP(N_\ell\ge m_n)\le\exp\{-m_nh(nq_\ell/m_n)\}\), together with
monotonicity of \(h\) on \((0,1)\), gives
\(\PP(N_\ell\ge m_n)\le\exp\{-m_nh(\alpha)\}\). It remains to count the
distinct leaves that can occur. A tree in \(\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})\) has at
most \(\bar L-1\) internal nodes, each labelled by one of at most \(pr_n\)
splits, and at most \(4^{\bar L-1}\) ordered binary shapes are available, so
the number of distinct leaf occurrences across the whole class is at most
\(C_{\bar L,p}\bar Lr_n^{\bar L-1}\) for a constant depending on \(\bar L\)
and \(p\) alone. A union bound over every such leaf of small mass therefore
gives
\[
  \PP\bigl\{
    \exists\,\tau\in\cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n,\ \exists\,\ell\in\tau:
    P_X(\ell)<\alpha m_n/n
  \bigr\}
  \ \le\
  C_{\bar L,p}\bar L\,r_n^{\bar L-1}e^{-m_nh(\alpha)}
  \longrightarrow0
\]
under the stated condition. That (R5) implies it is immediate:
\(\log r_n=o(\sqrt n)=o(m_n)\), the first equality by (R1) and the second
by (R5).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The floor obtained is \(\alpha m_n/n\), which \emph{shrinks}; the lemma does
not deliver, and Stage~A does not enforce, a floor of constant order. That is
sufficient because Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment}'s tail bound is stated in
terms of \(q_n\) and the two places it is invoked below both close at
\(q_n=\alpha m_n/n\) under (R5). The union bound is over leaf
\emph{occurrences}, not over trees, which is what keeps the exponent linear in
\(\bar L\); a union bound over trees would be far cruder. The argument
requires \(\bar L\) and \(p\) fixed, and fails if \(m_n\) grows more slowly
than \(\log r_n\).

\begin{remark}[How the two concentration lemmas compose]
\label{rem:uep-chain}
The two preceding lemmas are used together, and the composition is worth
setting out once, since the class in question is random and the comparator's
membership of it is not automatic. Write
\[
  \Delta^{\mathrm{emp}}_n
  :=
  \sup_{\tau\in\cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n}
  \Bigl|
    \bigl\{R_n(\tau)-R_n(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}
    -\bigl\{R(\tau)-R(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}
  \Bigr|,
  \qquad
  q_n^\star:=\frac{\alpha m_n}{n},
\]
this being the quantity Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}'s proof controls.
The chain has five links.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \emph{The comparator lies in the deterministic envelope.} By
  Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(c) every leaf of \(\tau_n^\circ\) has mass at
  least \(q_{\min}/2\), a fixed constant, while \(q_n^\star\to0\); so
  eventually \(q_n^\star<q_{\min}/2\) and
  \(\tau_n^\circ\in\cT^{\mathrm{pop}}_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n};q_n^\star)\)
  deterministically, not merely with high probability.
  \item \emph{The comparator is feasible.} \(\tau_n^\circ\in
  \cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n\) with probability tending to one, by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(d).
  \item \emph{The searched class lies in the envelope.} On the event of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:empirical-feasible-mass}, whose probability tends to one
  under (R5),
  \(\cT^{\mathrm{emp}}_n\subseteq
  \cT^{\mathrm{pop}}_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n};q_n^\star)\).
  \item \emph{Concentration on the envelope.} That envelope is deterministic
  and contains \(\tau_n^\circ\) by link (1), so
  Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment} applies to it with \(q_n=q_n^\star\).
  \item \emph{Restriction back to the random class.} Combining links (3)
  and (4) and adding the failure probability of link (3),
  \[
    \PP\bigl(\Delta^{\mathrm{emp}}_n>u_n\bigr)
    \le
    C_{\bar L,p}\bar Lr_n^{\bar L-1}e^{-m_nh(\alpha)}
    +C\left\{
      \frac{n}{\alpha^2m_n^2}+\frac1{nu_n^2}+\frac1{\alpha m_nu_n}
    \right\}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Under (R1)--(R5) this vanishes at both scales at which it is invoked below.
At \(u_n=\lambda_n/2\): \(n/m_n^2\to0\) by (R5),
\(1/\{n(\lambda_n/2)^2\}=4/(n\lambda_n^2)\to0\) by (R3)'s
\(\sqrt{\log r_n/n}\ll\lambda_n\), and \(2/(m_n\lambda_n)\to0\) by (R4). At
\(u_n=1/r_n\): \(r_n^2/n\to0\) by (R1) and \(r_n/m_n\to0\), the latter from
\(r_n/\sqrt n\to0\) (R1) and \(\sqrt n/m_n\to0\) (R5).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The main Stage-A result.}
The theorem below is stated self-containedly: it collects every hypothesis it
uses, including the rate conditions, rather than inheriting them by reference.
That is deliberate, because it is this full hypothesis set --- not merely the
conclusion --- that \S\ref{sec:stageb}'s results invoke.

\begin{theorem}[Stage-A topology recovery]\label{thm:stagea-topology}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:design},
\ref{ass:moments}, \ref{ass:construct}, \ref{ass:budget},
\ref{ass:global}, \ref{ass:solver-twostage}(a),
\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}, \ref{ass:jump-regularity} --- including
(Local-Jump) and (Reduced) --- \ref{ass:local-design},
and~\ref{ass:global-density} hold for Stage~A. Let \(r_n\ge r_0\), let
\(N_{r_n}:=\card{\cA_{r_n}}\asymp r_n^p\), and let
\((r_n,\lambda_n,m_n)\) satisfy the rate
conditions~\eqref{eq:stage-a-rates}. Then every Stage-A penalised global
minimiser
\[
  \hat\tau^A_n
  \in
  \argmin_{\tau\in\cT^A_n}\bigl\{R_n(\tau)+\lambda_ns(\tau)\bigr\}
\]
satisfies, with probability tending to one, all three of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
  \item \(s(\hat\tau^A_n)=s_0\) --- exactly as many splits as the true tree;
  \item \(\hat\tau^A_n\) structurally matches \(\tau_0\)
  (Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}), hence induces a canonical
  matching \(N\mapsto N_0\) of its nodes with the true nodes;
  \item under that matching,
  \[
    \max_N\ \abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N_0}}\ =\ O_p(1/r_n).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let \(\tau_n^\circ\) be the comparator of Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}, so
that \(R(\tau_n^\circ)\le R^\star+C_{\mathrm{app}}/r_n\) and
\(\tau_n^\circ\in\cT^A_n\) with probability tending to one, and write
\(Q_n(\tau):=R_n(\tau)+\lambda_ns(\tau)\). Three disjoint classes of candidate
are excluded in turn, each by a different margin.

\emph{Step 1: too many splits.} Take \(u_n:=\lambda_n/2\) in
Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain}, a fixed fraction of \(\lambda_n\) rather than
\(\lambda_n\) itself --- the latter would leave a margin of exactly zero
below, not a positive one. For any feasible \(\tau\) with
\(s(\tau)\ge s_0+1\), the Bayes-risk floor gives \(R(\tau)\ge R^\star\), hence
\(R(\tau)-R(\tau_n^\circ)\ge-C_{\mathrm{app}}/r_n\), and so on the
concentration event
\[
  Q_n(\tau)-Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)
  \ \ge\
  \lambda_n-\frac{C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n}-\frac{\lambda_n}2
  =
  \frac{\lambda_n}2\left\{1-\frac{2C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n\lambda_n}\right\}
  >0
\]
for \(n\) large, by (R3)'s \(1/(r_n\lambda_n)\to0\). This is the step the
reversed penalty regime exists to make work.

\emph{Step 2: at most \(s_0\) splits, structurally mismatched.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} gives \(R(\tau)-R^\star\ge c_{\mathrm{top}}\) for
such \(\tau\), a constant fixed in \(n\) and \(r_n\), while the largest
penalty advantage available to \(\tau\) is \(s_0\lambda_n\to0\). Hence
\[
  Q_n(\tau)-Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)
  \ \ge\
  c_{\mathrm{top}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n}-s_0\lambda_n
  -\frac{\lambda_n}2
  \longrightarrow c_{\mathrm{top}}>0 .
\]
Any vanishing \(u_n\) suffices here, unlike in Step 1, because the margin
itself does not vanish.

\emph{Step 3: structurally matched, but a badly placed threshold.} Suppose
\(\tau\) matches structurally yet some threshold error exceeds \(C/(2r_n)\).
Then no split of \(\tau\) on that coordinate lies within \(C/(2r_n)\) of the
corresponding true boundary --- which is precisely
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}'s omission condition, read as
Remark~\ref{rem:omission-is-localisation} explains --- so \(\tau\) pays a
population margin \(S_{r_n}(\tau)\ge c_\Delta/r_n\). Invoking
Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain} at the second scale, \(u_n=1/r_n\), and comparing
with the comparator as in Steps 1--2 excludes such \(\tau\) once
\(c_\Delta\) exceeds the constants absorbed on the other side, which is
arranged by \eqref{eq:C-margin-choice}'s choice of \(C\). Since \(C\) is
fixed, this is the assertion \(\max_N\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N_0}}=O_p(1/r_n)\).

Steps 1--3 leave only candidates with exactly \(s_0\) splits, structurally
matched, and with every threshold within \(O(1/r_n)\) of its match, which is
(i)--(iii). The full proof is given in the Supplementary Material,
\S\ref{sec:supp-topology}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The three steps use three different margins, and none substitutes for
another. Step 1's margin is \(\lambda_n\)-scale, which is why the penalty must
dominate \(1/r_n\) (R3) --- the reverse of a grid-exact regime, and the single
substantive change in the rate conditions. Step 2's margin is a fixed
constant, supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} and \emph{not} available from
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}, whose margin vanishes with \(r_n\). Step 3's
margin is \(1/r_n\)-scale and comes from Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}, which
Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} cannot supply, being silent about threshold precision
within a correct template. The concentration input is uniform over the random
feasible class and is assembled in Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain}; (R5) is what
makes it close, and is the one rate condition this architecture adds. Failure
can occur if the solver is not globally exact, if the density vanishes
anywhere on \(\cX\) (breaking Step 2), if two geometrically adjacent true
leaves share a value (breaking Step 2), if \(\lambda_n\) is taken below
\(1/r_n\) (breaking Step 1), or if \(m_n\) grows more slowly than \(\sqrt n\)
(breaking the concentration chain).

\begin{remark}[What is recovered, and at what level]\label{rem:identification}
Conclusion (ii) is a statement about \emph{partitions}, not about rooted-tree
order, and this is not a weakening forced by the proof but the only
identifiable target. A regression function can admit several tree
realisations with the same leaf partition --- split \(x_1\) then \(x_2\), or
\(x_2\) then \(x_1\), for identical leaf cells and values --- and these are
observationally indistinguishable, so no estimator can consistently recover a
specific split order. Definition~\ref{def:true-tree} already fixes the truth
at the leaf-partition equivalence-class level for exactly this reason, and
Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching} is stated to match. Writing
\([\tau_0]\) for that class, the content of (i)--(iii) is that
\(\PP([\hat\tau^A_n]=[\tau_0])\to1\) once thresholds are read up to the
\(O_p(1/r_n)\) tolerance, with boundaries matched by geometric signature
(coordinate, threshold, adjacent faces) under a fixed canonical
representation of each class ---
Definition~\ref{def:canonical-realisation} of \S\ref{sec:setup-uniqueness}
supplies one such representation, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-nonunique} there gives the split-$x_1$-then-$x_2$-versus-$x_2$-then-$x_1$
example just invoked in different words. Downstream, the same reading applies:
\S\ref{sec:stageb}'s conclusion
\(\max_N\abs{\hat c_N-t_{0,N_0}}=O_p(n^{-1})\) is a statement about the
matched boundaries of that partition class. A companion paper's
partition-recovery condition should be read the same way, since literal
rooted-tree equality is strictly stronger than, and not implied by,
partition-level recovery.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Search-interval validity]\label{prop:search-interval}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}, and with
\(\rho_n=M_n/r_n\) for \(M_n\to\infty\) with \(M_n/r_n\to0\) as in
Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}, the following hold simultaneously for
every node \(N\) of \(\topol(\hat\tau^A_n)\) with probability tending to one:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),nosep]
  \item \(t_{0,N}\in I_n(N)\);
  \item \(I_n(N)\) contains no \emph{other} true threshold on \(N\)'s
  splitting coordinate;
  \item \(I_n(N)\) lies inside the fixed density collar \(R_N^+\) of
  Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens) around \(t_{0,N}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(Y_n:=r_n\max_N\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N}}\), the maximum being over the
at most \(\bar L-1\) nodes of the matching supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(ii), a number fixed in \(n\). By
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(iii), \(Y_n=O_p(1)\), so tightness gives
\(\PP(Y_n>M_n)\to0\) for any \(M_n\to\infty\). It therefore suffices to argue
on \(\{Y_n\le M_n\}\), on which \(\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N}}\le M_n/r_n=\rho_n\)
for every \(N\) simultaneously.

Claim (1) is immediate: \(I_n(N)\) is the closed \(\rho_n\)-ball about
\(\hat t^A_N\), which contains \(t_{0,N}\) exactly when
\(\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N}}\le\rho_n\).

For (2), every \(u\in I_n(N)\) satisfies
\(\abs{u-t_{0,N}}\le\abs{u-\hat t^A_N}+\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N}}\le2\rho_n\).
Since \(\rho_n\to0\), eventually \(2\rho_n<\varsigma/2\). If a second true
threshold \(t_{0,N'}\) on the same coordinate lay in \(I_n(N)\), then
\(\abs{t_{0,N'}-t_{0,N}}\le2\rho_n<\varsigma\), contradicting
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep).

For (3), let \(c_{\mathrm{col}}=\varsigma/2\) be the collar half-width of
(Dens). Eventually \(2\rho_n<c_{\mathrm{col}}\), and the display in the
previous paragraph then places every \(u\in I_n(N)\) within
\(c_{\mathrm{col}}\) of \(t_{0,N}\), hence inside \(R_N^+\). A union bound
over the fixed number of nodes gives simultaneity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The whole proof is the observation that \(\rho_n=M_n/r_n\) exceeds the
\(O_p(r_n^{-1})\) Stage-A error by a diverging factor while still vanishing;
both halves of that are used, (1) needing the first and (2)--(3) the second.
The node count must be fixed in \(n\) for tightness to be available
uniformly, which Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S) supplies. Only
separation, not the grid mesh, sets the eventual scale in (2), so the
conclusion can fail if same-coordinate true boundaries approach one another
with \(n\).

\begin{remark}[Why the search interval shrinks, and what that costs]
\label{rem:interval-vs-bracket}
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval} guarantees that \(I_n(N)\) is
eventually \emph{contained} in any fixed interval about \(t_{0,N}\) --- in
particular in the \(B^-\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain} --- and not
that it eventually \emph{contains} one. This is the opposite containment from
what a bracket bounded by neighbouring splits would give, and it is worth
flagging because the direction matters for the argument that consumes it.
A minimiser over a large set that happens to land in a small subset is
automatically a minimiser over that subset; a minimiser over a small set
carries no such implication about a larger one. Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}
is therefore not a matter of reversing an inclusion in an existing argument:
it inserts a deterministic intermediate interval, derives the rate on the
\emph{fixed} \(B^-\) where the classical machinery applies, and then shows the
resulting confinement is tight enough to fall inside the shrinking
\(I_n(N)\). The rate condition that step needs, \(n\rho_n\to\infty\), comes
from (R1) rather than from Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}'s own
conditions on \(M_n\), which do not imply it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Separation and consistency on a fixed domain}
\label{sec:stageb-fixed-domain}

Both tiers below face the same preliminary difficulty before any rate
argument applies: the interval $I_n(N)$ that Stage B actually searches
(Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}; valid with probability tending to one by
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}) is a random set --- centred at the
data-dependent $\hat t^A_N$ --- and it moreover \emph{shrinks}, whereas the
classical results this section relies on
(\citealt{chan1993,seijosen2011}, and Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}'s own
localisation argument below) presume a fixed, data-independent search
region. Two lemmas discharge this in sequence, and the division of labour
matters. The present lemma supplies the population separation and the
argmin-consistency \emph{mechanism} on an arbitrary \emph{fixed} compact
domain, which is all the classical machinery ever needs;
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}, after the rate has been established on a
fixed interval, transfers it to the shrinking $I_n(N)$. Nothing in this
subsection refers to $I_n(N)$ at all.

\begin{lemma}[Separation and fixed-domain consistency]
\label{lem:fixed-domain}
Fix an internal node $N$ on coordinate $q$ with true threshold
$c_0:=c_{0,N}$, and let $[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ denote the fixed, compact extent of
the true node box $R_N$ along coordinate $q$ (well defined and bounded, by
the construction of $R_N^+$ and the bounded coordinate diameters assumed in
clause (Dens) of Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}). (Here and below, $R_N$ and
$R_N^+$ always denote the node's
axis-aligned \emph{box}, per clause (Dens); a superscripted $R_N^{\bullet}$
with $\bullet\in\{X,\mathrm{stump},\mathrm{prof}\}$ always denotes a
real-valued \emph{risk}. The two uses do not otherwise overlap.) Write
$R_N^X$ for the relevant population risk as a
function of the candidate threshold --- $R_N^{\mathrm{stump}}$ if both
children of $N$ are terminal, $R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}$ otherwise. Let
\[
  B^-:=\bigl(c_0-\varsigma/4,\,c_0+\varsigma/4\bigr),
\]
a FIXED interval whose width $\varsigma/2$ is strictly less than $\varsigma$,
so that Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep) alone excludes every
\emph{other} true threshold on coordinate $q$ from it, deterministically and
with no probabilistic argument. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item (Global separation) For every $\eta\in(0,q_N^+-q_N^-]$,
  \[
    \delta_N(\eta)
    :=
    \inf\Bigl\{R_N^X(c)-R_N^X(c_0):\
      c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+],\ \abs{c-c_0}\ge\eta\Bigr\}
    >0.
  \]
  In particular $\delta_N:=\delta_N(\varsigma/4)>0$.
  \item (Fixed-domain consistency) Let $D$ be any fixed, deterministic
  compact subset of $[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ containing $c_0$ in its interior, and let
  $\widehat c_N^D$ minimise the empirical criterion over $D$. Then
  $\widehat c_N^D\to_pc_0$. This holds in particular for
  $D=[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ and for $D=\overline{B^-}$, and moreover
  $\PP\bigl(\widehat c_N^{[q_N^-,q_N^+]}=\widehat c_N^{\overline{B^-}}\bigr)
  \to1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Part (a) is population-only: the ANOVA identity makes
$R_N^X(c)-R_N^X(c_0)$ nonnegative, and strictly positive for $c\ne c_0$
because the slab $\mathcal I_N(c,c_0)$ has strictly positive $\nu$-mass by
(Dens) while Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(Local-Jump) gives one of its
cells a nonzero jump, so $\tau(c)$
is not sufficient; continuity of $R_N^X$ plus compactness of
$\{c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+]:\abs{c-c_0}\ge\eta\}$ then forces a strictly positive
infimum. Part (b) applies the standard argmin-consistency argument for
M-estimators on a fixed compact parameter space \citep{vandervaart1998}, the
required
uniform convergence following from Glivenko--Cantelli applied to the finitely
many partial sums indexed by the monotone indicator $\mathbf 1\{X_q\le c\}$,
together with Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}'s second-moment bound. Because that
argument uses nothing about $D$ beyond its being fixed, compact, and
containing $c_0$ in its interior, it applies verbatim to each of the two
domains named; the final claim then follows because a global minimiser over
the larger domain which lands in the smaller one --- an event of probability
tending to one, by consistency --- is automatically the minimiser over the
smaller one as well. Both nested sets here are deterministic, so no property
of Stage~A is used. The full proof is
given in the Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.} Part (a) uses only (Dens), (Local-Jump), and
compactness --- no probabilistic tool. Part (b) uses only a
coordinatewise Glivenko--Cantelli argument on a FIXED compact domain. Neither
part refers to Stage~A, to $I_n(N)$, or to
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}, which is what makes the lemma available to
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} without circularity: that lemma's own
conclusion is what connects the fixed domain to the interval Stage~B
searches. The nesting step inside (b) is between two \emph{deterministic}
sets and is therefore not the discharge of any randomness; the randomness is
discharged in Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}. The lemma can fail if
$\varsigma$ (the separation constant) is not fixed, or if the node box $R_N$
is unbounded.

\subsection{Two tiers of refinement}\label{sec:stageb-tiers}

The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency} has two tiers.
Both are run on the FIXED interval $B^-$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}, where the classical machinery applies;
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} below then carries the resulting rate over
to the actual, shrinking search interval $I_n(N)$, for both tiers at once. The
tiers differ only in which criterion --- stump or profiled --- is analysed on
$B^-$.

\paragraph{Tier 1: leaf-parent nodes.}
If both children of \(N\) are terminal, the Stage-B objective is exactly the
classical two-group stump criterion, taken under the design measure \(\nu\)
rather than under \(P_X\); since \(\nu\) is a fixed measure equivalent to
\(P_X\) by Assumption~\ref{ass:design}, and the observations are i.i.d.\ by
Assumption~\ref{ass:iid}, the change-point results below apply unchanged with
\(\nu\) in place of \(P_X\) throughout.
Write $\widetilde c_N$ for the minimiser of that criterion over the FIXED
interval $B^-$ --- a legitimate, unconditionally-defined statistic, consistent
by Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(b). The random-design change-point
results of
\citet{chan1993} and \citet{seijosen2011}, whose
conditions are supplied here by (Dens), Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Node),
(Local-Jump), and the standing
second-moment condition, apply directly to $\widetilde c_N$ and give
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}
  \PP\!\left(n\abs{\widetilde c_N-c_{0,N}}>u\right)
  =:\pi_N(u),
  \qquad
  \pi_N(u)\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{as }u\to\infty ,
\]
that is, $n(\widetilde c_N-c_{0,N})=O_p(1)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}
transfers this to the actual Stage-B output:
\[
  n(\widehat c_N-c_{0,N})=O_p(1).
\]

\paragraph{Tier 2: non-terminal children.}
If at least one child is non-terminal, replacing each child subtree by a
single group is generally invalid.  In particular, a descendant split on the
same coordinate can make the resulting two-group population criterion attain
its minimum away from \(c_{0,N}\).  The Stage-B criterion must instead retain
and profile all descendant leaves.  The following lemma treats precisely that
criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Prism decomposition of the local profiled criterion]
\label{lem:prism-profile}
Fix an internal node \(N\), its splitting coordinate \(q\), its true threshold
\(c_0:=c_{0,N}\), and its true box \(R_N\).  Suppose that the topology,
the true ancestor row set, and the population-level descendant cell structure
are fixed.  (The corresponding statement with descendant thresholds held at
their Stage-A values instead of their true values follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} below, with \(p_{\min}\) replaced by
\(p_{\min}/2\) throughout.)  For a candidate \(c\) in the node's true box (in particular for
\(c\) in the fixed interval \(B^-\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain},
which is where parts (iv)--(v) below are ultimately applied), let
\[
  \mathcal I_N(c,c_0)
  :=
  R_N\cap
  \bigl\{x:x_q\in(c\wedge c_0,c\vee c_0]\bigr\}.
\]
Then:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
  \item \(\mathcal I_N(c,c_0)\) is exactly the set whose side-of-\(N\)
  membership differs under \(c\) and \(c_0\).

  \item The slab \(\mathcal I_N(c,c_0)\) admits a disjoint decomposition into
  at most
  \[
    L_N^-L_N^+
    \le \frac{(L_N^-+L_N^+)^2}{4}
    \le \frac{\bar L^2}{4}
  \]
  prism cells, where \(L_N^-\) and \(L_N^+\) are the numbers of
  descendant leaves below the two children of \(N\). The bound is on the
  total number of cells in the decomposition, whether or not a given cell
  turns out to be active in the sense of (iii).

  \item Call prism cell \(j\) \emph{active} if its \((p-1)\)-dimensional
  footprint \(G_j\) --- its projection onto the coordinates other than
  \(q\) --- has positive Lebesgue measure,
  \(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(G_j)>0\).
  If \(\omega_{N,j}(c)\) is the \(\nu\)-mass of prism cell \(j\), and
  \(\Delta_{N,j}\) is the jump between its adjacent leaves, then for every
  active cell \(j\),
  \[
    \omega_{N,j}(c)=\Theta\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}\right),
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\min}\le\abs{\Delta_{N,j}}\le\kappa_{\max}.
  \]
  An inactive cell --- one with \(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(G_j)=0\), i.e.\ a
  source--target leaf pair whose footprints do not overlap --- has
  \(\omega_{N,j}\equiv0\) and contributes zero to the drift sum of (iv); it is
  nonetheless included in the cell count of (ii), which therefore bounds the
  total number of cells rather than the number of active ones, and is retained
  there only as an upper-bound artefact.
  Thus, at \(c=c_0+u/n\) with fixed \(u\), every active cell
  has \(\omega_{N,j}(c)=\Theta(n^{-1})\).

  \item Writing \(R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)\) for the population squared-error
  risk after profiling all descendant-leaf means,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:prism-risk}
    R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)-R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c_0)
    =
    \sum_j \omega_{N,j}(c)\Delta_{N,j}^2
    +O\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}^2\right).
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, for all sufficiently small \(\abs{c-c_0}\),
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:prism-drift}
    R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)-R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c_0)
    \ge
    \frac{\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}}{4}
    \abs{c-c_0}.
  \end{equation}

  \item Let \(\widetilde c_N\) minimise the corresponding empirical,
  fully-profiled subtree SSE over observed values in the FIXED interval
  \(B^-\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}. Then
  \[
    n(\widetilde c_N-c_0)=O_p(1),
  \]
  and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} below, the same rate holds for
  \(\widehat c_N\), the oracle-criterion Stage-B output --- the same
  minimisation but over the shrinking search interval \(I_n(N)\) rather than
  the fixed \(B^-\):
  \[
    n(\widehat c_N-c_0)=O_p(1).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The argument has four steps. Step~1 identifies the moving slab: only an
observation whose $q$-coordinate lies between $c$ and $c_0$ changes side of
$N$, which is (i). Step~2 refines that slab into at most
$L_N^-L_N^+\le\bar L^2/4$ disjoint prism cells indexed by realised
source--target leaf pairs; this is legitimate because the domain on which
parts (iv)--(v) are applied is the fixed interval $B^-$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}, whose width $\varsigma/2$ is strictly less
than $\varsigma$, so that Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep) alone
excludes every other same-coordinate true threshold from $B^-$ ---
deterministically, with no appeal to Stage~A or to any probabilistic
statement. Hence no candidate in $B^-$ crosses another $q$-split, every
same-coordinate
descendant test is constant throughout the slab and may be absorbed into a
fixed path label, while cell masses and jump marks are read off (Dens) and
(Local-Jump) --- giving (ii) and (iii). Step~3 applies the weighted ANOVA
identity one destination leaf at a time; the pooling correction is
$O(\abs{c-c_0}^2)$ once (Node) bounds the correctly assigned core mass below,
which yields~\eqref{eq:prism-risk} and then, with the cross-sectional mass
bound $\sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)\ge p_{\min}\abs{c-c_0}/2$ derived below, the
linear drift
bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift}. Step~4 rescales to $c=c_0+u/n$, where every
active cell holds $\Theta(1)$ observations, and represents the local
unnormalised criterion as a finite sum --- at most $\bar L^2/4$ terms, a number
fixed in $n$ --- of marked rare-event counting processes with aggregate
one-sided drift at least $\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}\abs u/2$; this is the point
at which a tree differs from a two-group problem, since no single change-point
process carries the drift. Consistency on the fixed domain, from
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(b), confines the minimiser to shrinking
neighbourhoods of $c_0$, on which the argmax theorem for discontinuous,
locally-defined criteria \citep{seootsu2018} applies and yields tightness of
$n(\widetilde c_N-c_0)$; Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} transfers it to
the actual Stage-B output, proving (v). The full proof is given in the
Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
All structural conditions used above are contained in
Assumptions~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}--\ref{ass:local-design}; exact
minimisation comes from Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}, and the fixed
domain from Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain} together with (Sep) --- not from
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(b), whose search interval is the
shrinking $I_n(N)$ and is the wrong object for Step~2's exclusion of
same-coordinate crossings.  The cited change-point
results are used for their rate-\(n\) localisation machinery, while the new
prism and joint-process arguments verify the feature not covered directly by
those references.  The linear drift constant is explicitly
\(\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}/4\), and the quadratic ANOVA remainder cannot change
the rate.  The argument can fail if an active cell's adjacent leaf pair has
zero jump, if
the local design density vanishes, if leaf masses approach zero, or if the
number of leaves grows with \(n\).  With fixed nonzero jumps, the \(n^{-1}\)
rate is the usual sharp change-point rate.

\paragraph{The drift constant.}
The constant \(p_{\min}\) appearing in the drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift},
and in every drift bound in this section, is
\[
  p_{\min}:=f_{\min}\,c_{\mathrm{box}}\,q_{\min},
\]
with \(f_{\min}\) the density lower bound of clause (Dens), \(q_{\min}\) the
node-mass floor of clause (Node), and \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\) the
cross-sectional constant fixed before Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}. The
three factors are not interchangeable, and the \((p-1)\)-dimensional factor
cannot be omitted: \(\omega_{N,j}(c)\) is the \(\nu\)-mass of a
\(p\)-dimensional prism cell of thickness \(\abs{c-c_0}\), so it carries the
cell's \((p-1)\)-dimensional cross-sectional size as well as a density.
Explicitly, writing \(G_j\) for the \((p-1)\)-dimensional footprint of prism
cell \(j\) in the coordinates other than \(q\), clause (Dens) gives
\[
  \omega_{N,j}(c)
  \ \ge\
  f_{\min}\,\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(G_j)\,\abs{c-c_0}
  +o\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}\right).
\]
The footprints \(G_j\) are disjoint and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(i)--(ii),
exhaust the cross-section \(\Gamma_N\) of the box \(R_N\) transverse to
\(q\); the inactive cells of Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(iii) have
\(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(G_j)=0\) and so contribute nothing to either side of
the display below, while (Local-Jump) supplies a nonzero jump mark on each
active cell. Summing therefore
gives exactly
\[
  \sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)
  \ \ge\
  f_{\min}\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)\abs{c-c_0}
  +o\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}\right),
\]
and, as fixed above, \(\operatorname{Leb}_{p-1}(\Gamma_N)\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}
q_{\min}\). Absorbing the \(o(\abs{c-c_0})\) term by shrinking the
neighbourhood yields the bound
\[
  \sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)\ \ge\ \frac{p_{\min}}{2}\abs{c-c_0}
\]
used in Step~3 of the proof sketch above and in~\eqref{eq:prism-drift}. Making
the cross-sectional factor explicit weakens every drift constant in this
section by the fixed positive factor \(c_{\mathrm{box}}q_{\min}\),
and changes nothing else: no condition in this paper requires
\(p_{\min}\) to exceed a specified threshold.

\paragraph{From the fixed interval to the actual search interval.}
Both tiers deliver a rate for the minimiser over the FIXED interval \(B^-\).
Stage~B, however, minimises over \(I_n(N)\), which is random and shrinking.
The following lemma closes that gap, and does so for both tiers at once, since
it uses only the population drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift} --- whose stump
analogue is the classical two-group bound --- together with
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}. The argument is not a matter of reversing
an inclusion: as Remark~\ref{rem:interval-vs-bracket} notes, \(I_n(N)\) is
eventually contained in \(B^-\) rather than containing it, and the useful
implication runs the other way. What makes it work is inserting a
\emph{deterministic} pair of intervals between the two, so that the rate can
be established where the classical machinery applies and then shown tight
enough to land inside the shrinking set.

\begin{lemma}[Interval reduction]\label{lem:interval-reduction}
Fix an internal node \(N\) on coordinate \(q\), with true threshold
\(c_{0,N}\). Let \(S_n\) denote the \emph{oracle} Stage-B criterion at \(N\)
--- the local criterion formed under the true ancestor row set and the true
descendant leaf structure, i.e.\ the empirical, fully-profiled subtree SSE of
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} (or its Tier-1 stump analogue) --- and let
\(\hat c_N\) be \emph{any} measurable selection from \(S_n\)'s argmin set over
\(I_n(N)\). Assume Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}, the population
drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift} for the relevant criterion (or its stump
analogue), and the rate conditions~\eqref{eq:stage-a-rates}. Then
\[
  n\bigl(\hat c_N-c_{0,N}\bigr)=O_p(1).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} and Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}
transfer this rate to the fully estimated criterion, under Stage-A-precision
descendants and Stage-B-refined ancestors respectively; this lemma supplies
only the base (oracle-ancestor, true-descendant) case those two build on.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The argument has five steps, and the first is what makes the rest ordinary.

\emph{1. A deterministic sandwich.} On the event
\(E_n:=\{\abs{\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}}\le\rho_n/2\}\) --- of probability tending to
one, since \(\abs{\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}}=O_p(1/r_n)=o_p(\rho_n)\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(iii) and \(M_n\to\infty\); taking \(E_n\)
from the same \(\{Y_n\le M_n/2\}\) used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}'s proof gives it simultaneously over
all nodes at no extra cost --- define the \emph{non-random} intervals
\[
  J_n^-:=\bigl[c_{0,N}-\tfrac{\rho_n}2,\,c_{0,N}+\tfrac{\rho_n}2\bigr],
  \qquad
  J_n^+:=\bigl[c_{0,N}-\tfrac{3\rho_n}2,\,c_{0,N}+\tfrac{3\rho_n}2\bigr].
\]
Writing \(d:=\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}\) with \(\abs d\le\rho_n/2\), the endpoints of
\(I_n(N)=[c_{0,N}+d-\rho_n,\,c_{0,N}+d+\rho_n]\) satisfy
\(c_{0,N}-\tfrac32\rho_n\le c_{0,N}+d-\rho_n\le c_{0,N}-\tfrac12\rho_n\) and
symmetrically on the right, so
\[
  J_n^-\ \subseteq\ I_n(N)\ \subseteq\ J_n^+
  \qquad\text{on }E_n,
\]
both inclusions being attained exactly at \(\abs d=\rho_n/2\), so the
constants \(\tfrac12\) and \(\tfrac32\) are sharp.

\emph{2. Constant bookkeeping.} Since \(\rho_n\to0\), eventually
\(\rho_n<\varsigma/8\), whence \(3\rho_n/2<\varsigma/4\) and
\(J_n^+\subseteq B^-\); and eventually \(3\rho_n/2<c_{\mathrm{col}}\), placing
\(J_n^+\) inside the fixed density collar. Both are deterministic consequences
of \(\rho_n\to0\) alone, not of
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}'s clauses, which concern \(I_n(N)\).
Note \(\abs{B^-}=\varsigma/2<\varsigma\), used next.

\emph{3. The rate on the fixed interval, for \emph{every} minimiser.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a) supplies a well-separated minimum on
\(B^-\subseteq[q_N^-,q_N^+]\) and (b) supplies argmin-consistency there, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(i)--(iv) supply the prism decomposition and the
linear drift --- the decomposition being legitimate on \(B^-\) because
\(\abs{B^-}<\varsigma\) makes (Sep) exclude every other same-coordinate true
threshold from it outright. The conclusion needed is stronger than tightness
of a chosen selection, and is obtained by strict domination rather than by any
weak-convergence argument: the escape-exclusion step already used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}'s proof, applied here to the fixed
\(B^-\), shows that for every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(U<\infty\) with which,
with probability at least \(1-\epsilon\) eventually, the criterion strictly
exceeds its value at the competitor \(c_{0,N}\in B^-\) throughout
\(\{c\in B^-:\abs{c-c_{0,N}}>U/n\}\). Since \(c_{0,N}\) is itself available
and achieves a strictly lower value, \emph{no} point of that region can be a
global minimiser over \(B^-\); hence \emph{every} minimiser over \(B^-\) lies
in \(\{\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\}\).

\emph{4. Nesting.} Fix \(\epsilon>0\), take \(U\) from step 3, and let \(n\)
be large enough that \(U\le n\rho_n/2\). This last is where
\(n\rho_n\to\infty\) is used, and it is supplied by (R1): \(r_n=o(\sqrt{n/\log
r_n})\) gives \(n/r_n\to\infty\), so \(n\rho_n=nM_n/r_n\to\infty\) with
\(M_n\ge1\). It is \emph{not} implied by
Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}'s conditions on \(M_n\) alone: for
instance \(r_n=n^2\), \(M_n=\log n\) satisfies \(M_n\to\infty\) and
\(M_n/r_n\to0\) yet gives \(n\rho_n\to0\). On \(E_n\) intersected with step
3's event, every \(c\in\argmin_{B^-}\) satisfies
\(\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\le\rho_n/2\), hence lies in
\(J_n^-\subseteq I_n(N)\subseteq J_n^+\subseteq B^-\). So the whole argmin
over the larger \(B^-\) lies inside the subset \(I_n(N)\), giving
\(\min_{I_n(N)}=\min_{B^-}\) and \(\argmin_{I_n(N)}=\argmin_{B^-}\) exactly,
not merely with a common intersection. Consequently \emph{any} measurable
selection \(\hat c_N\in\argmin_{I_n(N)}\) lies in \(\argmin_{B^-}\), so
\(\abs{\hat c_N-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\). No uniqueness of the minimiser is needed,
and no assumption on the law of \(Z\) beyond
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} is used.

\emph{5. Combine.} For every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
  \PP\bigl(n\abs{\hat c_N-c_{0,N}}>U\bigr)
  \le
  \PP(E_n^c)
  +\PP\bigl(\argmin_{B^-}\not\subseteq\{\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\}\bigr)
  \longrightarrow 0+\epsilon
\]
for \(n\) large enough, depending on \(U\) via step 4. As \(\epsilon\) was
arbitrary, \(n(\hat c_N-c_{0,N})=O_p(1)\). The full proof is given in the
Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
Tightness is established entirely on the \emph{fixed} interval \(B^-\), in
step 3; nothing in the argument attempts to establish it directly on a
shrinking or growing range. That distinction is essential rather than
stylistic: the escape-exclusion mechanism of
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} is uniform in \(u\) only because its
contaminating set has \(u\)-independent mass \(O(M/n)\), whereas the moving
prism slab relevant here has mass \(\Theta(\abs u/n)\), growing with \(u\), so
that mechanism cannot be borrowed for a growing range --- and borrowing it
would in any case be circular, since Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}'s
own proof assumes the oracle tightness this lemma helps supply. The role of
\(n\rho_n\to\infty\) is confined to step 4, where it only guarantees that a
\emph{fixed} \(U\), however large, is eventually below \(n\rho_n/2\); it is
sourced from (R1), not from Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}. Step 3's
conclusion is a for-every-minimiser statement obtained by domination, which is
what allows step 4 to conclude an exact equality of argmin \emph{sets} and so
to hold for an arbitrary measurable selection; a tightness statement about one
chosen selection would not suffice, and appealing to uniqueness of the
minimiser would additionally require an atomless response law that
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} deliberately does not impose. The lemma can fail
if \(\rho_n\) does not vanish (breaking step 2), if \(n\rho_n\) stays bounded
(breaking step 4), or if the drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift} degenerates.

\begin{remark}[Profiling the descendant-leaf means]
\label{rem:profiling-correction}
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} remains valid when every descendant-leaf mean is
re-fitted at every candidate threshold, as Stage B requires.  To see this
explicitly, fix \(\abs u\le U\), put \(h=u/n\), and let \(L(c)\) be a candidate
leaf.  Since its population mass is at least \(q_{\min}/2\), its candidate
population mean satisfies
\[
  \mu_{L(c)}-\mu_{L(c_0)}
  =
  O(\abs h).
\]
Indeed, only \(O(n\abs h)\) observations in expectation are reassigned, their
population contribution is \(O(\abs h)\), and the leaf denominator is bounded
away from zero.  Conditional sampling fluctuation contributes the usual
root-\(n\) term, so uniformly over the finitely many leaves and
\(\abs u\le U\),
\begin{align*}
  \widehat\mu_{L(c)}-\mu_{L(c_0)}
  &=
  \{\mu_{L(c)}-\mu_{L(c_0)}\}
  +\{\widehat\mu_{L(c)}-\mu_{L(c)}\}
  \\
  &=
  O(\abs{c-c_0})+O_p(n^{-1/2}).
\end{align*}
The first term is deterministic misclassification bias; the second is
sampling noise under a fixed leaf assignment.

Because squared error is quadratic in the leaf mean, the resulting
population-risk correction is
\[
  O\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}^2\right)
  +
  O_p\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}\,n^{-1/2}\right).
\]
More precisely, if \(n_L(c)\) is the leaf count and
\(d_L(c)=\widehat\mu_{L(c)}-\mu_{L(c)}\), the least-squares identity gives
\[
  \sum_{i\in L(c)}\{Z_i-\widehat\mu_{L(c)}\}^2
  =
  \sum_{i\in L(c)}\{Z_i-\mu_{L(c)}\}^2
  -
  n_L(c)d_L(c)^2.
\]
For \(\abs u\le U\), only \(O_p(1)\) observations change assignment, whence
\[
  n_L(c)-n_L(c_0)=O_p(1),
  \qquad
  d_L(c_0)=O_p(n^{-1/2}),
  \qquad
  d_L(c)-d_L(c_0)=o_p(n^{-1/2}).
\]
The last display follows because only \(O_p(1)\) of the leaf's \(\Theta(n)\)
observations are reassigned, contributing a mean shift of
\(O_p(1)/\Theta(n)=O_p(n^{-1})\), strictly smaller than the leaf's own
\(O_p(n^{-1/2})\) sampling fluctuation.
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
  &n_L(c)d_L(c)^2-n_L(c_0)d_L(c_0)^2
  \\
  &\quad=
  \{n_L(c)-n_L(c_0)\}d_L(c_0)^2
  +n_L(c)\{d_L(c)-d_L(c_0)\}
       \{d_L(c)+d_L(c_0)\}
  \\
  &\quad=
  O_p(n^{-1})+o_p(n^{-1/2})O_p(n^{1/2})
  =
  o_p(1)
\end{align*}
on the unnormalised local-SSE scale, uniformly over \(\abs u\le U\).
On the normalised risk scale, the deterministic and cross terms are
\(O(n^{-2})\) and \(O_p(n^{-3/2})\), whereas
\eqref{eq:prism-drift} is at least
\(\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}\abs u/(4n)\).  Thus profiling contributes only a
second-order perturbation and does not change the \(n^{-1}\) threshold rate.
The conclusion concerns threshold locations; the profiled leaf means
themselves retain their ordinary \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\) rate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Transfer to Stage-A descendant thresholds}
\label{sec:stageb-descendant}

Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain} and Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} are
both stated with every descendant threshold at its \emph{true} value.
Stage~B does not have access to true descendant values: by
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(b), a node's refinement proceeds
levelwise with every strictly-deeper threshold still held at its
Stage-A grid value. The next lemma closes this gap.

\begin{lemma}[Stability under Stage-A descendant thresholds]
\label{lem:descendant-stability}
Let \(N\) be an internal node and let \(\widetilde R^X_{N,n}\) denote the
population criterion of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain} computed with
every descendant threshold of \(N\) held at its Stage-A value rather than at
its true value. Fix \(\epsilon>0\) and let
\[
  E_n:=
  \Bigl\{
    \hat\tau^A_n\text{ structurally matches }\tau_0,\text{ and }
    \max_M\abs{\hat t^A_M-t_{0,M}}\le C/r_n
  \Bigr\},
\]
the maximum being over the matched nodes; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology} a fixed \(C<\infty\) can be chosen with
\(\PP(E_n)\ge1-\epsilon\) for all large \(n\). On \(E_n\) every descendant
threshold of \(N\) lies within \(C/r_n\) of its true value. Then, with
probability at least \(1-\epsilon\) for all large \(n\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
  \item \(\sup_{c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+]}
    \abs{\widetilde R^X_{N,n}(c)-R_N^X(c)}=O(1/r_n)=o(1)\);
  \item for every fixed \(\eta>0\),
    \(\inf_{\abs{c-c_0}\ge\eta}
      \{\widetilde R^X_{N,n}(c)-\widetilde R^X_{N,n}(c_0)\}
      \ge\tfrac12\delta_N(\eta)>0\);
  \item the perturbed prism masses and jump marks of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} satisfy
    \(\widetilde\omega_{N,j}(c)=\omega_{N,j}(c)\{1+O(1/r_n)\}\) and
    \(\abs{\widetilde\Delta_{N,j}}\ge\kappa_{\min}/2\), so
    \eqref{eq:prism-drift} holds for \(\widetilde R^X_{N,n}\) with
    \(p_{\min}\) replaced by \(p_{\min}/2\).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently Lemmas~\ref{lem:fixed-domain} and~\ref{lem:prism-profile}
apply verbatim to the criterion Stage~B actually minimises, with the drift
constant halved.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
On the event $E_n$ every descendant threshold is displaced by $O(1/r_n)$,
which by the upper density bound of (Dens) and the bounded coordinate
diameters perturbs the $\nu$-mass of the affected leaf regions by $O(1/r_n)$;
part (a) follows from this and the boundedness of the profiled leaf means
(Assumptions~\ref{ass:design} and~\ref{ass:construct}(c)). Part (b) combines
(a) with Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a): the perturbed infimum lies
within $O(1/r_n)$ of the true one and so exceeds $\delta_N(\eta)/2$ once
$1/r_n<\delta_N(\eta)/2$. Part (c) follows because each perturbed
adjacent-leaf pair differs from a true pair only through regions of $\nu$-mass
$O(1/r_n)$, whose profiled means therefore differ by $O(1/r_n)$ given the
leaf-mass floor of (Node), so (Local-Jump) transfers to the perturbed pairs
with $\kappa_{\min}$ replaced by $\kappa_{\min}/2$. The full proof is given in
the Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ancestor-refinement contamination}\label{sec:stageb-ancestor}

\begin{lemma}[Contamination-robust levelwise localisation]
\label{lem:ancestor-localisation}
Suppose the conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} and
Remark~\ref{rem:profiling-correction} hold for a node at level \(\ell\) when
its row set is defined by the true ancestor thresholds.  If every ancestor
threshold has already been shown to satisfy
\[
  n(\widehat c_a-c_{0,a})=O_p(1),
\]
then the same conclusion holds when the level-\(\ell\) Stage-B criterion uses
the row set induced by the refined ancestor thresholds:
\[
  n(\widehat c_\ell-c_{0,\ell})=O_p(1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The induction hypothesis is used only to place every ancestor error inside a
deterministic collar $\mathcal C_{n,M}$ of half-width $M/n$, at the cost of one
third of an $\epsilon$-budget. Clause (Joint) of
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design} and a bare union bound then show
that, with probability tending to one, no observation lies simultaneously in
that collar and in the level-$\ell$ window
$\abs{x_{q_\ell}-c_{0,\ell}}\le U/n$, the relevant probability being
$O(MU/n)$; when ancestor and descendant share a coordinate, (Sep) makes the two
collars disjoint outright. On that event a contaminated observation's
prism-cell membership does not vary as $c$ ranges over the local window, so,
using the $\Theta_p(n)$ leaf denominators granted by
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(f), it perturbs the actual-minus-oracle
local contrast by only $O_p(n^{-1})=o_p(1)$ on the unnormalised SSE scale.
Because that control holds on a fixed compact $u$-window, escape must be
excluded separately: every contributing observation lies in
$\mathcal C_{n,M}$ \emph{regardless} of $u$ --- collar membership is a property
of the ancestor coordinate alone --- so the discrepancy is $O_p(1)$ uniformly
over $\abs u\le\varsigma n/4$ with no peeling and no $u$-indexed probability
calculation, and the escape-exclusion step that underlies the oracle
criterion's own tightness (\citealt{chan1993,seijosen2011} in Tier~1,
\citealt{seootsu2018} for the profiled criterion) then forces the actual
criterion to exceed its value at $c_{0,\ell}$ throughout
$U\le\abs u\le\varsigma n/4$. Combining the two ranges gives
$n(\widehat c_\ell-c_{0,\ell})=O_p(1)$. The full proof is given in the
Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The induction hypothesis is used only to construct the deterministic
ancestor collars.  Clause (Joint) is used only when ancestor and descendant
coordinates differ; (Sep) handles repeated coordinates.  The collar contains
\(O_p(1)\), not deterministically bounded, observations.  Direct
differentiation gives an \(O_p(n^{-1})=o_p(1)\) perturbation of the
unnormalised local contrast, which is dominated by its \(O_p(1)\) marked
change-point fluctuations and \(O(1)\) drift.  The argument would fail if the
joint density had a singular component concentrated at the crossing of two
boundaries, or if node masses vanished with \(n\).

\subsection{All levels and all recovered boundaries}\label{sec:stageb-all}

\begin{remark}[Non-local descendant splits]
\label{rem:nonlocal-descendants}
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(iii) places every recovered split, not
only the one currently being refined, within \(O_p(1/r_n)\) of its true
boundary.
Since \(r_n\to\infty\), these
other split locations converge to their true values.  Their induced prism
masses, jump marks, and positive drift constants therefore differ from their
population counterparts by \(o_p(1)\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability};
the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:prism-drift} remains bounded away from zero (at half its stated
value) with probability tending
to one.  No rate faster than consistency is required for these non-local
descendant splits.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous superconsistency of all refined boundaries]
\label{prop:twostage-superconsistency}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}, \ref{ass:design}, \ref{ass:moments},
\ref{ass:construct}, and~\ref{ass:budget} hold.
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}--\ref{ass:global-density},
including (Local-Jump), (Reduced), and (Joint),
and~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}, and under the full hypothesis set of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology} --- not merely its conclusion --- so
that along the joint sequence \((n,r_n,\lambda_n,m_n)\) the rate
conditions~\eqref{eq:stage-a-rates} hold, namely \(r_n\ge r_0\) with
\[
  r_n\to\infty,
  \quad
  r_n=o\bigl(\sqrt{n/\log r_n}\bigr),
  \quad
  \max\Bigl(\frac1{r_n},\sqrt{\frac{\log r_n}{n}}\Bigr)\ll\lambda_n\ll1,
\]
\[
  m_n\to\infty,
  \quad
  \frac{m_nr_n}{n}\to0,
  \quad
  m_n\lambda_n\to\infty,
  \quad
  \frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}\to\infty,
\]
and with \(M_n\to\infty\), \(M_n/r_n\to0\) fixing the search-interval scale
\(\rho_n=M_n/r_n\) of Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}, every
Stage-B-refined boundary
satisfies
\[
  \widehat c_k-c_{0,k}=O_p(n^{-1}),
  \qquad k=1,\ldots,K,
  \qquad K\le\bar L-1.
\]
Equivalently, the result holds simultaneously:
\[
  \max_{1\le k\le K}
  n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}
  =
  O_p(1).
\]
Thus the conclusion applies to an arbitrary fixed-\(\bar L\), fixed-depth
tree, not merely to a two- or three-leaf special case.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The root carries no ancestor contamination, so its rate on the fixed interval
$B^-$ is the classical stump
argument when both children are terminal and Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}
otherwise, with Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} covering its
still-Stage-A-precision descendants and
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} carrying the rate over to the actual
search interval $I_n(N)$; Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} then
propagates the rate one
level at a time, and the induction terminates after at most $\bar D$ levels because
the depth is fixed. Simultaneity is a finite union bound over the at most
$\bar L-1$ boundaries: choosing $M_k$ with
$\limsup_n\PP(n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}>M_k)<\epsilon/K$ for each $k$ and
taking $M=\max_{k\le K}M_k$ bounds the maximum. Since $K\le\bar L-1<\infty$, no
growing-dimensional union bound is required. The full proof is given in the
Supplementary Material, \S\ref{sec:supp-stageb}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Proof audit.}
The proof uses structural topology recovery
(Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}), fixed depth, fixed leaf count, exact
Stage-B optimisation, positive leaf masses, nonvanishing adjacent jumps, and
the local density conditions stated before the proposition.  Tier~1 is the
classical single-change-point case; Tier~2 is supplied by the new prism and
profiling arguments rather than by treating a non-terminal subtree as a
stump. Note what the induction does \emph{not} require: exact recovery of the
true partition by Stage~A, which is unavailable on a refining grid. What it
requires is the structural match of Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(ii)
together with the \(O_p(1/r_n)\) threshold localisation of (iii), the latter
entering only through Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability}'s halved drift
constant and Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}'s interval validity.
Every local process has \(O(1)\) drift and fluctuation on the
\(n^{-1}\) threshold scale, and all profiling and ancestor-row-set
perturbations are \(o_p(1)\) on the unnormalised SSE scale. Negligibility of
those perturbations is \emph{not} the blanket claim that \(O_p(1)\)
observations out of \(n\) cannot matter --- after the \(n\)-rescaling used for
the local argmin, a carelessly handled \(O_p(1)\)-count perturbation can be
\(O_p(1)\) on the rescaled criterion --- but follows specifically from
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability}'s density-perturbation argument and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}'s collar and union-bound argument, both
proved.  The rate is sharp
for discontinuous regression boundaries.  Failure is localised to vanishing
jumps, vanishing face density, collapsing leaf mass, singular
ancestor--descendant designs, imperfect optimisation, or a leaf count or
depth that grows with \(n\).

\begin{lemma}[Cell closure]\label{lem:cell-closure}
Assume Assumption~\ref{ass:support-geometry} and that \(\nu\) and Lebesgue
measure are mutually absolutely continuous on \(\cX\) (as supplied by
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density}, or by
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens-i) at the root node when there is
more than one leaf). Let \(A=\widetilde R_A\cap\cX\) and \(B=\widetilde
R_B\cap\cX\) be nonempty, full-dimensional relative box cells. If
\(\nu(A\triangle B)=0\), then \(\overline A=\overline B\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since \(\nu\) and Lebesgue measure are mutually absolutely continuous on
\(\cX\), \(\nu(A\triangle B)=0\) gives \(\operatorname{Leb}(A\triangle B)=0\).
Suppose
\(\overline A\ne\overline B\); without loss of generality fix
\(z\in\overline A\setminus\overline B\), so
\(\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(z,\overline B)>0\). Since \(A\) is nonempty,
full-dimensional, and convex, \(\overline A=\overline{\operatorname{int}(A)}\),
so there is \(z'\in\operatorname{int}(A)\cap B(z,\delta/4)\), and some
\(\varepsilon<\delta/4\) has \(B(z',\varepsilon)\subseteq A\). Every
\(w\in B(z',\varepsilon)\) then satisfies
\(\operatorname{dist}(w,\overline B)\ge\delta-\|w-z'\|-\|z'-z\|>\delta/2\),
so \(B(z',\varepsilon)\subseteq A\setminus B\), an open ball of positive
Lebesgue measure, contradicting \(\operatorname{Leb}(A\triangle B)=0\).
Hence \(\overline A\subseteq\overline B\), and by symmetry
\(\overline A=\overline B\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Boundary points lie on a split hyperplane]\label{lem:boundary-hyperplane}
For every leaf \(A=\widetilde R_A\cap\cX\) of a tree \(\tau^{\dagger}\),
\(\partial A\cap\operatorname{int}\cX\subseteq\bigcup\{\text{split
hyperplanes of }\tau^{\dagger}\}\) --- where a split hyperplane attached to
an internal node with test \((q,c)\) means the full affine hyperplane
\(\{x_q=c\}\subseteq\R^d\), not merely its trace on that node's own box.
Consequently \(\cF_0\cap\operatorname{int}\cX\subseteq\bigcup\{\text{split
hyperplanes of }\tau^{\dagger}\}\) as well, where
\(\cF_0:=\bigcup_{A\in\cP(\tau^{\dagger})}\partial A\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\(\partial A\subseteq(\partial \widetilde R_A\cap\cX)\cup(\overline{\widetilde R_A}\cap\partial\cX)\).
On \(\operatorname{int}\cX\) the second term is empty, so
\(\partial A\cap\operatorname{int}\cX\subseteq\partial \widetilde R_A\), a union of the
finitely many facets of the box \(\widetilde R_A\); each such facet lies in the split
hyperplane of some ancestor node of \(A\) (the hyperplane whose test cuts
out that facet of \(\widetilde R_A\)).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}]
\emph{Proof strategy.} Fix an arbitrary \(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\). Step~1
shows each \(\tau\)-leaf meets the interior of exactly one
\(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaf: a generic path inside the \(\tau\)-leaf converts the
alternative --- straddling two nonadjacent \(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaves of equal
value --- into a chain of face-adjacent \(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaves, and
(Reduced) then contradicts constancy on the \(\tau\)-leaf. Step~2 upgrades
this to refinement modulo \(\nu\). Step~3 uses (Node) for surjectivity of
the induced leaf map; Step~4 uses equal leaf counts for minimal trees to get
a bijection, and Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-closure} to upgrade \(\nu\)-equivalence
to equality of closures.

\paragraph*{Step 1: the strong one-cell claim.}
Assume \(d\ge2\) throughout (for \(d=1\) the argument goes through with the
set \(\Sigma\) below vacuous: leaf boundaries are single points, \(\cF_0\)
is finite, and the closing contradiction via (Reduced) is unchanged). Claim:
for every leaf \(L\in\cP(\tau)\),
\[
\tag{\(\star\)}
\operatorname{int}(L)\text{ meets the interior of exactly one leaf of }\tau^{\dagger}.
\]
Existence: \(\cF_0\) has empty interior, so \(\operatorname{int}(L)\), being
nonempty and open, cannot be contained in it, and meets the interior of at
least one \(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaf.

For uniqueness, suppose instead \(L\) meets the interiors of two distinct
\(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaves \(L_0^{(1)}\ne L_0^{(2)}\), and fix
\(x\in\operatorname{int}(L_0^{(1)})\cap\operatorname{int}(L)\); by (Conn),
\(\operatorname{int}(L)\) is open and convex. Call distinct leaves
\(A,A'\) of \(\tau^{\dagger}\) \emph{face-adjacent} when
\(\dim(\overline A\cap\overline{A'})=d-1\), and then call
\(F:=\overline A\cap\overline{A'}\) a \((d-1)\)-face; since \(F\) is convex,
\(\operatorname{relint}F\) is a relatively open codimension-one face in the
sense of (Reduced).

Every point of \(\cF_0\cap\operatorname{int}\cX\) that lies on exactly one
split hyperplane \(H\) and on no face's relative boundary lies in the
relative interior of a unique \((d-1)\)-face: such a \(p\) lies in
\(\partial A\) for some leaf \(A\); since \(\tau^{\dagger}\) has finitely
many split hyperplanes and boundary faces, and \(p\) lies on only \(H\)
among the former and on no relative boundary among the latter, there is
\(\rho>0\) with \(B(p,\rho)\subseteq\operatorname{int}\cX\) meeting no other
split hyperplane and no face's relative boundary; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-hyperplane}, \(B(p,\rho)\cap\cF_0\subseteq
H\cap B(p,\rho)\), so the open half-ball of \(B(p,\rho)\setminus H\)
opposite \(A\) is connected and meets \(\cF_0\) nowhere, hence lies entirely
in the interior of a single leaf \(A''\), giving
\(p\in\overline A\cap\overline{A''}=:F\), a \((d-1)\)-face not containing
\(p\) on its relative boundary. (Uniqueness of \(F\) follows from the next
paragraph: a second such face \(F'\ne F\) would satisfy \(F,F'\subset H\),
so \(p\in F\cap F'\subseteq\operatorname{rel}\partial F\) by the argument
given there, a contradiction.)

Two distinct such faces meet only in their relative boundaries. Let
\(F=\overline A\cap\overline{A'}\), \(F'=\overline B\cap\overline{B'}\),
both \(\subset H\), \(F\ne F'\), with \(A,B\) on the \(-\) side and
\(A',B'\) on the \(+\) side of \(H\). If \(A=B\) and \(A'\ne B'\), then
\(F\cap F'\subseteq\overline{A'}\cap\overline{B'}\); distinct leaves
\(A',B'\) have a lowest common ancestor node \(N''\) whose split hyperplane
\(H''\) separates them into its two closed half-spaces --- a purely
tree-structural fact using no convexity, since descendant boxes nest inside
ancestor boxes and the two leaves diverge into different children exactly
at \(N''\). Applying this to \(A,A'\) themselves shows \(F\) lies in their
own split hyperplane, i.e.\ \(H\); and \(H''\ne H\), since otherwise one of
\(A',B'\) would lie in the closed half-space of \(H\) opposite the one
containing it, forcing that leaf into \(H\) itself, contrary to
full-dimensionality. Since \(\overline{A'},\overline{B'}\) lie in the two
opposite closed half-spaces of \(H''\), \(F\subseteq\overline{A'}\) lies in
one closed half-space of \(H''\) while \(\operatorname{aff}(F)=H\not\subseteq H''\),
so any point of \(F\cap H''\) lies in \(F\)'s relative boundary (a relative-interior
point would have \(F\)-points strictly on both sides of \(H''\)); this uses
the convexity of leaf cells, (Conn). Hence
\(F\cap F'\subseteq\operatorname{rel}\partial F\cap H\cap H''\). The case
\(A\ne B\) is symmetric, and \(A=B,A'=B'\) gives \(F=F'\), excluded.

Let \(\Sigma\) be the union of the relative boundary of every \((d-1)\)-face
of \(\tau^{\dagger}\) together with all pairwise intersections of distinct
split hyperplanes of \(\tau^{\dagger}\) (pairwise intersections of distinct
faces are already absorbed into one item or the other, by the preceding
paragraph). The second part is a finite union of affine subspaces of
codimension two; the first is a finite union of sets of Hausdorff dimension
\(d-2\), since each \((d-1)\)-face is a compact convex body of dimension
\(d-1\) (whether or not \(\cX\) is a box) whose relative boundary is
\((d-2)\)-rectifiable. Hence \(\dim_{\mathcal H}\Sigma\le d-2\), and \(\Sigma\)
is closed. Separately, \(\cF_0\) itself has Hausdorff dimension at most
\(d-1\): each leaf is a \(d\)-dimensional compact convex body, so its
boundary has dimension \(d-1\), and \(\cF_0\) is a finite union of such
boundaries.

The set \(\Sigma\cup\bigcup\{\text{split hyperplanes}\}\) is closed and
Lebesgue-null. Since \(\operatorname{int}(L_0^{(1)})\cap\operatorname{int}(L)\)
is open and nonempty, take \(x\) in it generically, off that closed null
set; in particular \(x\) lies on no split hyperplane and \(x\notin\Sigma\).
Let \(U_2:=\operatorname{int}(L_0^{(2)})\cap\operatorname{int}(L)\), nonempty
and open. Consider bad points \(y\in U_2\) for which \([x,y]\) meets
\(\Sigma\); since \([x,y]\subseteq\cX\) by convexity, any such meeting point
lies in \(\Sigma\cap\cX\), which is bounded (unlike \(\Sigma\) itself, whose
hyperplane-intersection part is unbounded for \(d\ge3\)) and still has
\(\dim_{\mathcal H}\le d-2\). If \([x,y]\) meets \(\Sigma\cap\cX\) at \(p\),
then \(y=x+s(p-x)\) for some \(s\in[1,\infty)\), so the bad set lies in the
image of \((\Sigma\cap\cX)\times[1,\infty)\) under
\((p,s)\mapsto x+s(p-x)\). This map is Lipschitz on each bounded piece
\((\Sigma\cap\cX)\times[1,n]\) (bounded because \(\Sigma\cap\cX\subseteq\cX\)
is), with constant depending on \(n\) and \(\operatorname{diam}(\cX)\), so
each piece's image has Hausdorff dimension at most \((d-2)+1=d-1\) by the
product formula for a factor of finite upper box dimension (here \([1,n]\)
has upper box dimension \(1\); the naive additive bound is false in general
but valid here). The full image, a countable union of such pieces, thus has
dimension at most \(d-1<d\), hence Lebesgue measure zero; since \(U_2\) has
positive measure, some \(y\in U_2\) has \([x,y]\cap\Sigma=\emptyset\); fix
such a \(y\).

The segment \(\pi(t)=(1-t)x+ty\), \(0\le t\le1\), lies in
\(\operatorname{int}(L)\) and meets \(\Sigma\) nowhere; since \(x\) is off
every split hyperplane, every crossing of \(\pi\) with \(\cF_0\) is
transversal. At any crossing \(p=\pi(t_i)\), \(p\in\cF_0\) and
\(\pi\subseteq\operatorname{int}\cX\), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-hyperplane}
\(p\) lies on some split hyperplane, and on exactly one since \(p\notin\Sigma\);
call it \(H=\{x_q=c\}\). Since the finitely many other split hyperplanes
and other faces are at positive distance from \(p\), and
\(\operatorname{dist}(p,\Sigma)>0\) (\(\Sigma\) closed, \(p\notin\Sigma\))
and \(\operatorname{dist}(p,\partial\cX)>0\) (\(p\in\operatorname{int}\cX\)),
there is \(r>0\) smaller than all of these with
\(B(p,r)\subseteq\operatorname{int}\cX\) meeting \(\Sigma\) nowhere and no
face other than the one lying locally in \(H\); every point of
\(B(p,r)\cap\cF_0\) then lies on exactly one split hyperplane and no
relative boundary, so lies in that face's relative interior, giving
\(B(p,r)\cap\cF_0\subseteq H\). Hence the two open half-balls of
\(B(p,r)\setminus H\) meet no boundary and each lies in a single leaf's
interior --- \(A_{i-1}\) before \(p\) along \(\pi\), \(A_i\) after. Each
point of \(B(p,r)\cap H\) lies in the closure of both open half-balls,
hence in \(\overline{A_{i-1}}\cap\overline{A_i}=:F\); so \(F\) contains the
nonempty relatively open \((d-1)\)-dimensional set \(B(p,r)\cap H\), and
\(A_{i-1}\ne A_i\) are face-adjacent.

Because \(\pi\) avoids \(\Sigma\), no crossing lies on two hyperplanes at
once, so crossings occur at finitely many well-separated
\(0<t_1<\cdots<t_m<1\) (finite since \(\pi\) is bounded and each hyperplane
is hit at most once by a line not contained in it), and \(\pi\) visits a
finite chain \(A_0=L_0^{(1)},A_1,\ldots,A_m=L_0^{(2)}\) of face-adjacent
\(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaves in order, each meeting \(\operatorname{int}(L)\)
in a nonempty open set. Also \(m\ge1\): otherwise \(\pi([0,1])\) would lie
in a single leaf interior, forcing \(L_0^{(1)}=L_0^{(2)}\), a contradiction.
Since \(A_i,A_{i+1}\) are face-adjacent, (Reduced) for \(\tau^{\dagger}\)
gives \(v_{A_i}\ne v_{A_{i+1}}\) for every \(i\). But (S) gives \(v_1\) with
\(\gamma_0=v_1\) on \(\operatorname{int}(L)\), and each \(A_i\) meets
\(\operatorname{int}(L)\) in a nonempty open set (established above), so
choosing \(z_i\in\operatorname{int}(A_i)\cap\operatorname{int}(L)\)
gives \(v_{A_i}=\gamma_0(z_i)=v_1\) for every \(i\), so
\(v_{A_0}=v_1=v_{A_1}\), contradicting \(v_{A_0}\ne v_{A_1}\). This proves
\((\star)\); the argument used (Reduced) and (Conn), but neither minimality
of \(\tau\) nor (Dens).

\paragraph*{Step 2: general refinement.}
For \(L\in\cP(\tau)\), let \(\phi(L)\) be the unique \(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaf
whose interior meets \(\operatorname{int}(L)\). By \((\star)\),
\(\operatorname{int}(L)\subseteq\phi(L)\cup\cF_0\), and
\(L\setminus\operatorname{int}(L)\) is contained in a finite union of sets
of Hausdorff dimension at most \(d-1\) (the box facets of \(L\)'s defining
rectangle, together with its intersection with \(\partial\cX\) if any,
which has dimension \(d-1\) whether or not \(\cX\) is a box). Combined with
\(L\setminus\phi(L)\subseteq(L\setminus\operatorname{int}(L))\cup\cF_0\)
and the dimension bounds just recalled, \(L\setminus\phi(L)\) is a finite
union of sets of Hausdorff dimension at most \(d-1\), hence Lebesgue-null,
hence \(\nu(L\setminus\phi(L))=0\) by the mutual absolute continuity of
\(\nu\) and Lebesgue measure assumed in Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}.
Since \(\tau\) was
arbitrary, every \(\tau\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\) refines
\(\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) modulo \(\nu\) (no reverse refinement is asserted
for a nonminimal \(\tau\)).

Distinct \(\tau^{\dagger}\)-leaves \(A,A''\) have \(\nu\)-null intersection:
applying the lowest-common-ancestor argument of Step~1 to the pair directly
(not only to face-adjacent pairs) shows \(\overline A,\overline{A''}\) lie
in the two opposite closed half-spaces of a single hyperplane \(H''\), so
\(\overline A\cap\overline{A''}\subseteq H''\), so has Hausdorff dimension
at most \(d-1\); a fortiori so does \(A\cap A''\), hence it is \(\nu\)-null
by the same mutual absolute continuity.

\paragraph*{Step 3: surjectivity of the leaf map.}
Fix \(A\in\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\); by hypothesis (2) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}, \(\nu(A)\ge q_{\min}>0\). The
finite \(\tau\)-partition covers \(\cX\), so
\(A=\bigcup_{L\in\cP(\tau)}(A\cap L)\). If no \(L\) had \(\phi(L)=A\), then
for every \(L\), \(\phi(L)\ne A\), so \(\nu(A\cap\phi(L))=0\) by the
null-intersection fact, and with \(\nu(L\setminus\phi(L))=0\) and
subadditivity, \(\nu(A\cap L)\le\nu(A\cap\phi(L))+\nu(A\cap(L\setminus\phi(L)))=0\)
for every \(L\); summing over the finitely many \(L\) would give
\(\nu(A)=0\), contradicting \(\nu(A)\ge q_{\min}>0\). Hence \(\phi\) is
surjective, so \(\card\tau\ge L^\star\) (a surjection from
\(\cP(\tau)\) onto the \(L^\star\)-element set
\(\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\)), and \(\tau^{\dagger}\) is a coarsest realising
partition.

\paragraph*{Step 4: minimal elements.}
If \(\tau\) is minimal, \(\card\tau=\card{\tau^{\dagger}}=L^\star\), so the
surjective \(\phi:\cP(\tau)\to\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) is a bijection. For
\(A\in\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\), let \(L_A=\phi^{-1}(A)\); refinement gives
\(\nu(L_A\setminus A)=0\). If \(\nu(A\setminus L_A)>0\), some \(L'\ne L_A\)
covering part of \(A\setminus L_A\) would have \(\nu(A\cap L')>0\), yet
\(\phi(L')\ne A\) gives \(\nu(A\cap\phi(L'))=0\) as before, so
\(\nu(A\cap L')\le\nu(A\cap\phi(L'))+\nu(A\cap(L'\setminus\phi(L')))=0\), a
contradiction; hence \(\nu(A\setminus L_A)=0\) too, and
\(\nu(A\triangle L_A)=0\), giving \(\cP(\tau)\equiv_\nu\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\).
Conversely \(\cP(\tau)\equiv_\nu\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) gives
\(\card\tau=L^\star\), i.e.\ minimality. Finally,
Lemma~\ref{lem:cell-closure} applied to every matched pair gives
\(\overline{L_A}=\overline A\), so all minimal realisations have identical
leaf closures, boundary faces, boundary set, and threshold locations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-transfer}]
Let \(L\in\cP(\tau)\) be matched to \(A\in\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) under the
bijection of Theorem~\ref{thm:true-partition}, conclusions (2)--(3).
Conclusion (3) gives \(\overline L=\overline A\); since face-adjacency and
the resulting \((d-1)\)-faces of a partition are determined entirely by the
leaf closures, this yields an identical face-adjacency graph on
\(\cP(\tau)\) and \(\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) under the matching, so the
leaf-level clause of (Reduced) transfers. Since \(L\) and \(A\) are
full-dimensional convex sets with \(\overline L=\overline A\),
\(\operatorname{int}(L)=\operatorname{int}(A)\ne\emptyset\); fixing any
point there, the pointwise reading of (S)
(\S\ref{sec:setup-uniqueness}) gives \(v_L=v_A\), so leaf population values
transfer. Finally, \(\cP(\tau)\equiv_\nu\cP(\tau^{\dagger})\) gives
\(\nu(L\triangle A)=0\), hence \(\nu(L)=\nu(A)\), so the leaf-level clause
of (Node) transfers.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:canonical-welldefined}]
\emph{Proof strategy.} Maintain the invariant that the current partial tree
has at least one admissible completion in \(\mathfrak T(\cP^\star)\), and
that every unresolved node's region \(R\) satisfies
\(R\cap\cX=\bigcup\cA\) exactly, for its assigned target cells
\(\cA\subseteq\cP^\star\). Feasibility gives a valid next split;
finiteness of the boundary-face set gives a lexicographically least choice;
restricting to admissible completions preserves the leaf and depth budgets.

Both invariants hold initially: \(\mathfrak T(\cP^\star)\ne\emptyset\), and
at the root \(R=\cX\), \(\cA=\cP^\star\), so
\(R\cap\cX=\cX=\bigcup\cP^\star\) exactly, since \(\cP^\star\) is a genuine
partition of \(\cX\). The region invariant is preserved by every split,
since a split satisfying (i) assigns each cell of \(\cA\) entirely to one
child.

At an unresolved node with more than one target leaf, any admissible
completion --- a non-degenerate tree --- must split the node, and its split test
separates two nonempty groups without cutting any target cell's interior
and admits the completion's remainder; hence at least one feasible split
exists.

Every feasible threshold \(c\) that is lexicographically least for a fixed
\(q\) coincides with a recorded boundary coordinate of \(\cA\) along axis
\(q\) (the finite set \(\{m_A,M_A:A\in\cA\}\) of extents). Suppose \((q,c)\)
feasible with \(c\) not among them; by condition (ii), \(c\) lies strictly
between two consecutive recorded coordinates
\(c_{\mathrm{lo}}<c<c_{\mathrm{hi}}\), with
\(c_{\mathrm{lo}}:=\max\{\text{recorded}<c\}\) and
\(c_{\mathrm{hi}}:=\min\{\text{recorded}>c\}\) both well defined, since if
\(c\) were at or below the smallest recorded coordinate every cell would
lie in \(\{x_q\ge c\}\), and if at or above the largest every cell would lie
in \(\{x_q\le c\}\) --- either way violating (ii). No cell can have
\(m_A\le c_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(M_A\ge c_{\mathrm{hi}}\), since that would
give \(m_A<c<M_A\), cutting its interior and violating (i) for the feasible
\((q,c)\); and since \(m_A,M_A\) are themselves recorded coordinates,
consecutiveness forbids either from lying strictly inside
\((c_{\mathrm{lo}},c_{\mathrm{hi}})\); so every cell lies entirely on one
side of
\([c_{\mathrm{lo}},c_{\mathrm{hi}}]\), and by the region invariant the open
slab \(\{c_{\mathrm{lo}}<x_q<c_{\mathrm{hi}}\}\cap R\cap\cX\) is exactly
empty. Hence splitting at \(c_{\mathrm{lo}}\) assigns every cell identically
to splitting at \(c\), satisfying (i)--(ii) identically, and induces the
identical partition of \(R\cap\cX\) --- the symmetric difference of
\(\{x_q\le c\}\cap R\cap\cX\) and \(\{x_q\le c_{\mathrm{lo}}\}\cap R\cap\cX\)
lies in the now-empty slab --- hence identical children regions
intersected with \(\cX\), so the remainder of any completion admissible for
\((q,c)\) reattaches verbatim below \((q,c_{\mathrm{lo}})\), with the same
leaf cells and the same depth; verifying (iii) too.
Since \(c_{\mathrm{lo}}<c\), \((q,c_{\mathrm{lo}})<_{\mathrm{lex}}(q,c)\),
contradicting leastness of \(c\). Hence the least feasible \(c\) for each
\(q\) is always a recorded coordinate --- though a continuum of
non-recorded, non-lex-minimal feasible thresholds can still lie in a gap,
so this does not make the full feasible set finite. It does mean the
lexicographically least feasible pair, if one exists, lies in the finite
set \(\cC\) of pairs \((q,c)\) with \(c\) a recorded boundary coordinate of
\(\cA\) (a subset of the analogous finite set for \(\cP^\star\)): every
feasible pair outside \(\cC\) is lex-dominated by one inside, so the
lexicographic infimum over all feasible pairs equals that over
\(\cC\cap\{\text{feasible}\}\), nonempty and finite, giving existence and
uniqueness of the lexicographically least feasible pair.

Choosing that split preserves the invariant, and each split increases the
terminal-node count by one; since every admissible completion has exactly
\(L^\star\) leaves, no compatible partial tree exceeds \(L^\star-1\)
internal nodes, so the procedure terminates within \(L^\star-1\) splits,
with final leaves exactly the cells of \(\cP^\star\), so
\(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}}\) realises \(\gamma_0\) on the minimal
partition, \(L^\star\le\bar L\), and (respecting the depth budget at every
stage) \(\operatorname{depth}(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}})\le\bar D\); hence
\(\tau^\star_{\mathrm{can}}\in\cS^{\mathrm{cont}}\). Determinism of the
traversal and lexicographic choice proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We first lower-bound the approximation loss caused by omitting one true
boundary, using pairs of points that differ only in the splitting coordinate.
We then construct a feasible grid-aligned comparator by snapping every true
boundary to a neighbouring cutpoint.  Subtracting the comparator's
\(O(r^{-1})\) approximation loss gives the stated margin.

Fix an omitted true boundary \(b=(q,c_{0,N})\) in the true node \(R_N\), and
write
\[
  h_r:=\frac{C}{2r}.
\]
For each point \(z\) on an active \((p-1)\)-dimensional face cell and each
\(t\in(0,h_r]\), define
\[
  x^-(z,t):=(z,c_{0,N}-t),
  \qquad
  x^+(z,t):=(z,c_{0,N}+t),
\]
with the splitting coordinate inserted in the \(q\)-th position.
By the choice of \(r_0\), these points lie in the true node's local
neighbourhood.  Clause (Dens) gives
\[
  f_{\nu,N}\{x^-(z,t)\}\ge f_{\min},
  \qquad
  f_{\nu,N}\{x^+(z,t)\}\ge f_{\min}
\]
in density notation.  In particular, both sides belong to the support; the
disjoint-support counterexample is therefore excluded.

The two points have identical coordinates other than \(q\).  Hence no split
on another coordinate can separate them.  Because \(\tau\) omits \(b\), no
\(q\)-split of \(\tau\) lies between \(c_{0,N}-h_r\) and
\(c_{0,N}+h_r\) --- this being exactly what omission asserts, whether
\(\tau\) carries no \(q\)-split at all or one placed farther away
(Remark~\ref{rem:omission-is-localisation}).  Thus \(x^-(z,t)\) and
\(x^+(z,t)\) lie in the same leaf of \(\tau\).

Let \(g_\tau\) be any function constant on that leaf.  By (Local-Jump),
\[
  \abs{\gamma_0\{x^+(z,t)\}
       -\gamma_0\{x^-(z,t)\}}
  \ge\kappa_{\min}.
\]
The triangle inequality implies
\[
  \kappa_{\min}
  \le
  \abs{\gamma_0(x^+)-g_\tau(x^+)}
  +
  \abs{g_\tau(x^-)-\gamma_0(x^-)}.
\]
Therefore at least one summand is at least \(\kappa_{\min}/2\), and hence
\[
  \{\gamma_0(x^+)-g_\tau(x^+)\}^2
  +
  \{\gamma_0(x^-)-g_\tau(x^-)\}^2
  \ge \frac{\kappa_{\min}^2}{4}.
\]
Integrating over \(t\in(0,h_r]\), over all active face cells, and using
(Dens), (Node), and the cross-sectional lower bound gives
\begin{align*}
  R(\tau)-R(\gamma_0)
  &\ge
  \frac{\kappa_{\min}^2}{4}
  f_{\min}
  \bigl(c_{\mathrm{box}}q_{\min}\bigr)h_r\\
  &=
  \frac{c_{\mathrm{box}}}{8}
  \kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}\frac{C}{r}\\
  &=
  c_{\mathrm{topol}}\kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}\frac{C}{r}.
\end{align*}
Grid cells that meet the boundary of the fixed face patch can be discarded.
Their partial-overlap volume is \(o(r^{-1})\), because the face patch has
fixed positive \((p-1)\)-dimensional measure and the grid mesh tends to zero.
After increasing \(r_0\), the displayed bound holds with \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\)
replaced by \(c_{\mathrm{box}}/2\), i.e.\ with \(c_{\mathrm{topol}}\) above
replaced by \(c_{\mathrm{topol}}/2\) in the final display, since
\(c_{\mathrm{topol}}:=c_{\mathrm{box}}/8\) is linear in \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\);
\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice}'s choice of \(C\) is made to accommodate this
factor of \(2\) loss, so no circularity arises from fixing \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\) net of
this discard loss before choosing \(C\).

It remains to compare this loss with the best grid tree.  Snap every true
boundary to a nearest grid cutpoint while preserving the true ancestor order
--- the comparator \(\tau_r^\circ\) of Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}.
The resulting tree has at most \(\bar L\) leaves, so it lies in
\(\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)\) itself; no inflated budget is needed for it, which is
why the restatement of this lemma at the true budget requires no change to
the argument.  Each snapped
boundary disagrees with the truth only in a slab of width \(O(r^{-1})\).
By (J) and the upper density bound in (Dens), the loss contributed by one
such slab is at most
\(\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}/r\), after absorbing the fixed quasi-uniformity
constant \(\bar Q\) into \(f_{\max}\).  Since a tree with at most \(\bar L\) leaves has
at most \(\bar L-1\) boundaries,
\[
  \min_{\sigma\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_r)}R(\sigma)
  -
  R(\gamma_0)
  \le
  \frac{(\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}}{r}.
\]
Subtracting this upper bound from the omitted-boundary lower bound (using
the halved \(c_{\mathrm{topol}}/2\) from the discard adjustment above) yields
\[
  S_r(\tau)
  \ge
  \frac{
    \frac12 c_{\mathrm{topol}}\kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}C
    -
    (\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}^2f_{\max}
  }{r}
  =
  \frac{c_\Delta}{r}.
\]
Condition~\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice} implies not only \(c_\Delta>0\), but
\[
  c_\Delta
  >
  \frac14c_{\mathrm{topol}}\kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}C.
\]
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We first reduce the \(\nu\)-weighted risk excess to a purely
Lebesgue-geometric discrepancy, using the global density floor. We then show
that a candidate achieving \emph{zero} discrepancy must refine the true
partition, and hence must structurally match it. Finally we upgrade this
pointwise positivity to a uniform positive constant by a compactness argument
over the finitely many templates, the delicate point being continuity of the
discrepancy functional at the degenerate boundary of the parameter space.

\emph{Step 1: risk decomposition and reduction to Lebesgue geometry.}
Write \(Z=\gamma_0(X)+\varepsilon\) with \(\E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0\). For
every \(g\in L_2(\nu)\), squared-error loss gives
\[
  \E\bigl[(Z-g(X))^2\bigr]
  =
  \E\bigl[(Z-\gamma_0(X))^2\bigr]
  +\E\bigl[(\gamma_0(X)-g(X))^2\bigr],
\]
the cross term vanishing by the conditional-mean-zero property. Hence, writing
\(\cG(\tau)\) for the functions constant on the leaves of \(\tau\),
\[
  R(\tau)-R^\star
  =
  \inf_{g\in\cG(\tau)}\int_\cX\{\gamma_0(x)-g(x)\}^2f(x)\,dx
  \ \ge\
  f_{\min}\inf_{g\in\cG(\tau)}\int_\cX\{\gamma_0-g\}^2\,dx,
\]
using \(f\ge f_{\min}\) Lebesgue-a.e.\ on \(\cX\), which is
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density}. Define therefore
\[
  D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau)
  :=
  \inf_{g\in\cG(\tau)}\int_\cX\{\gamma_0-g\}^2\,d\lambda,
  \qquad
  R(\tau)-R^\star\ \ge\ f_{\min}D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau),
\]
\(\lambda\) denoting Lebesgue measure on \(\cX\). The essential feature of
\(D_\lambda\) is that it is a pure Lebesgue-geometric quantity: it does not
depend on the shape of \(f\) at all, only the constant \(f_{\min}\) surviving
from the design. In particular no compactness of a density class is needed at
this or any later step, and the bound cannot degrade as the design becomes
more strongly correlated.

\emph{Step 2: the weighted-variance mechanism.}
Let \(C\) be a candidate leaf and \(L,L'\) two true leaves adjacent across a
relatively open codimension-one face, with values \(m_L,m_{L'}\) satisfying
\(\abs{m_L-m_{L'}}\ge\kappa_{\min}\) by
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(Reduced). Put \(a=\lambda(C\cap L)\) and
\(b=\lambda(C\cap L')\). Completing the square in \(c\) gives the exact
identity
\[
  \inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{(C\cap L)\cup(C\cap L')}(\gamma_0-c)^2\,d\lambda
  =
  \frac{ab}{a+b}\,(m_L-m_{L'})^2
  \ \ge\
  \kappa_{\min}^2\,\frac{ab}{a+b}.
\]
Thus any positive-volume mixing of two adjacent true leaves inside one
candidate leaf creates strictly positive geometric error, quantitatively in
the two overlap volumes.

\emph{Step 3: zero geometric error forces refinement.}
We prove the following.

\emph{Refinement lemma.} Let \(\mathcal{P}_0\) be a finite rectangular partition on
whose cells \(\gamma_0\) is constant, and suppose \(\mathcal{P}_0\) is
\emph{reduced}: any two of its cells sharing a relatively open
codimension-one face carry values differing by at least \(\kappa_{\min}\). If
a finite rectangular partition \(\mathcal{Q}\) has leafwise-constant best Lebesgue-%
\(L_2\) approximation error to \(\gamma_0\) exactly zero, then every
positive-volume \(C\in\mathcal{Q}\) is contained, modulo null sets, in a single member
of \(\mathcal{P}_0\).

\emph{Proof.} Suppose some \(C\in\mathcal{Q}\) meets the interiors of two distinct
cells of \(\mathcal{P}_0\) in sets of positive Lebesgue measure. Choose Lebesgue
density points \(x,y\) of those two intersections. Since \(C\) is a rectangle,
hence convex, the segment \([x,y]\) lies in \(C\). Perturb the segment by an
arbitrarily small amount so that it avoids every codimension-two intersection
of boundaries of cells of \(\mathcal{P}_0\) --- possible because those intersections
form a Lebesgue-null, finite union of codimension-two sets, and the endpoints
may be moved within the density-point neighbourhoods without leaving \(C\).
The perturbed segment then crosses a finite sequence of cells of \(\mathcal{P}_0\),
passing between consecutive ones through relatively open codimension-one
faces. Since its endpoints lie in different cells, at least one crossing
occurs; at that crossing the two adjacent cells' values differ by at least
\(\kappa_{\min}\) by reducedness, and both cells meet \(C\) in
positive-Lebesgue-measure subsets (each contains a neighbourhood of a point of
the perturbed segment interior to \(C\)). A function constant on \(C\) cannot
equal two values differing by \(\kappa_{\min}\) on two positive-measure
subsets of \(C\), contradicting a.e.\ equality with \(\gamma_0\). \(\square\)

The hypotheses of the refinement lemma are met with \(\mathcal{P}_0\) the true
partition: its cells are rectangles on which \(\gamma_0\) is constant by
Assumption~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}(S), and it is reduced by
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}(Reduced) --- which is exactly why that
clause is stated for \emph{geometrically} adjacent pairs rather than only for
pairs adjacent across a true split's own face
(Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-independent}).

Now suppose \(D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau)=0\) for a candidate \(\tau\) with at
most \(s_0\) splits. Since \(\tau\) has finitely many leaves, the infimum
defining \(D_\lambda\) is attained at the leafwise Lebesgue averages, so zero
error means the candidate's leafwise-constant function equals \(\gamma_0\)
Lebesgue-a.e. By the refinement lemma, \(\tau\)'s partition refines the true
one. The true partition has exactly \(s_0+1\) positive-volume cells, and a
refinement of an \((s_0+1)\)-cell partition needs at least \(s_0+1\) cells;
but a candidate with at most \(s_0\) splits has at most \(s_0+1\)
positive-volume leaves. Hence \(\tau\) has exactly \(s_0+1\) positive-volume
leaves, each true cell contains exactly one of them, and the two partitions
agree modulo null sets. That realised equality is itself a non-degenerate
witness in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}, so \(\tau\)
structurally matches \(\tau_0\). Contrapositively,
\[
  \tau\text{ does not structurally match }\tau_0
  \ \Longrightarrow\
  D_\lambda(\gamma_0,\tau)>0 ,
\]
pointwise in \(\tau\). Note this also disposes of candidates with strictly
fewer than \(s_0\) splits: they have at most \(s_0\) positive-volume leaves
and so cannot refine an \((s_0+1)\)-cell partition at all.

\emph{Step 4: compactness gives uniform positivity.}
Pointwise positivity is not enough; a uniform constant is needed. Enumerate
the objects. There are finitely many rooted binary tree shapes with at most
\(\bar L\) leaves, and finitely many assignments of one of \(p\) coordinates
to each internal node, \(p\) and \(\bar L\) being fixed by
Assumptions~\ref{ass:budget} and~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}; so there are
finitely many templates, both for the truth and for candidates. Fix one
template \(T\). Its threshold-parameter space is
\(\prod_{\text{internal }N}[a_{j(N)},b_{j(N)}]\), with the recursive splitting
read using \emph{weak} inequalities, and is compact by Heine--Borel. This
compactification deliberately \emph{includes} thresholds at the support
boundary, coincident thresholds, and consequently zero-volume leaves; those
are precisely the limits at which a naive argument would fail, so they must be
in the space rather than excluded from it.

The admissible true-tree parameter set is likewise compact. Its threshold
constraints --- pairwise separation at least \(\varsigma\) and distance at
least \(\varsigma\) from the support boundary, by
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep) --- are closed conditions. No true cell
can degenerate in a limit: clause (Node) gives every true node \(\nu\)-mass at
least \(q_{\min}\), and the density ceiling of
Assumption~\ref{ass:global-density} converts this into the volume floor
\(\lambda(A)\ge q_{\min}/f_{\max}>0\). The true value vectors are also
confined to a compact set: fixing one true leaf's value to zero (the risk
excess is invariant to a common shift only in the sense needed here, since
\(\gamma_0\) is determined up to nothing --- rather, we use that the
adjacency graph on the at most \(\bar L\) true leaves is connected and each
edge carries a value gap at most \(\kappa_{\max}\) by
Assumption~\ref{ass:jump-regularity}), every normalised leaf value is bounded
by \((\bar L-1)\kappa_{\max}\).

Let \(\Phi(\theta_0,m,\theta)\) denote the geometric error
\(D_\lambda(\gamma_{\theta_0,m},\tau_{T,\theta})\), as a function of the true
template's thresholds \(\theta_0\) and values \(m\) and the candidate
template's thresholds \(\theta\). Then \(\Phi\) is \emph{continuous} on the
compactified parameter space, including at its degenerate points. To see this,
write
\[
  \Phi
  =
  \int_\cX\gamma_{\theta_0,m}^2\,d\lambda
  -
  \sum_{C}\frac{\bigl(\int_C\gamma_{\theta_0,m}\,d\lambda\bigr)^2}
                {\lambda(C)},
\]
the sum over the candidate's leaves, with a summand defined as \(0\) when
\(\lambda(C)=0\). Rectangle volumes and volumes of intersections of rectangles
are continuous in the defining endpoints, and each summand's magnitude is at
most \(\|\gamma_{\theta_0,m}\|_\infty^2\lambda(C)\) by Cauchy--Schwarz, which
tends to \(0\) as \(\lambda(C)\to0\); so a leaf collapsing to zero volume
contributes continuously, and the whole expression is continuous where a
naive treatment would leave a removable-looking but genuine discontinuity.
This verification is the step that would otherwise silently break the
argument.

Let \(\mathfrak W\) be the finite set of pairs \((T_0,T)\) of templates for
which \(T\) is \emph{not} structurally equivalent to \(T_0\)
(Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}), and put
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}
  :=
  \min_{(T_0,T)\in\mathfrak W}\ \min_{\text{admissible }(\theta_0,m,\theta)}
  \Phi(\theta_0,m,\theta).
\]
By continuity and compactness the inner minima are attained, and the outer
minimum is over a finite set, so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}\) is attained. Were
it \(0\), Step 3 applied at a minimising configuration would force the
candidate template to be structurally equivalent to the true one, contradicting
\((T_0,T)\in\mathfrak W\). (Step 3's conclusion also delivers exactly the
non-degeneracy that Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching}'s witnesses
require: a configuration with exactly \(s_0+1\) \emph{positive-volume} leaves
realising the true partition cannot be a degenerate collapse.) Hence
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}>0\), and setting
\[
  c_{\mathrm{top}}:=f_{\min}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{geom}}>0
\]
gives \(R(\tau)-R^\star\ge c_{\mathrm{top}}\) for every structurally
mismatched \(\tau\) with at most \(s_0\) splits, by Step 1. The constant
depends only on \(\cX\), \(f_{\min}\), \(f_{\max}\), \(\kappa_{\min}\),
\(\kappa_{\max}\), \(\varsigma\), \(q_{\min}\), \(\bar L\), \(\bar D\), and
\(p\), all fixed in \(n\) and in \(r\).

\emph{Step 5: the coarsening sub-case, explicitly.}
For the special case in which one candidate leaf contains two adjacent true
leaves \(L,L'\) outright, with \(\nu\)-masses \(a,b\ge q_{\min}\), Step 2
applies directly and, since \(ab/(a+b)\) is increasing in each argument,
\[
  R(\tau)-R^\star
  \ \ge\
  \kappa_{\min}^2\frac{ab}{a+b}
  \ \ge\
  \frac{q_{\min}\kappa_{\min}^2}{2},
\]
an explicit bound not requiring the compactness argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:uep-second-moment}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We reduce the supremum to a supremum of the \emph{leaf-refit gain} functional,
which exactly cancels the heavy-tailed \(Z^2\) term, decompose that
functional's error into a linear part and a quadratic remainder, control each
by VC-type entropy rather than by a union bound over trees, and combine with
Markov's inequality on an event where every empirical leaf mass is bounded
below.

Throughout, \(\tau\) ranges over
\(\cT^{\mathrm{pop}}_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n};q_n)\), and \(A\) over the leaves of
such trees. Write, for a leaf \(A\),
\[
  q_A:=P\mathbf 1_A,
  \qquad
  s_A:=P(Z\mathbf 1_A),
  \qquad
  \mu_A:=s_A/q_A,
\]
with \(\hat q_A,\hat s_A\) the empirical analogues, and note
\(\abs{\mu_A}\le\sqrt{C_Z}=:B\) by conditional Jensen and
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. With \(G(\tau):=\sum_{A\in\tau}s_A^2/q_A\) and
\(\hat G(\tau):=\sum_{A\in\tau}\hat s_A^2/\hat q_A\) (a summand read as \(0\)
when \(\hat q_A=0\)), the leaf-refit risks are
\[
  R(\tau)=PZ^2-G(\tau),
  \qquad
  R_n(\tau)=P_nZ^2-\hat G(\tau).
\]

\emph{Step 1: the heavy-tailed term cancels.}
The term \(P_nZ^2\) appears in \(R_n(\tau)\) and in \(R_n(\tau_n^\circ)\)
identically, and \(PZ^2\) likewise in the population risks, so it cancels
exactly in the difference of differences:
\[
  \Delta_n
  =
  \sup_\tau\Bigl|
    \bigl\{\hat G(\tau)-G(\tau)\bigr\}
    -\bigl\{\hat G(\tau_n^\circ)-G(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}
  \Bigr|
  \ \le\
  2\sup_\tau\bigl|\hat G(\tau)-G(\tau)\bigr| ,
\]
the supremum on the right taken over the class, which contains
\(\tau_n^\circ\) by hypothesis. This is the step that makes
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}'s bounded conditional \emph{second} moment
sufficient: no empirical process indexed by \(Z^2\) survives, so no
exponential or sub-Gaussian tail on \(Z\) is required anywhere below.

\emph{Step 2: an exact leafwise identity.}
Put \(\delta_{q,A}:=\hat q_A-q_A\) and \(\delta_{s,A}:=\hat s_A-s_A\). A
direct computation gives, whenever \(\hat q_A>0\),
\[
  \frac{\hat s_A^2}{\hat q_A}-\frac{s_A^2}{q_A}
  =
  2\mu_A\delta_{s,A}-\mu_A^2\delta_{q,A}
  +\frac{\bigl(\delta_{s,A}-\mu_A\delta_{q,A}\bigr)^2}{\hat q_A}.
\]
Summing the first two terms over the leaves of \(\tau\) yields
\((P_n-P)h_\tau\) for
\[
  h_\tau(x,z):=\sum_{A\in\tau}\bigl(2\mu_Az-\mu_A^2\bigr)\mathbf 1_A(x),
  \qquad
  \abs{h_\tau}\le2B\abs z+B^2 ,
\]
an envelope which is square-integrable, \(\E(2B\abs Z+B^2)^2\le10C_Z^2\), by
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}. The third term is the quadratic remainder, and
its numerator is \((P_n-P)\{(Z-\mu_A)\mathbf 1_A\}\).

\emph{Step 3: fixed VC-type entropy, not a union bound over trees.}
Because \(p\) and \(\bar L\) are fixed
(Assumptions~\ref{ass:budget},~\ref{ass:continuum-sparsity}), three classes of
functions are involved and each is VC-type with fixed characteristics: the
leaf indicators \(\{\mathbf 1_A\}\), the leaves of recursive axis-aligned
trees with at most \(\bar L\) leaves at fixed \(p\) forming a VC class of
bounded dimension; the linear functionals \(\{h_\tau\}\), each a fixed
linear combination of at most \(\bar L\) such indicators with coefficients in
a fixed compact range; and \(\{(z-b)\mathbf 1_A:\abs b\le B\}\). For each
class \(\mathcal{H}\) with envelope \(F\) there are fixed \(A_0,v\) with
\[
  \sup_Q N\bigl(\epsilon\|F\|_{Q,2},\mathcal{H},L_2(Q)\bigr)
  \le(A_0/\epsilon)^v ,
\]
so symmetrisation together with the entropy integral for VC-type classes
gives \(\E\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\abs{(P_n-P)h}^2\le CPF^2/n\). Applying this to the
three classes yields
\[
  \E Q_n^2\le\frac Cn,
  \qquad
  \E U_n^2\le\frac Cn,
  \qquad
  \E W_n^2\le\frac Cn,
\]
where \(Q_n:=\sup_A\abs{(P_n-P)\mathbf 1_A}\),
\(U_n:=\sup_\tau\abs{(P_n-P)h_\tau}\), and
\(W_n:=\sup_{A,\abs b\le B}\abs{(P_n-P)\{(z-b)\mathbf 1_A\}}\), with \(C\)
depending only on \(C_Z\), \(p\), \(\bar L\), and the VC characteristics.

It is worth recording why the entropy route is necessary rather than merely
convenient. The class \(\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})\) has cardinality
\(\Theta(r_n^{\,d})\) with \(d=O(\bar Lp)\); a Chebyshev bound plus a union
bound over its members would require \(r_n^{\,d+2}/n\to0\), which is strictly
stronger than (R1) for every \(p\ge1\). The VC argument uses the class's fixed
complexity instead of its cardinality and needs no such condition.

\emph{Step 4: combine on the good event.}
Let \(\mathcal{E}_n:=\{Q_n\le q_n/2\}\). On \(\mathcal{E}_n\), every leaf of every member of
the class has \(\hat q_A\ge q_A-q_n/2\ge q_n/2\), so the quadratic remainder
of Step 2 is bounded, summing over the at most \(\bar L\) leaves, by
\[
  \sup_\tau\sum_{A\in\tau}
  \frac{(\delta_{s,A}-\mu_A\delta_{q,A})^2}{\hat q_A}
  \ \le\
  \frac{2\bar LW_n^2}{q_n},
\]
whence \(\Delta_n\le2U_n+4\bar LW_n^2/q_n\) on \(\mathcal{E}_n\). Therefore, for
\(u>0\),
\[
  \PP(\Delta_n>u)
  \le
  \PP(\mathcal{E}_n^c)
  +\PP\bigl(U_n>u/4\bigr)
  +\PP\bigl(4\bar LW_n^2/q_n>u/2\bigr),
\]
and Markov's inequality applied to \(\E Q_n^2\), \(\E U_n^2\), and
\(\E W_n^2\) bounds the three terms by \(C/(nq_n^2)\), \(C/(nu^2)\), and
\(C/(nuq_n)\) respectively, which is the stated display.

\emph{Step 5: the consequences.}
Substituting \(u=\eta/r_n\) and \(q_n\ge c_q/r_n\) gives
\[
  \PP\bigl(\Delta_n>\eta/r_n\bigr)
  \le
  C\left\{\frac{r_n^2}{c_q^2n}+\frac{r_n^2}{\eta^2n}
    +\frac{r_n^2}{\eta c_qn}\right\}
  =
  C_\eta\frac{r_n^2}{n}
  \longrightarrow0
\]
whenever \(r_n=o(\sqrt n)\), which (R1) supplies. Taking instead
\(u_n=K\sqrt{\log r_n/n}\) and using \(q_n\gtrsim r_n^{-1}\) with
\(r_n=o(\sqrt n)\) makes each of the three terms vanish, giving
\(\Delta_n=O_p(\sqrt{\log r_n/n})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
Write \(Q_n(\tau):=R_n(\tau)+\lambda_ns(\tau)\) for the Stage-A objective and
let \(\tau_n^\circ\) be the comparator of Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}. The
feasible set \(\cT^A_n\) is partitioned into four classes: those with more
than \(s_0\) splits; those with at most \(s_0\) splits that do not
structurally match \(\tau_0\); those that match but misplace a threshold by
more than \(C/(2r_n)\); and the remainder, which is the conclusion. The first
three are excluded in turn, each against \(\tau_n^\circ\) and each by a
margin of a different order. Throughout, we work on the intersection of the
probability-tending-to-one events supplied by
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(d) --- comparator feasibility --- and by
Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain} at the relevant scale.

\emph{Step 0: the concentration events.}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and let \(q_n^\star=\alpha m_n/n\). By
Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain}, for a sequence \(u_n>0\),
\[
  \PP\bigl(\Delta^{\mathrm{emp}}_n>u_n\bigr)
  \le
  C_{\bar L,p}\bar Lr_n^{\bar L-1}e^{-m_nh(\alpha)}
  +C\left\{
    \frac{n}{\alpha^2m_n^2}+\frac1{nu_n^2}+\frac1{\alpha m_nu_n}
  \right\},
\]
and this vanishes both at \(u_n=\lambda_n/2\) and at \(u_n=1/r_n\), as
verified there under (R1)--(R5). Denote by \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(1)}\) the event
\(\{\Delta^{\mathrm{emp}}_n\le\lambda_n/2\}\) and by \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(2)}\) the event
\(\{\Delta^{\mathrm{emp}}_n\le1/r_n\}\); both have probability tending to one.
Let \(\mathcal{F}_n:=\{\tau_n^\circ\in\cT^A_n\}\), of probability tending to one by
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(d). All three are intersected below without
further comment; a minimiser over \(\cT^A_n\) satisfies
\(Q_n(\hat\tau^A_n)\le Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)\) on \(\mathcal{F}_n\), which is the only use
made of \(\mathcal{F}_n\).

\emph{Step 1: exclude \(s(\tau)>s_0\).}
Let \(\tau\in\cT^A_n\) with \(s(\tau)\ge s_0+1\). Since \(R\) is a squared-error
population risk with Bayes value \(R^\star\), \(R(\tau)\ge R^\star\); and
\(R(\tau_n^\circ)\le R^\star+C_{\mathrm{app}}/r_n\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(b). Hence
\[
  R(\tau)-R(\tau_n^\circ)\ \ge\ -\frac{C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n},
  \qquad
  \lambda_n\bigl\{s(\tau)-s(\tau_n^\circ)\bigr\}\ \ge\ \lambda_n,
\]
the second because \(s(\tau_n^\circ)=s_0\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(a). On \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(1)}\), the empirical
difference tracks the population one to within \(\lambda_n/2\), so
\[
  Q_n(\tau)-Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)
  \ \ge\
  \lambda_n-\frac{C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n}-\frac{\lambda_n}{2}
  =
  \frac{\lambda_n}{2}
  \left\{1-\frac{2C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n\lambda_n}\right\}
  \ >\ 0
\]
for all \(n\) large, since \(1/(r_n\lambda_n)\to0\) by (R3). Note the margin
and the concentration tolerance are both of order \(\lambda_n\); taking
\(u_n=\lambda_n\) rather than \(\lambda_n/2\) would leave exactly zero and
prove nothing, which is why the tolerance is a fixed \emph{fraction} of the
penalty. This is also the step for which the penalty regime is reversed
relative to a grid-exact analysis: a candidate with an extra split can
genuinely reduce the population risk, by up to
\(\Theta(1/r_n)\) --- that being the cost of representing an interior boundary
at a cutpoint --- so the penalty must exceed that gain, not fall below it.
Hence no minimiser has more than \(s_0\) splits.

\emph{Step 2: exclude structurally mismatched \(\tau\) with
\(s(\tau)\le s_0\).}
Let \(\tau\in\cT^A_n\) have \(s(\tau)\le s_0\) and fail to structurally match
\(\tau_0\). Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b} applies --- its hypotheses being among
those assumed here --- and gives \(R(\tau)-R^\star\ge c_{\mathrm{top}}\), a
constant fixed in \(n\) and \(r_n\). Combining with
Lemma~\ref{lem:snapped-oracle}(b) and bounding the penalty advantage by
\(\lambda_ns_0\),
\[
  Q_n(\tau)-Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)
  \ \ge\
  c_{\mathrm{top}}-\frac{C_{\mathrm{app}}}{r_n}
  -s_0\lambda_n-\frac{\lambda_n}{2}
  \ \longrightarrow\ c_{\mathrm{top}}\ >\ 0
\]
on \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(1)}\), using \(r_n\to\infty\) (R1) and \(\lambda_n\to0\) (R2).
Unlike Step 1, here any vanishing tolerance would do, because the margin
itself does not vanish; and Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} could \emph{not}
substitute for Theorem~\ref{thm:gtm-b}, its margin being \(\Theta(1/r_n)\),
of the same order as the comparator's own approximation error.

\emph{Step 3: threshold localisation among structurally matched \(\tau\).}
By Steps 1 and 2, with probability tending to one the minimiser has exactly
\(s_0\) splits and structurally matches \(\tau_0\); fix the induced matching
\(N\mapsto N_0\) of its nodes with the true nodes, which exists and is unique
by the equality of leaf partitions witnessed in
Definition~\ref{def:structural-matching} together with the geometric signature
of each boundary (coordinate, adjacent faces). Suppose such a \(\tau\)
satisfies \(\abs{t_{\tau,N}-t_{0,N_0}}>C/(2r_n)\) for some \(N\), \(C\) being
the constant fixed by~\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice}. Two cases are possible: no
split of \(\tau\) on that coordinate lies within \(C/(2r_n)\) of
\(t_{0,N_0}\), or some other split of \(\tau\) on that coordinate does. The
second is excluded for \(r_n\ge r_0\): two splits of \(\tau\) matched to
distinct true boundaries are separated by at least
\(\varsigma-2\cdot\)(mesh) \(>\varsigma/2>C/r_n\) by
Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep), so a split within \(C/(2r_n)\) of
\(t_{0,N_0}\) would be \(N\)'s own. Hence \(\tau\) omits the true boundary
\((q(N),t_{0,N_0})\) in the sense fixed before
Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin} --- the sense in which that lemma's proof uses
omission being exactly the absence of a \(q\)-split in the
\(C/(2r_n)\)-neighbourhood, not the absence of a \(q\)-split altogether
(Remark~\ref{rem:omission-is-localisation}). Lemma~\ref{lem:topol-margin},
read at the budget \(\bar L\), therefore gives
\[
  R(\tau)-\min_{\sigma\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})}R(\sigma)
  \ \ge\
  \frac{c_\Delta}{r_n},
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  R(\tau)-R(\tau_n^\circ)\ \ge\ \frac{c_\Delta}{r_n},
\]
the second inequality because \(\tau_n^\circ\in\cT_{\bar L}(\cA_{r_n})\) so
that \(R(\tau_n^\circ)\ge\min_\sigma R(\sigma)\). Since both \(\tau\) and
\(\tau_n^\circ\) carry \(s_0\) splits, the penalties cancel exactly, and on
\(\mathcal{G}_n^{(2)}\) with tolerance \(u_n=1/r_n\),
\[
  Q_n(\tau)-Q_n(\tau_n^\circ)
  \ \ge\
  \frac{c_\Delta}{r_n}-\frac1{r_n}
  =
  \frac{c_\Delta-1}{r_n}
  \ >\ 0
\]
provided \(c_\Delta>1\). This is a normalisation, not a further hypothesis:
\eqref{eq:C-margin-choice} fixes \(C\) only up to being sufficiently large,
and by the display following it, \(c_\Delta>\tfrac14c_{\mathrm{topol}}
\kappa_{\min}^2f_{\min}q_{\min}C\), which exceeds any prescribed constant once
\(C\) is enlarged --- the tolerance \(u_n=1/r_n\) being available at any fixed
multiple by Remark~\ref{rem:uep-chain}, since only \(r_n^2/n\to0\) and
\(r_n/m_n\to0\) are used there. Enlarging \(C\) enlarges \(r_0\) but changes
no rate condition. Hence no minimiser misplaces a threshold by more than
\(C/(2r_n)\), and since \(C\) is fixed,
\(\max_N\abs{\hat t^A_N-t_{0,N_0}}=O_p(1/r_n)\).

\emph{Conclusion.}
On the intersection of \(\mathcal{F}_n\), \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(1)}\) and \(\mathcal{G}_n^{(2)}\) ---
whose probability tends to one --- Steps 1--3 exclude every candidate other
than those with exactly \(s_0\) splits, structurally matching \(\tau_0\), and
with every threshold within \(C/(2r_n)\) of its match. Since
\(\hat\tau^A_n\) minimises \(Q_n\) over \(\cT^A_n\) and \(\tau_n^\circ\) is
available there, \(\hat\tau^A_n\) is such a candidate, which is (i)--(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}]
\emph{Proof strategy.} Part (a) is a strict-inequality/compactness argument
using only population quantities. Part (b) upgrades it to consistency of
the empirical minimiser via a uniform law of large numbers on an arbitrary
fixed compact domain, and then identifies the minimisers over two such
domains by a nesting argument in which both sets are deterministic.

\emph{(a).} For every $c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+]$,
$R_N^X(c)-R_N^X(c_0)=\|\Pi^\nu_{\tau(c)}\gamma_0-\gamma_0\|_\nu^2\ge0$,
the population ANOVA identity, with equality if and only if $\tau(c)$ is
sufficient for $\gamma_0$ (i.e.\ leaf-constant for it). For $c\ne c_0$, the
slab $\mathcal I_N(c,c_0)$ has strictly positive $\nu$-mass by (Dens), and by
(Local-Jump) at least one of its cells carries a nonzero jump; hence
$\tau(c)$ is not sufficient and the inequality is strict for every
$c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+]\setminus\{c_0\}$. By (Dens), $R_N^X$ is continuous in
$c$ on $[q_N^-,q_N^+]$. For every fixed $\eta\in(0,q_N^+-q_N^-]$, a
strictly positive continuous function on the
compact set $\{c\in[q_N^-,q_N^+]:\abs{c-c_0}\ge\eta\}$ attains a
strictly positive infimum, giving (a).

\emph{(b).} Let $D\subseteq[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ be fixed, deterministic, compact,
and containing $c_0$ in its interior, and let $\widehat c_N^D$ minimise the
empirical criterion over $D$. The empirical criterion depends on $c$ only
through the
finitely many leaf or prism-cell counts and response sums, each of which
is a partial sum indexed by the single indicator $\mathbf 1\{X_q\le c\}$,
monotone in $c$. Uniform convergence of each such partial sum to its
population counterpart over the fixed compact $D$ follows from
the classical Glivenko--Cantelli theorem together with
Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}'s bound on the response second moment.
Combined with the separation supplied by (a) --- which holds on
$[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ and hence, restricted, on $D$ --- the standard
argmin-consistency argument for
M-estimators on a fixed compact parameter space
(\citealt{vandervaart1998}) gives
\[
  \widehat c_N^D\to_pc_0 .
\]
Nothing in this argument uses any property of $D$ beyond compactness,
determinacy, and interiority of $c_0$; in particular it applies verbatim to
$D=[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ and to $D=\overline{B^-}$.

For the final claim, write $\widehat c_N^{\mathrm{full}}$ for the minimiser
over $[q_N^-,q_N^+]$. By the display just proved,
$\PP(\widehat c_N^{\mathrm{full}}\in B^-)\to1$, since $B^-$ is a fixed
neighbourhood of $c_0$. On that event, $\widehat c_N^{\mathrm{full}}$ is a
global minimiser over $[q_N^-,q_N^+]$ that lands inside the subset
$\overline{B^-}$; a global minimiser over a set which lands inside a subset of
that set is automatically a minimiser over the subset, so
$\widehat c_N^{\mathrm{full}}$ is also a minimiser over $\overline{B^-}$,
which gives
$\PP\bigl(\widehat c_N^{\mathrm{full}}=\widehat c_N^{\overline{B^-}}\bigr)
\to1$ under the common tie-breaking convention of
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(e). Both sets here are deterministic, so
no property of Stage~A, and in particular no statement about $I_n(N)$, is
used; the corresponding step for the \emph{random} search interval is
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}]
\emph{Proof strategy.} Sandwich the random $I_n(N)$ between two deterministic
intervals centred at the truth; place the larger of them inside the fixed
$B^-$; establish, on $B^-$, that \emph{every} minimiser is confined to an
$O(1/n)$ window, by strict domination against the competitor $c_{0,N}$; then
show that window is inside the smaller deterministic interval, so that the
argmin sets over $B^-$ and over $I_n(N)$ coincide exactly.

\emph{Step 1: the deterministic sandwich.} Let
\[
  E_n:=\Bigl\{\abs{\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}}\le\rho_n/2\Bigr\}.
\]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(iii),
$\abs{\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}}=O_p(1/r_n)$, while $\rho_n/2=M_n/(2r_n)$ with
$M_n\to\infty$; hence $\PP(E_n)\to1$. Taking $E_n$ from the event
$\{Y_n\le M_n/2\}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}'s proof --- with
$Y_n=r_n\max_M\abs{\hat t^A_M-t_{0,M}}=O_p(1)$ --- gives the same conclusion
simultaneously at every node, at no extra cost. Define the \emph{non-random}
intervals
\[
  J_n^-:=\bigl[c_{0,N}-\tfrac{\rho_n}2,\,c_{0,N}+\tfrac{\rho_n}2\bigr],
  \qquad
  J_n^+:=\bigl[c_{0,N}-\tfrac{3\rho_n}2,\,c_{0,N}+\tfrac{3\rho_n}2\bigr].
\]
Write $d:=\hat t^A_N-c_{0,N}$, so that on $E_n$ we have $\abs d\le\rho_n/2$
and $I_n(N)=[c_{0,N}+d-\rho_n,\,c_{0,N}+d+\rho_n]$. Its left endpoint
satisfies
\[
  c_{0,N}-\tfrac32\rho_n
  \ \le\ c_{0,N}+d-\rho_n\ \le\
  c_{0,N}-\tfrac12\rho_n,
\]
and symmetrically the right endpoint lies between $c_{0,N}+\tfrac12\rho_n$ and
$c_{0,N}+\tfrac32\rho_n$. Hence
\[
  J_n^-\ \subseteq\ I_n(N)\ \subseteq\ J_n^+
  \qquad\text{on }E_n ,
\]
and both inclusions are attained at $\abs d=\rho_n/2$, so the constants
$\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac32$ cannot be improved.

\emph{Step 2: constant bookkeeping.} Since $\rho_n=M_n/r_n\to0$, eventually
$\rho_n<\varsigma/8$, hence $3\rho_n/2<\varsigma/4$ and
\[
  J_n^+\ \subseteq\ B^-=\bigl(c_{0,N}-\varsigma/4,\,c_{0,N}+\varsigma/4\bigr).
\]
Eventually also $3\rho_n/2<c_{\mathrm{col}}$, where $c_{\mathrm{col}}$ is the
collar half-width of Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Dens), so $J_n^+$ lies
inside the fixed density collar. Both statements are deterministic
consequences of $\rho_n\to0$ alone; neither is an application of
Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}, whose clauses concern the random
$I_n(N)$ and would be the wrong object here. Finally
$\abs{B^-}=\varsigma/2<\varsigma$, which is used in the next step.

\emph{Step 3: on the fixed interval, every minimiser is $O_p(1/n)$-confined.}
Four inputs are available on $B^-$, and each is checked to apply there
specifically.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
  \item \emph{Separation.} Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a) gives
  $\delta_N(\eta)>0$ for every $\eta$, on the box $[q_N^-,q_N^+]\supseteq
  B^-$, hence on $B^-$.
  \item \emph{Consistency.} Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(b), applied with
  $D=\overline{B^-}$, gives consistency of the minimiser over $B^-$.
  \item \emph{The prism decomposition and drift.}
  Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(i)--(iv) apply on $B^-$: their Step~2 requires
  that no candidate in the domain cross another same-coordinate split, and
  since $\abs{B^-}=\varsigma/2<\varsigma$, clause
  Assumption~\ref{ass:local-design}(Sep) excludes every other true threshold
  on coordinate $q$ from $B^-$ \emph{outright} --- a deterministic exclusion
  requiring no probabilistic input, and the reason the base radius is fixed at
  $\varsigma/4$. The drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift}, stated for small
  $\abs{c-c_0}$, extends to all of $B^-$ by taking
  $\psi(\eta):=\min\{\delta_N(\eta),\,\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}\eta/4\}$, positive
  and non-decreasing in $\eta$ by (i); the two-group analogue holds identically
  in Tier~1.
  \item \emph{The stochastic control.} Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}'s
  conditional second-moment bound is the standing hypothesis under which the
  argmax-consistency arguments of \citet{chan1993,seijosen2011} (Tier~1) and
  \citet{seootsu2018} (Tier~2) control the empirical criterion's fluctuations
  uniformly over growing windows --- the same bound
  Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} invokes for its own escape-exclusion
  step; nothing further is needed here.
\end{enumerate}
The conclusion required is stronger than tightness of a chosen selection: it
must exclude \emph{every} minimiser from the complement of an $O(1/n)$ window.
That is obtained by strict domination, not by a weak-convergence argument. The
argmax-consistency arguments cited in (iv) proceed by first showing that,
outside any fixed neighbourhood of the true threshold, the criterion exceeds
its minimising value by an amount tending to infinity with the
neighbourhood's radius, uniformly, and only then concluding that the argmin
is tight --- the tightness conclusion is a corollary of this escape-exclusion
step, not an independent fact. Applied here on the fixed $B^-$, using (i) and
(iv) for the separation and stochastic control and (iii) for the population
drift that dominates the fluctuations, this gives: for every $\epsilon>0$
there is $U<\infty$ such that, with probability at least $1-\epsilon$ for all
large $n$,
\[
  \inf_{\{c\in B^-:\abs{c-c_{0,N}}>U/n\}}
  \bigl\{S_n(c)-S_n(c_{0,N})\bigr\}\ >\ 0.
\]
Since $S_n$ is piecewise constant in $c$, with jumps only at observed values
of coordinate $q$, the largest such value $c^\star_n\le c_{0,N}$ satisfies
$S_n(c^\star_n)=S_n(c_{0,N})$; by (Dens), $\PP(c^\star_n\in B^-)\to1$ with
$\abs{c^\star_n-c_{0,N}}=O_p(1/n)$, an offset absorbed into $U$ below. Since
$c^\star_n$ is thus an available competitor achieving increment zero up to
this negligible displacement, no point of
$\{c\in B^-:\abs{c-c_{0,N}}>U/n\}$ can be a global minimiser of $S_n$ over
$B^-$. By (Dens) and the interiority half of (Sep), every leaf induced by any
$c\in B^-$ retains population mass bounded below by a fixed constant, so
with probability tending to one the observed-value candidate set in $B^-$ is
non-empty and every such leaf contains observations, making $S_n$ well
defined throughout $B^-$; $S_n$ attains its minimum on the compact
$\overline{B^-}$ by continuity, and by the displayed inequality every such
minimiser lies in the closed $U/n$-window, which is interior to $B^-$ for $n$
large. Hence $\argmin_{B^-}S_n$ is non-empty and, with probability at least
$1-\epsilon$ eventually,
\[
  \argmin_{B^-}S_n\ \subseteq\ \bigl\{c:\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\bigr\}.
\]
This uses only Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a)--(b),
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}(i)--(iv), and Assumption~\ref{ass:moments}, and
in particular nothing about $I_n(N)$ and no appeal to
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology} --- so no circularity arises with
Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}, which invokes the same
escape-exclusion mechanism independently, for its own oracle criterion
$S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}$, rather than being cited by this step.

\emph{Step 4: nesting.} Fix $\epsilon>0$ and let $U$ be as in Step 3. Let $n$
be large enough that
\[
  U\ \le\ \frac{n\rho_n}{2},
\]
which is eventually true because $n\rho_n=nM_n/r_n\to\infty$: by (R1),
$r_n=o(\sqrt{n/\log r_n})$ gives $r_n^2\log r_n=o(n)$ and hence
$n/r_n\to\infty$, while $M_n\ge1$. This is the only place
$n\rho_n\to\infty$ is used, and it is \emph{not} implied by
Definition~\ref{def:stageb-interval}'s conditions on $M_n$ alone: taking
$r_n=n^2$ and $M_n=\log n$ satisfies $M_n\to\infty$ and $M_n/r_n\to0$ yet
gives $n\rho_n=n\log n/n^2\to0$. The cross-reference to (R1) is therefore
substantive.

On $E_n$ intersected with the event of Step 3 --- of probability at least
$1-\epsilon$ for large $n$ --- every $c\in\argmin_{B^-}S_n$ satisfies
$\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\le\rho_n/2$, hence $c\in J_n^-$. By Step 1,
$J_n^-\subseteq I_n(N)$, and by Steps 1--2, $I_n(N)\subseteq J_n^+\subseteq
B^-$. Thus the \emph{entire} argmin set over the larger domain $B^-$ is
contained in the subset $I_n(N)$, which forces
\[
  \min_{I_n(N)}S_n=\min_{B^-}S_n
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \argmin_{I_n(N)}S_n=\argmin_{B^-}S_n
\]
exactly --- not merely a non-empty intersection. Consequently \emph{any}
measurable selection $\hat c_N\in\argmin_{I_n(N)}S_n$ lies in
$\argmin_{B^-}S_n$ and therefore satisfies $\abs{\hat c_N-c_{0,N}}\le U/n$.
No uniqueness of the minimiser is required, and no assumption on the law of
$Z$ beyond Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} is used; in particular the response
need not be atomlessly distributed.

\emph{Step 5: combine.} For the $U$ just fixed,
\[
  \PP\bigl(n\abs{\hat c_N-c_{0,N}}>U\bigr)
  \le
  \PP(E_n^c)
  +\PP\Bigl(\argmin_{B^-}S_n\not\subseteq
    \bigl\{\abs{c-c_{0,N}}\le U/n\bigr\}\Bigr)
  \le
  o(1)+\epsilon
\]
for all $n$ large enough (the threshold depending on $U$, via Step 4). Since
$\epsilon>0$ was arbitrary and $U=U(\epsilon)$, this is precisely
$\lim_{U\to\infty}\limsup_n\PP(n\abs{\hat c_N-c_{0,N}}>U)=0$, i.e.
$n(\hat c_N-c_{0,N})=O_p(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability}]
Each descendant threshold displacement of size \(O(1/r_n)\) alters the
\(\nu\)-mass of the affected leaf regions by \(O(1/r_n)\), by the upper
density bound of (Dens) and the bounded coordinate diameters, so (a) follows
from the boundedness of the profiled leaf means
(Assumption~\ref{ass:design}, Assumption~\ref{ass:construct}(c)). Part (b)
follows from (a) and the
strengthened Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a), for \(r_n\) large: the
perturbed infimum is within \(O(1/r_n)\) of the true one, hence exceeds
\(\delta_N(\eta)/2\) once \(1/r_n<\delta_N(\eta)/2\). Part (c) follows
because each perturbed adjacent-leaf pair differs from a true adjacent pair
only through leaf regions of \(\nu\)-mass \(O(1/r_n)\), whose profiled means
therefore differ by \(O(1/r_n)\) given the leaf-mass floor of (Node); with
\(r_n\to\infty\), (Local-Jump) transfers to the perturbed pairs with
\(\kappa_{\min}\) replaced by \(\kappa_{\min}/2\) for \(n\) large enough.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We first identify the observations whose assignment changes.  We then refine
the moving slab into finitely many cells, one for each possible source--target
leaf pair.  A weighted ANOVA identity gives an exact first-order
decomposition of the excess risk.  Finally, the local empirical criterion is
represented as a finite sum of marked change-point processes with positive
aggregate drift.

\emph{Step 1: identify the moving slab.}
Suppose first that \(c>c_0\).  If \(x_q\le c_0\), then both thresholds send
\(x\) to the left child of \(N\).  If \(x_q>c\), both send \(x\) to the right
child.  If \(c_0<x_q\le c\), the true threshold sends \(x\) right whereas the
candidate sends it left.  Therefore,
\[
  \bigl\{x\in R_N:
    \text{the side of \(N\) differs under \(c\) and \(c_0\)}
  \bigr\}
  =
  R_N\cap\{c_0<x_q\le c\}.
\]
The case \(c<c_0\) is identical with left and right reversed, proving (i).

\emph{Step 2: form the prism cells.}
The domain on which parts (iv)--(v) are applied is the FIXED interval $B^-$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}, whose width is
$\abs{B^-}=\varsigma/2<\varsigma$. By (Sep), distinct true thresholds on
coordinate \(q\) are at least \(\varsigma\) apart, so \(B^-\) contains no true
threshold on coordinate \(q\) other than \(c_0\) --- a deterministic
exclusion, requiring no probabilistic input and, in particular, no property of
Stage~A or of the search interval \(I_n(N)\). (This is exactly why the base
radius of \(B^-\) is fixed at \(\varsigma/4\).) Hence no candidate threshold
in \(B^-\) crosses another split on coordinate \(q\), so
every descendant test on coordinate \(q\) has the same outcome
throughout the slab and may be absorbed into a fixed path label.  The
remaining descendant tests are on coordinates other than \(q\), and their
outcomes are unaffected by moving \(c\).  Thus the other-coordinate values of
an observation determine exactly one leaf below the true side of \(N\) and
exactly one leaf below the candidate side.

Intersecting the corresponding side-specific leaf regions partitions the
slab into disjoint cells indexed by realised source--target pairs.  There are
at most \(L_N^-L_N^+\) such pairs.  Since
\(L_N^-+L_N^+\le\bar L\), the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
  L_N^-L_N^+
  \le
  \left(\frac{L_N^-+L_N^+}{2}\right)^2
  \le \frac{\bar L^2}{4}.
\]
This proves (ii).  By (Dens), each active cell has mass
\[
  \omega_{N,j}(c)
  =
  f_{N,j}^{\operatorname{sgn}(c-c_0)}(c_0)
  \abs{c-c_0}
  +o\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}\right),
\]
and (Local-Jump) gives
\(\kappa_{\min}\le\abs{\Delta_{N,j}}\le\kappa_{\max}\).
This proves (iii).

\emph{Step 3: derive the population excess risk.}
Consider one destination leaf.  Let its correctly assigned core have mass
\(a>0\) and population mean \(\mu_0\).  Suppose that the moving prism cells
entering this leaf have masses \(b_1,\ldots,b_s\) and means
\(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_s\).  Put
\[
  B=\sum_{j=1}^s b_j,
  \qquad
  \Delta_j=\mu_j-\mu_0.
\]
The candidate leaf mean is
\[
  \bar\mu
  =
  \frac{a\mu_0+\sum_{j=1}^s b_j\mu_j}{a+B}
  =
  \mu_0+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^s b_j\Delta_j}{a+B}.
\]
The weighted ANOVA identity states that the excess squared error from pooling
groups with masses \(w_g\) and means \(\mu_g\) equals
\(\sum_g w_g(\mu_g-\bar\mu)^2\).  Applying it here and expanding gives
\begin{align*}
  &a(\mu_0-\bar\mu)^2
    +\sum_{j=1}^s b_j(\mu_j-\bar\mu)^2
  \\
  &\quad=
    \sum_{j=1}^s b_j\Delta_j^2
    -
    \frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^s b_j\Delta_j\right)^2}{a+B}.
\end{align*}
Indeed,
\begin{align*}
  \sum_{g}w_g(\mu_g-\bar\mu)^2
  &=
  \sum_g w_g\mu_g^2-(a+B)\bar\mu^2
  \\
  &=
  a\mu_0^2+\sum_j b_j(\mu_0+\Delta_j)^2
  -
  \frac{\left\{(a+B)\mu_0+\sum_j b_j\Delta_j\right\}^2}{a+B}
  \\
  &=
  \sum_j b_j\Delta_j^2
  -
  \frac{\left(\sum_j b_j\Delta_j\right)^2}{a+B}.
\end{align*}
The destination leaf fixed at the start of this step is a leaf of the
\emph{true} partition: by the lemma's own hypothesis, the population-level
descendant cell structure below \(N\)'s two children is held at its true
configuration throughout this local expansion (only the threshold at \(N\)
itself moves), so (Node)'s leaf-mass floor applies to it directly and gives
\(a\ge q_{\min}\); in particular \(a\ge q_{\min}/2\) for all sufficiently
small \(\abs{c-c_0}\).  Moreover,
\[
  0
  \le
  \frac{\left(\sum_j b_j\Delta_j\right)^2}{a+B}
  \le
  \frac{\kappa_{\max}^2 B^2}{a+B}
  \le
  \frac{2\kappa_{\max}^2}{q_{\min}}B^2.
\]
Summing over the finitely many destination leaves, and using
\(B=O(\abs{c-c_0})\), yields
\[
  R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)-R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c_0)
  =
  \sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)\Delta_{N,j}^2
  +O\!\left(\abs{c-c_0}^2\right),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:prism-risk}.  Equivalently,
\[
  R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)-R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c_0)
  =
  \left\{\sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)\Delta_{N,j}^2\right\}
  \{1+o(1)\}.
\]

By (Dens),
\[
  \sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)
  \ge \frac{p_{\min}}{2}\abs{c-c_0}
\]
for all sufficiently small \(\abs{c-c_0}\).  Therefore,
\[
  \sum_j\omega_{N,j}(c)\Delta_{N,j}^2
  \ge
  \frac{\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}}{2}\abs{c-c_0}.
\]
Shrinking the neighbourhood once more so that the quadratic remainder is no
larger than half this leading term gives
\[
  R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c)-R_N^{\mathrm{prof}}(c_0)
  \ge
  \frac{\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}}{4}\abs{c-c_0},
\]
proving (iv).

\emph{Step 4: identify the local empirical process.}
Let \(c=c_0+u/n\), with \(u\) in a fixed compact set.  The expected number of
observations in each active prism cell is
\[
  n\omega_{N,j}(c)
  =
  \abs{u}\,
  f_{N,j}^{\operatorname{sgn}(u)}(c_0)+o(1),
\]
and is therefore of constant order.  If an observation moves from a leaf
with mean \(\mu_s\) to one with mean \(\mu_d\), write
\(Z=\mu_s+\varepsilon\), where
\(\E(\varepsilon\mid X)=0\).  Its unnormalised SSE increment is
\begin{align*}
  (Z-\mu_d)^2-(Z-\mu_s)^2
  &=
  (\varepsilon+\mu_s-\mu_d)^2-\varepsilon^2
  \\
  &=
  2\varepsilon(\mu_s-\mu_d)
  +(\mu_s-\mu_d)^2.
\end{align*}
Conditional on its prism cell, this increment has mean
\[
  \E\!\left[
    (Z-\mu_d)^2-(Z-\mu_s)^2
    \mid X
  \right]
  =
  (\mu_s-\mu_d)^2
  =
  \Delta_{N,j}^2
  \ge\kappa_{\min}^2,
\]
and finite variance by the standing conditional second-moment bound.

Consequently, the local unnormalised criterion is a finite sum of marked
rare-event counting processes, all indexed by the same scalar \(u\), with
aggregate one-sided mean drift at least
\(\kappa_{\min}^2p_{\min}\abs u/2\).  The number of component processes is
bounded by \(\bar L^2/4\), independently of \(n\).  Joint convergence follows
from the standard finite-dimensional marked point-process argument: the
disjoint prism-cell counts converge jointly to Poisson counts with their
one-sided limiting intensities, while the associated marks have finite second
moments.  Summation is a continuous linear map on this finite vector of
processes.  Thus the sum has the same \(n\)-localisation as a single
change-point process.

This is the random-design extension of the single-break mechanism in
\citet{chan1993} and \citet{seijosen2011}.  The simultaneous-break
least-squares machinery of \citet{baiperron1998} provides the corresponding
finite-dimensional profiling precedent, while \citet{yaoau1989} and
\citet{boysen2009} establish the same \(n^{-1}\) location rate when finitely
many step heights are estimated simultaneously.  Those references do not by
themselves identify the present leaf-pair sum; the prism decomposition and
joint marked-process argument above supply that missing reduction.

Step 4 is a purely LOCAL statement, valid for \(c=c_0+u/n\) with \(u\) in a
fixed compact set: on its own it bounds the criterion's behaviour near
\(c_0\) but does not rule out the empirical minimiser settling elsewhere in
\(B^-\).  That is supplied separately, and globally, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}: applying it with \(R_N^X=R_N^{\mathrm
{prof}}\) and \(D=\overline{B^-}\) gives \(\widetilde c_N\to_pc_0\) (part (b),
via the same
Glivenko--Cantelli argument used there, now applied to the profiled
criterion's finitely many leaf/cell sufficient statistics). Consistency and
the local drift bound~\eqref{eq:prism-drift} together license the standard
non-standard-rate argmax theorem for change-point processes
(\citealt{seootsu2018} give a general version covering discontinuous,
possibly localised criteria of exactly this kind): consistency confines the
search to shrinking neighbourhoods of \(c_0\), on which Step 4's local
process governs the limit.

Because the limiting two-sided process has positive linear drift away from
zero, its set of minimisers is tight.  The standard change-point argmin
argument therefore yields
\[
  \lim_{U\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
  \PP\!\left(n\abs{\widetilde c_N-c_0}>U\right)=0,
\]
which is equivalent to
\(n(\widetilde c_N-c_0)=O_p(1)\), where \(\widetilde c_N\) is the minimiser
of the profiled criterion over the FIXED interval \(B^-\) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}.  The transfer of this rate to the actual
Stage-B output --- which minimises over the random, shrinking \(I_n(N)\)
rather than over \(B^-\) --- is Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}, whose
proof reuses exactly parts (i)--(iv) above and
Lemma~\ref{lem:fixed-domain}(a)--(b), and gives
\(n(\widehat c_N-c_0)=O_p(1)\).  This proves (v).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
We localise every ancestor error to a deterministic \(M/n\) collar.  A joint
density union bound shows that, with probability tending to one, no
ancestor-contamination observation also lies in the level-\(\ell\)
\(U/n\)-window.  On that event, contaminated observations have assignments
that are constant in the local candidate threshold and perturb each profiled
cell criterion by \(o_p(1)\) on the unnormalised local-SSE scale.

\emph{Step 1: deterministic ancestor collars.}
Fix \(\epsilon>0\), and let
\(\operatorname{anc}(\ell)\) be the finite set of ancestors of the
level-\(\ell\) node.  By the induction hypothesis, there is \(M<\infty\) such
that, for every \(a\in\operatorname{anc}(\ell)\),
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    n\abs{\widehat c_a-c_{0,a}}>M
  \right)
  <
  \frac{\epsilon}
       {3\card{\operatorname{anc}(\ell)}}.
\]
Hence, by the union bound,
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    \max_{a\in\operatorname{anc}(\ell)}
    n\abs{\widehat c_a-c_{0,a}}>M
  \right)
  <\frac{\epsilon}{3}.
\]
On the complementary event, every observation whose membership differs
between true and refined ancestor conditioning lies in
\[
  \mathcal C_{n,M}
  :=
  \bigcup_{a\in\operatorname{anc}(\ell)}
  \left\{
    x:\abs{x_{q_a}-c_{0,a}}\le\frac{M}{n}
  \right\}.
\]

\emph{Step 2: exclude overlap with the descendant localisation window.}
Fix \(U<\infty\).  If \(q_a\ne q_\ell\), clause (Joint) and a bare union bound
give
\begin{align*}
  &\PP\!\left(
    \exists i:
    \abs{X_{i,q_a}-c_{0,a}}\le\frac{M}{n},
    \ 
    \abs{X_{i,q_\ell}-c_{0,\ell}}\le\frac{U}{n}
  \right)
  \\
  &\quad\le
  n\,
  \PP\!\left(
    \abs{X_{q_a}-c_{0,a}}\le\frac{M}{n},
    \ 
    \abs{X_{q_\ell}-c_{0,\ell}}\le\frac{U}{n}
  \right)
  \\
  &\quad\le
  n\left(\frac{2M}{n}\right)
       \left(\frac{2U}{n}\right)\bar f_2
  =
  \frac{4\bar f_2MU}{n}
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
A further finite union bound handles all ancestors.  If
\(q_a=q_\ell\), (Sep) gives
\(\abs{c_{0,a}-c_{0,\ell}}\ge\varsigma\); hence the two collars are disjoint
for all \(n>2(M+U)/\varsigma\), and the corresponding bad event is empty.

It follows that, with probability tending to one, every
ancestor-contamination observation lies outside
\[
  \left\{
    x:\abs{x_{q_\ell}-c_{0,\ell}}\le U/n
  \right\}.
\]
The number of observations in \(\mathcal C_{n,M}\) is \(O_p(1)\), because its
expectation is bounded uniformly in \(n\) by the marginal upper-density bound
and the fixed number of ancestors.

\emph{Step 3: bound the criterion perturbation.}
On the preceding good event, each contaminated observation enters or leaves
a specific prism cell in the level-\(\ell\) subtree, but its cell membership
does not change as \(c\) ranges over
\(c_{0,\ell}+[-U,U]/n\).  Thus it changes the count and response sum of each
affected cell by \(O_p(1)\).  By Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(f),
every leaf of the refined partition contains at least \(\rho n\)
observations with probability tending to one, so its fitted mean changes by
\[
  O_p(1)/\Theta_p(n)=O_p(n^{-1}).
\]
The level-\(\ell\) local contrast itself involves only \(O_p(1)\) observations
whose \(q_\ell\)-coordinate lies in the \(U/n\)-window.  Differentiating a
single squared residual with respect to a leaf mean gives
\[
  (Z-\mu-\varrho)^2-(Z-\mu)^2
  =
  -2\varrho(Z-\mu)+\varrho^2.
\]
With \(\varrho=O_p(n^{-1})\), a tight number of local observations, and the
standing
conditional second-moment bound, summing this display gives \(O_p(n^{-1})\).
The corresponding change in the profiled least-squares gain is also
\(O_p(n^{-1})\), by the quadratic identity used in
Remark~\ref{rem:profiling-correction}.  Therefore,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:ancestor-op}
  \sup_{\abs u\le U}
  \Big|
  &\{S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)
     -S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\}
  \nonumber\\[-2pt]
  &-
  \{S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)
     -S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\}
  \Big|
  =
  O_p(n^{-1})
  =
  o_p(1),
\end{align}
where \(S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}\) and \(S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}\) are the
unnormalised actual-row-set and oracle-row-set subtree SSEs.

The oracle local process has a tight minimiser by
Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile}. Step~3 controls the actual--oracle
discrepancy only on a fixed compact $u$-window; tightness of the actual
process additionally requires that its criterion cannot be minimised far
from $c_{0,\ell}$, which is Step~4 below.

\emph{Step 4: exclude escape from the compact window.} Only observations in
the ancestor collar $\mathcal C_{n,M}$ can contribute to the actual--oracle
discrepancy outside the $U/n$-window used in Step~3; write
\[
  \Xi_n(u)
  :=
  \{S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)-S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\}
  -
  \{S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)-S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\}
\]
for $\abs u\le\varsigma n/4$.

\emph{Step 4a: the discrepancy is uniformly $O_p(1)$, with no $u$-dependence.}
As in Step~3, only two mechanisms contribute: observations whose
$q_\ell$-side membership changes between $c_{0,\ell}$ and $c_{0,\ell}+u/n$,
and the indirect effect through the profiled leaf means; the indirect
effect is $O_p(n^{-1})$ uniformly in $u$ by Step~3's own argument (the
$O_p(1)$ collar count and the $\Theta_p(n)$ leaf denominators of
Assumption~\ref{ass:solver-twostage}(f) do not depend on $u$), hence
$o_p(1)$ uniformly and negligible below. For the direct effect, every
contributing observation lies in $\mathcal C_{n,M}$ \emph{regardless of
$u$} --- the collar is defined by the ancestor coordinate alone, and only
an observation's \emph{side} assignment, never its collar membership,
depends on $u$. Write
\[
  K_n
  :=
  \sum_{i:X_i\in\mathcal C_{n,M}}
  \{Z_i-\gamma_0(X_i)\}^2
\]
for the aggregate squared residual over the collar. By the standing
conditional second-moment bound and $\PP(X\in\mathcal C_{n,M})=O(M/n)$
(Step~1),
\[
  \E[K_n]
  =
  n\,\PP(X\in\mathcal C_{n,M})\,
  \sup_x\E\bigl[\{Z-\gamma_0(X)\}^2\mid X=x\bigr]
  =
  O(M)=O(1),
\]
so $K_n=O_p(1)$ by Markov's inequality. The direct effect on $\Xi_n(u)$, at
any $u$, is a difference of squared-residual sums restricted to these same
collar observations, and is therefore bounded by a fixed multiple of $K_n$
\emph{uniformly in $u$}: no observation outside $\mathcal C_{n,M}$
contributes at any $u$, so there is nothing left to grow as $\abs u$ grows.
Hence
\[
  \sup_{\abs u\le\varsigma n/4}\abs{\Xi_n(u)}=O_p(1).
\]
This uniform bound needs no peeling and no $u$-indexed probability
calculation: once every contributing observation is confined, independently
of $u$, to the $O(1/n)$-mass collar $\mathcal C_{n,M}$, the range over which
$u$ varies cannot affect the bound.

\emph{Step 4b: the oracle criterion excludes escape.} The argmax-consistency
argument that establishes tightness for a profiled change-point criterion of
exactly this form --- via \citet{chan1993,seijosen2011} for Tier~1's
leaf-parent stump criterion, or via \citet{seootsu2018} for the profiled
criterion of part~(v) above --- proceeds by first showing that outside any
fixed neighbourhood of the true threshold, the criterion exceeds its
minimising value by an amount tending to infinity with the neighbourhood's
radius, uniformly, and only then concluding that the argmin is tight; the
tightness conclusion is a corollary of this escape-exclusion step, not an
independent fact. Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation}'s own hypothesis
grants exactly this apparatus for $S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}$: the
conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} (or its Tier-1 analogue) are
assumed to hold at level $\ell$ under true ancestor conditioning, which is
precisely $S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}$. Applying the same escape-exclusion
step to $S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}$ therefore gives: for every $K<\infty$,
there exists $U_K<\infty$ such that
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    \inf_{U_K\le\abs u\le\varsigma n/4}
    \bigl\{S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)
      -S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\bigr\}
    >K
  \right)
  \longrightarrow1.
\]

\emph{Step 4c: combine.} By Step~4a, choose $K_0<\infty$ with
$\PP\bigl(\sup_{\abs u\le\varsigma n/4}\abs{\Xi_n(u)}>K_0\bigr)<\epsilon/3$
for $n$ large. By Step~4b with $K=K_0$, choose $U\ge U_{K_0}$ with
\[
  \PP\!\left(
    \inf_{U\le\abs u\le\varsigma n/4}
    \bigl\{S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)
      -S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\bigr\}
    \le K_0
  \right)
  <\epsilon/3
\]
for $n$ large. On the intersection of these two probability-tending-to-one
events, for every $u$ with $U\le\abs u\le\varsigma n/4$,
\[
  S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)-S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})
  \ \ge\
  \bigl\{S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell}+u/n)
    -S^{\mathrm{or}}_{n,\ell}(c_{0,\ell})\bigr\}
  -\abs{\Xi_n(u)}
  \ >\ K_0-K_0\ =\ 0,
\]
so the actual criterion strictly exceeds its value at $c_{0,\ell}$
throughout this range; since $\widehat c_\ell$ globally minimises
$S^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell}$ over $B^-$ and $c_{0,\ell}\in B^-$ is a
competitor achieving increment exactly zero, $\widehat c_\ell$ cannot lie at
any such $u$, on an event of probability at least $1-2\epsilon/3$. The true
minimiser also cannot lie in $\{c:\varsigma n/4\le n\abs{c-c_{0,\ell}}\}$,
since $B^-$ already excludes it by construction. Together with Step~3's
control on $\abs u\le U$ (valid on the same Step~1/Step~2 good event), a
union bound over the three budgeted bad events --- the ancestor-collar
exceedance budgeted at $\epsilon/3$ in Step~1, and the two further
$\epsilon/3$ exceedances fixed in Steps~4a and~4b above --- together with
the vanishing-probability event of Step~2, gives
$\PP(n\abs{\widehat c_\ell-c_{0,\ell}}>U)\le\epsilon+o(1)$ for $n$ large.
Since $\epsilon>0$ was arbitrary, this holds for every $\epsilon$, so
\[
  n(\widehat c_\ell-c_{0,\ell})=O_p(1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage-superconsistency}]
\emph{Proof strategy.}
Apply the two-tier argument at the root and then proceed by induction over
tree levels, using Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} at every node with
ancestors. At every node, the rate is first established on the fixed interval
\(B^-\) and then transferred to the actual search interval \(I_n(N)\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}.

The root has no ancestors, so its row set requires no ancestor conditioning.
Its rate on \(B^-\) follows from the classical stump argument when both
children are terminal and from Lemma~\ref{lem:prism-profile} otherwise, in
either case with Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} supplying the
corresponding statement when its descendants are held at their Stage-A grid
values rather than their true values --- available because
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}(ii)--(iii) place every such descendant
within \(O_p(1/r_n)\) of its true value, at the cost of halving the drift
constant. Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} then transfers the rate from
\(B^-\) to \(I_n(\mathrm{root})\). If every boundary above level \(\ell\) has
the asserted rate, Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} establishes it for
every boundary at level \(\ell\), again combined with
Lemma~\ref{lem:descendant-stability} for that node's still-Stage-A-precision
descendants and with Lemma~\ref{lem:interval-reduction} for the transfer to
\(I_n(N)\).  Since the depth is at most the fixed number \(\bar D\), induction
covers the whole tree.

For each fixed \(k\) and every \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(M_k<\infty\) such that
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}
  \PP\!\left(
    n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}>M_k
  \right)
  <
  \frac{\epsilon}{K}.
\]
With \(M=\max_{k\le K}M_k\), the finite union bound gives
\[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}
  \PP\!\left(
    \max_{1\le k\le K}
    n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}>M
  \right)
  \le
  \sum_{k=1}^K
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}
  \PP\!\left(
    n\abs{\widehat c_k-c_{0,k}}>M
  \right)
  <\epsilon.
\]
Hence the maximum is \(O_p(1)\).  Since
\(K\le\bar L-1<\infty\), no growing-dimensional union bound is required, and
the finitely many probability-tending-to-one events invoked above --- Stage-A
recovery (Theorem~\ref{thm:stagea-topology}), search-interval validity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:search-interval}), and the per-node events of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:interval-reduction}, \ref{lem:descendant-stability},
and~\ref{lem:ancestor-localisation} --- may likewise be intersected by a finite
union bound without loss.
\end{proof}
